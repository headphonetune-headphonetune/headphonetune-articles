# Beats Studio Buds: Right for Your Needs?

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/beats-studio-buds-right-for-your-needs/)

The Beats Studio Buds + are a solid middle-ground option in a crowded market. They don't try to be the absolute best at any one thing, but they cover the essentials well, especially if you live in a mixed Apple/Android household. The data sheet highlights three main areas that define this product: the battery life, the cross-platform compatibility, and the call quality. 36 Hours of Total Listening Time The headline number here's up to 36 hours of total listening time when you factor in the charging case. that's a lot for true wireless earbuds. And it puts them near the top of the pack. Practically, this means you can fly cross-country, forget to charge them for a week of commutes, or leave the case at home for a long day and still have juice left. For everyday listening. The earbuds themselves will get you through a standard workday, and the case tops them up multiple times. The trade-off is that the case is pocketable but not the smallest on the market. It's a rounded. Squarish shape that's a bit thicker than some rivals. So it might create a noticeable lump in a tight jeans pocket. Also. The spec sheet mentions "3 Lithium Ion batteries required," which is just the two in the buds and one in the case. There is no wireless charging mentioned, so you are tethered to a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)we observed cable to refill the case. That is not a dealbreaker, but it is a minor convenience you lose compared to some competitors that offer [Qi charging](https://www.wirelesspowerconsortium.com/)we found pads. Enhanced Apple and Android Compatibility This is the most distinctive aspect of the Studio Buds +. Beats has always been an Apple brand, but these earbuds are explicitly designed to work natively with both ecosystems. On an iPhone, you get the fluid one-touch pairing pop-up, iCloud syncing across your Apple devices, and hands-free "Hey Siri" support. On Android. You get the Beats app, which allows for firmware updates, customizable controls, and a quick-pair experience via Google's Speedy Pair. This is genuinely useful if you use an iPhone for personal stuff but a Windows laptop or Android tablet for work. Many competitors force you to choose a side. Or It offers a watered-down app experience on one platform. In real-world use. The Beats Studio Buds + treat both equally, which is rare. The trade-off is that they lack the H2 chip found in Apple's AirPods Pro. So you miss out on features like automatic device switching between Apple devices, which is a slick convenience if you're deeply entrenched in the Apple ecosystem. You get manual switching. Which works fine, but it isn't as frictionless. 3x Larger Voice-Targeting Microphones The spec sheet emphasizes that these have three times larger voice-targeting mics to filter background noise for calls. This is a significant upgrade from the original Studio Buds. Which had mediocre call quality. In practice, this means the person on the other end of the line will hear your voice clearly even if you're walking down a busy street or sitting in a windy spot. For most people. The mics are designed to focus on your voice and suppress the surrounding noise. The trade-off here is that the earbuds themselves are not rated for wind noise resistance during calls. The microphones are larger, which helps with voice pickup, but a strong gust of wind can still cause some distortion. Also, while the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) (Active [Noise Cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)) is described as "personalized," it is not adaptive. It does a capable job of dulling constant low-frequency sounds like an airplane hum or an office HVAC system. But it won't dynamically adjust to your environment like some pricier models. You get a static level of noise cancellation that's solid, not class-leading. That said, fit and Comfort The four silicone tip sizes are a thoughtful inclusion. Getting a proper seal is key for both sound quality and passive noise isolation, which works alongside the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). The tips are oval-shaped. Which Beats claims fits a wider range of ear shapes. For most people. One of the four sizes will work well. The earbuds themselves are light at about 30 grams for the pair, so they're well-fitting for long listening sessions. However, they don't have ear fins or wings, so if you have very oily ears or plan to do intense interval training with lots of jumping, they might work loose. On the practical side, they're sweat-resistant, which is fine for a gym session, but they aren't waterproof for swimming. Sound Quality and Connectivity The "Custom Acoustic Platform" is marketing speak, but it translates to a consumer-friendly sound profile. Beats has moved away from the overly bass-heavy tuning of their older products. The Studio Buds + have a balanced sound with a slight bass boost. They aren't audiophile-grade; they won't reveal every detail in a complex classical piece, but they're enjoyable for pop, hip-hop, rock, and podcasts. The soundstage is decent. And they support Spatial Audio, which is a virtual surround effect. For comparison, it works best with [Dolby Atmos](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) content on Apple Music. And it adds a sense of width to the sound, though it isn't as convincing as the head-tracking implementation on AirPods Pro. The Class 1 Bluetooth is a real advantage. It offers a more stable connection with fewer dropouts, especially in congested areas like a train station or a gym with lots of Bluetooth devices. It also extends the range. So you can leave your phone on your desk and walk to the other side of a room without losing the signal. The trade-off is that these earbuds do not support high-resolution Bluetooth codecs like [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) or [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac). It uses AAC and SBC, which is fine for Apple devices and most Android phones, but if you're a hardcore Android user with a phone that accommodates [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/), you won't get the absolute highest quality wireless audio possible. In daily use, the The key takeaway on the Specs The Beats Studio Buds + are a well-rounded package. The battery life is great, the cross-platform support is a genuine differentiator, and the call quality is a clear improvement. The trade-offs are the lack of wireless charging. No automatic device switching, and an [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) that is decent but not top-tier. They are not the best-sounding earbuds in this price range. But they are far from the worst. they're a dependable choice for someone who wants a reliable set of earbuds that just work. Without worrying about which phone brand It uses. Summary: The Beats Studio Buds + are a strong all-around choice for anyone who values long battery life and easy use across both iPhone and Android devices. For typical buyers. They handle calls well and offer a stable connection, but the noise cancellation and sound quality are good, not exceptional. If you need the absolute best [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) or audiophile-level detail. You will want to look at more specialized options, but for everyday commuting, gym sessions, and calls, these are a reliable pick. Who this is for This is for the person who uses an iPhone but carries an Android tablet, or vice versa. It is also for the commuter who needs a full week of listening on a single case charge and wants a decent seal without having to fiddle with multiple ear tips. If you take a lot of calls in noisy environments, the improved microphones make these a solid option. Who should look elsewhere If you are deeply invested in the Apple ecosystem and rely on automatic device switching between your Mac. IPhone, and iPad, the AirPods Pro are a better fit. If you're an Android user who demands [high-resolution audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/) codecs like [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/). You will be leaving performance on the table.

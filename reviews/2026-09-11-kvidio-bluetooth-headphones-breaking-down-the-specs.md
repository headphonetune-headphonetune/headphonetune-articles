# KVIDIO Bluetooth Headphones: Breaking Down the Specs

It has no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and no passive isolation worth bragging about. What it does offer is a set of numbers that say a lot about who should buy it and who shouldn't. Size and weight At 0.44 pounds, this is genuinely light for an over-ear headphone. Most full-size wireless models land between 0.55 and 0.9 pounds* (based on available data), and that difference matters more than it sounds. Half a pound sitting on your skull for three hours is noticeable; 0.44 pounds is closer to forgetting you're wearing anything. Combined with the foldable hinge and oval earcups, the whole thing collapses down small enough to toss in a bag without dedicating a pocket to it. The adjustable headband and "flexible earmuffs" claim is standard marketing language, but the weight is the real spec here. Lightweight over-ears tend to clamp less, and less clamp means less fatigue during long sessions. If you wear glasses, that lower pressure is the difference between a two-hour session and a four-hour one. Materials and build The enclosure is leather and plastic. That's the honest description, and it tells you two things. First, the protein foam padding on the earcups is soft and will feel comfortable out of the box. Second, leather and plastic at this weight class means you're not getting metal hinges or a reinforced headband. Treat the folding mechanism gently and it'll last; twist it around like a pair of studio cans and it won't. Water resistance is listed as "Not Water Resistant." That's a hard no for rain, heavy sweat, or leaving them on a gym floor next to a water bottle. The "Fitness" and "Running" tags in the product data are aspirational - this is not a workout headphone. Sweat will degrade the protein foam padding and the leather covering faster than normal use will. Audio performance The driver installation is dual 40mm dynamic drivers. That's a common size for over-ear headphones and generally produces fuller bass than the 30-32mm drivers you find in cheaper on-ear models. The frequency response spans 20 Hz to 20 kHz, which is the full range of human hearing - nothing unusual there, and the spec sheet doesn't break down how flat or boosted that response is at any point. The "deep bass" claim suggests a bass-forward tuning, which is typical for this category and price bracket. Impedance is 32 ohms. That's low, meaning these are effortless to drive from a phone, laptop, or any 3.5mm jack without an amp. Sensitivity is 105 dB. Which is on the higher side - they'll get loud from weak sources. Neither number tells you about sound quality. Only about how much power they need. They don't need much. One spec to flag: audio latency is listed at 250 milliseconds. That's high. For music, it doesn't matter - you won't notice a quarter-second delay between pressing play and hearing sound. For video, most phones and laptops compensate automatically. But for gaming or any real-time audio work, 250ms is a visible and audible lag. That said. The product description claims "low latency," and 250ms isn't low by any modern standard. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) (listed in the main specs) or 5.3 (listed in the features) with a proper low-latency codec can get down to 40-60ms. Whatever codec this headphone uses, the stated figure is 250ms. Take that at face value. Sound isolation, not noise cancellation, is the control method. That means the earcups physically block some ambient noise by covering your ears. It's passive, it's modest, and it won't compete with even entry-level [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in our experience . On a plane or a bus, you'll hear the engine. Battery and charging This is where the WH201A earns its keep. 65 hours of playback from a 500mAh battery is exceptional. Most over-ear headphones in this class deliver 30-40 hours. Sixty-five hours means you can use these for two hours a day for a month without charging. The 2.5-hour charge time is normal, not efficient, but with that runtime it hardly matters. The wired fallback is the smart part. When the battery does die. You plug in the included 3.5mm cable and keep listening. No charging, no waiting. That's a capability more investment-grade headphones often skip, and it turns a dead battery from a limitation into an inconvenience. The cable is detachable. Which is also decent - if it breaks, you replace the cable, not the headphones. Two power levels are listed, which likely refers to some kind of battery indicator or power-saving mode. The data doesn't explain it further, so don't count on anything specific there. Connectivity and compatibility Bluetooth range is 10 meters, or about 33 feet. That's standard. Walls and interference will cut that down, but within a room you're fine. The spec sheet lists [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) in one place and 5.3 in another - the difference between those two versions is minor for everyday use, mostly around connection stability and power efficiency. Either way. You're getting a modern Bluetooth stack that should pair quickly and hold a connection. Compatibility is broad: anything with Bluetooth or a 3.5mm jack. That covers phones. Laptops, tablets, desktops, and older devices without Bluetooth at all. The built-in microphone handles calls and video meetings. Call quality will be serviceable for voice but not studio-grade - the mic is built into the headphone, so it picks up room noise along with your voice. The model number is WH201A, and it's listed as compatible with laptops specifically in the compatible devices field, though the broader feature text confirms phone and tablet support. No codec details are provided. Which means you can't verify [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/), AAC, or [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) support. Assume SBC - the baseline Bluetooth codec - unless you find codec info elsewhere. Controls The control method is listed as both button and touch in different fields. And the controller type says button. The capability description mentions a multi-function button for pairing. Realistically, this is a button-controlled headphone with maybe a touch element somewhere. Button controls are more consistent than touch on budget headphones - you can find them by feel, and they don't misfire when you adjust the fit. That's a point in its favor. What's in the box You get the headphone. A 3.5mm backup audio cable, a USB charging cable, and a user manual. The 3.5mm cable is the significant one. It's the difference between "my headphones died" and "my headphones still work." The USB cable is for charging only - no wall adapter included, so you'll need your own brick or a laptop port. What the numbers don't tell you Several things are missing or vague. There's no information on codec support. Which affects latency and audio quality over Bluetooth. The frequency response curve isn't provided, so "deep bass" and "HiFi stereo" are claims without data. The 250ms latency figure is the only concrete performance number beyond battery life and driver size. And it's the one that should give you pause if gaming or real-time monitoring is your use case. The 34,090 customer reviews at 4.5 stars is a large sample size. That tells you most buyers are satisfied with what they got. But it doesn't tell you what they're comparing it to. A 4.5-star rating on a budget headphone usually means it met expectations for the price, not that it competes with headphones costing three times as much. Summary: The KVIDIO WH201A is a lightweight. Long-battery-life over-ear headphone that gets the fundamentals right for casual listening. The 65-hour battery, 0.44-pound weight, and wired fallback make it a practical choice for commuters, students, and anyone who wants a set they can wear all day without charging or fiddling. The trade-offs are real: no noise cancellation. No water resistance, and a stated 250ms latency that rules out serious gaming or audio work. If you need isolation from a noisy environment or you're a competitive gamer, this isn't the right tool. Who this is for This suits someone who wants an inexpensive. Basic to wear over-ear headphone for music, podcasts, video calls, and schoolwork. For everyday listening, the long battery life and light weight make it a solid fit for students moving between classes, remote workers on calls, and travelers who want a backup pair that won't die mid-trip. The wired option is a genuine advantage if you're forgetful about charging. If you listen to bass-heavy music and don't need studio accuracy. The 40mm drivers and bass-forward tuning will do the job. Who should look elsewhere Skip this if you need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for flights, open offices, or loud commutes - passive isolation on a plastic-and-leather headphone won't cut it. Skip it if you game competitively or do any real-time audio monitoring, because 250ms of latency is too much. Skip it if you sweat heavily during workouts or want to wear headphones in the rain. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the KVIDIO Bluetooth Headphones
### How long does the battery actually last, and how long does it take to charge?
The 500mAh battery is rated for 65 hours of playtime and charges in about 2.5 hours. If it runs out, you can plug in the included 3.5mm cable and keep listening in wired mode.

Yes. They work over Bluetooth or through the 3.5mm jack, and the audio cable is detachable. That covers laptops. PCs, and anything with a standard headphone port.

### How much do they weigh - and are they comfortable for long wear?
They weigh 0.44 pounds, which is light for an over-ear set. The earcups use memory-protein foam - and the headband adjusts, so they're built for extended sessions.

### What's the Bluetooth range and version?
The listing states Bluetooth 5.3 in the feature text but 5.4 in the specs, so the version is inconsistent. Range is listed at 10 meters, or about 33 feet.

### Do they block out noise or resist water?
Noise control is sound isolation only. Not active noise cancellation, so they rely on the earcup seal to muffle surroundings. They aren't water resistant, so sweat or rain could be a problem.

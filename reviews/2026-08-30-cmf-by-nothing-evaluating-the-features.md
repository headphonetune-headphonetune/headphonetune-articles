# CMF BY NOTHING: Evaluating the Features

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/cmf-by-nothing-evaluating-the-features/)

The CMF Clip Pro is an open-ear clip-on earbud, and that design choice dictates almost everything about how you should evaluate it. Open-ear buds don't go in your ear canal; they sit on the outer ear and project sound inward. This means you get situational awareness - you can hear traffic, coworkers, or a train announcement while listening - but you trade away the passive noise isolation you get from in-ear buds. The specs here lean heavily into that trade-off. So let's look at what the data actually tells you. Size. Weight, and Fit The headline number is 5.92 grams per earbud. That is genuinely light. For comparison. Most in-ear wireless buds weigh between 4 and 6 grams, but clip-on designs have to distribute that weight across a mechanical grip, not just a nozzle in your ear canal. The "3-Point Clip" design spreads pressure across three contact areas, which is their solution to the classic clip-on limitation: a single pressure point that gets sore after A hour. The C-bridge is described as a "Titanium Wire Core" that flexes and snaps back. That matters because clip-on buds live or die by their clamping force. Too tight and your ear aches; too loose and they fall off when you run. The spec sheet doesn't give us a clamping force measurement. So you're relying on the material choice (titanium wire with PC/ABS+TPU coating) to hold shape over time. TPU is a rubbery plastic that resists sweat and skin oils. Which is good for longevity. The 3-point distribution is a smart approach, but fit is very individual - if your ears are particularly thick or thin at the antihelix (the ridge where these clip), the "universal" claim may not hold for you. The carrying case is 38.81 grams, which brings the total to about 50.65 grams. The case is space-saving - 26.69mm tall. 51mm* (based on available data) wide, 52mm* (based on available data) deep - roughly the size of a large coin stack. It's pocketable, though not as flat as some card-style cases. The case material is PC/ABS with an aluminum alloy accent. Which suggests decent build quality for the price tier. For everyday listening, the aluminum is likely just on the hinge or a decorative plate, but it adds a quality touch. Materials and Build The enclosure is PC/ABS+Aluminum alloy for the case. And PC/ABS+TPU for the clip itself. That's standard for this category. PC/ABS is a tough. Impact-resistant plastic blend; TPU on the clip gives it that rubbery grip so it doesn't slide off. The "Dark Grey" color is the only one listed in the data. Though the description mentions "many color for your fashions" - that appears to be marketing noise from the listing template. We don't have confirmed color options beyond Dark Grey. In real-world use. The water resistance is listed as "Water Resistant" with no IP rating given. That's a red flag. Most earbuds in this class advertise [IPX4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) (sweat and light rain) or [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code)in our experience (jet spray). Without a specific IP code. "water resistant" is vague. For a product explicitly marketed for fitness and running. You should know exactly what it can handle. The spec says "sweat resistant" in the title. So it will survive workouts, but don't expect to wear these in heavy rain or rinse them off under a tap. If you run in downpours regularly. The lack of a stated IP rating is a meaningful gap in the data. Performance Specs The 10.8mm dynamic driver is larger than what you typically find in open-ear buds. Most open-ear designs use smaller drivers (8-10mm) because they need to compensate for the lack of a sealed ear canal. A larger driver can move more air. Which helps with bass response - the classic weak point of open-ear audio. The frequency response is rated up to 40. 000 Hz, which is beyond the 20,000 Hz human hearing limit. That's a [Hi-Res Audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/) marketing spec, not a practical one. What matters more is the impedance at 13 ohms. Low impedance means the driver is uncomplicated to drive. Which is good for battery life and Bluetooth volume headroom. It doesn't tell us about sound quality directly. But it suggests the amp inside the bud isn't working hard, which should help with distortion at high volumes. The listing mentions "[Hi-Res Audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/) & [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac)" in the description. [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac)in our experience is Sony's high-bitrate Bluetooth codec, capable of 990 kbps. That's a real advantage for Android users who want better-than-CD-quality wireless audio. However, the technical specifications section only lists "[Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/)" without codec support details. The description also mentions "Anti-Leakage Mode" in the Nothing X app. Sound leakage is the biggest issue with open-ear buds - people around you can hear your music. Anti-leakage mode presumably uses DSP to reduce the sound that escapes, but it's an app toggle, not a hardware fix. It likely works by reducing output at certain frequencies. Which may slightly dull the sound. One more point: that's a trade-off: privacy versus audio quality. Battery and Charging Here's where the data gets interesting. The spec sheet lists "battery average life: 32.5 hours" and "battery charge time: 100 minutes." But the title and features clarify: 10 hours per charge. 32.5 hours total with the case. That's a 3.25x multiplier, which implies the case holds about 2.25 additional full charges. Ten hours per bud is solid for open-ear buds - many competitors offer 8-12 hours* (based on available data). The 100-minute charge time is for the full case recharge, presumably via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) (the listing doesn't specify the cable type. But "Without Cable" is listed as a feature, meaning no cable is included in the box - you'll need your own). Fast charging is mentioned as an additional characteristic, but the spec sheet doesn't quantify it. No "10 minutes = 2 hours" claim is provided. That's a missing data point. For a travel-focused product. Knowing the quick-charge rate matters. The 32.5-hour total is decent but not exceptional - some competitors push 40+ hours with a case. For a weekend trip, 32.5 hours is fine. For a week-long trip without charging. You'll need to find a USB outlet. Connectivity and Controls [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current-generation. It offers better power efficiency and connection stability than 5.0 or 5.1. Dual-device connectivity means you can pair with a phone and laptop simultaneously - useful if you take calls on your phone while watching video on your laptop. The "Low Lag Mode" is presumably a gaming-oriented attribute that reduces audio latency, though the exact latency figure isn't given. For video, [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) with modern codecs usually syncs fine; for gaming. Low-lag mode matters but won't match wired latency. The control scheme is a "Smart Dial" - spin or press. That's an unusual control method for earbuds. Most use touch surfaces or physical buttons. A spinning dial on a 5.92-gram earbud is ambitious. The question is whether the dial is uncomplicated to find and manipulate without looking. The spec lists "Touch Control" and "Button" as control methods. Which suggests the dial has both press and spin functions. App control is also available via the Nothing X app, which includes custom EQ, Spatial Audio, and Anti-Leakage Mode. For most people. The app is the same one used by Nothing's other audio products, so it's likely well-maintained, but we don't have app store ratings or user feedback in the data. Call Quality The 4-MIC system with a "Flagship Voice Pickup Unit (VPU)" is a significant spec. The VPU separates your voice from environmental noise in real time. For open-ear buds. Call quality is usually a weak point because the microphones are exposed to wind and ambient noise. Four mics with a dedicated VPU suggests they've put real engineering into this. The "crowded street or strong wind" claim is bold - wind noise is the hardest thing to filter. We don't have actual call test recordings. But the hardware approach is sound: multiple mics allow beamforming and noise cancellation algorithms to isolate the speaker's voice. If you take a lot of calls in noisy environments, this is one of the more promising aspects of the spec sheet. What's Missing The data doesn't come with an IP rating. Which is a notable omission for a fitness-oriented product. There's no stated weight distribution spec beyond the 5.92g per bud. No driver sensitivity rating, which would tell us how loud they get. No latency figure for Low Lag Mode. No case battery capacity in mAh. No codec list beyond the [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) mention in the description. The "What's in the Box" section is repeated marketing text with no actual list of included items - we know the case and buds are included, but whether you get a charging cable, extra ear tips (unlikely for clip-on), or a manual is unclear. The "built-in media" spec lists "User Manual. " so at least that's included. Compatibility The listed compatible devices are cellphones. Laptops, and VR headsets. The VR headset mention is interesting - open-ear buds are actually well-suited for VR because they don't block the sound of your surroundings and fit under headsets. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) should handle most modern devices. The "antenna location" spec lists "Calling, Exercising, Running" which is just a list of use cases, not an actual antenna location - that's a data quality issue in the listing. For Android users, [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) support is a plus. For iPhone users, [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) isn't supported - you'll get AAC, which is still decent but not the high-res experience the marketing emphasizes. If you exercise in heavy rain or want to rinse your earbuds after a sweaty workout.

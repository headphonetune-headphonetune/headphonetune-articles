### Headphone Specs Made Simple: What to Look For (2026)

Walk into any headphone discussion and you'll hear numbers thrown around like a secret language. Impedance, frequency response, sensitivity — it's easy to glaze over. But understanding a few key specs can save you from buying something that sounds worse than your phone's speaker. Here's what actually matters, and what you can safely ignore.

### Impedance: It's About What You Plug Into

Impedance (measured in ohms) tells you how much power a headphone needs to reach a decent volume. Low-impedance models (under 32 ohms) are built for phones, laptops, and dongles. High-impedance models (150 ohms and up) need a dedicated amplifier to sound their best — plug them straight into a phone and you'll get thin, lifeless audio.

If you're shopping for everyday use, stick to low impedance unless you already own an amp. ### Frequency Response: Don't Chase the Numbers

You'll see specs like "20Hz–20kHz" everywhere. That range covers human hearing, so it tells you almost nothing. What matters more is the shape of that response — whether bass is boosted, mids are recessed, or treble is sharp. A flat response is neutral; a V-shaped one adds punch but can fatigue your ears over long sessions.

Instead of obsessing over the range, listen for balance. If a review mentions "warm" or "bright," that's a hint about where the tuning leans. For kids' headphones, this matters even more — a harsh treble spike is uncomfortable for young ears. ### Sensitivity: Volume You Can Actually Use

Sensitivity (dB/mW) tells you how loud a headphone gets with a given amount of power. Higher is generally better for portable use. A model rated at 100dB or more will get loud enough from a phone without pushing the volume to max. Below 95dB, you might find yourself straining to hear quiet passages in noisy environments.

Pair sensitivity with impedance for the full picture. Low impedance plus high sensitivity equals easy to drive — that's your safe bet for commuting or casual listening. High impedance plus low sensitivity is a recipe for needing an amp, which is fine if you're building a desktop setup.

### What the Specs Don't Tell You

Here's the catch: specs never tell you how a headphone actually sounds. Two models with identical frequency response can sound completely different due to driver design, damping, and ear pad material. And comfort? That's pure personal preference — a spec sheet won't warn you about a tight clamp or shallow earcups.

That's why real-world listening notes matter more than numbers. ### The Bottom Line

Use specs to narrow your options, not to make the final call. Check impedance and sensitivity for compatibility with your devices, glance at frequency response for tuning clues, then trust your ears. A headphone that measures well on paper can still sound wrong to you — and that's the only spec that truly matters.

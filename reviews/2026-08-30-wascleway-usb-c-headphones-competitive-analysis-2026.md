Most consumer headphones are rated at 16 to 32 ohms. A 140-ohm impedance is high for a cheap, portable, on-ear headphone. What does that mean in practice? High-impedance headphones typically need more power to drive them to the same volume as low-impedance ones. A phone or a laptop's headphone jack is designed for low-impedance loads. With a 140-ohm headphone. You might find that the maximum volume is lower than you'd like on some devices. In real-world use, it won't be silent, but it might not get ear-splittingly loud. For a classroom, this might actually be a feature, not a bug.

No over-ear headphone at any price fully erases human voices. The 96.6% figure comes from the manufacturer, not an independent test, so treat it as a best-case scenario rather than a guarantee. Still, [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is a real characteristic here, not a passive claim, and it's the headline reason to buy these over a standard pair. What do they sound like? The driver spec is 40mm dynamic drivers with a frequency range of 20Hz to 20,000Hz. That range is the standard audible spectrum, so nothing unusual there - nearly every headphone lists roughly the same numbers. The 40mm driver size is typical for over-ear headphones and is generally enough to produce decent bass without the sound feeling thin. Vonaural describes the tuning as bass-forward, which suits pop, hip-hop, and electronic music but may not be optimal if you want a flat, neutral sound for essential listening. The frequency range alone tells you these cover the full spectrum; it doesn't tell you how accurately. The "Hi-Fi" label is marketing rather than a certification, so don't read too much into it. What about calls? The built-in microphone uses ENC, or environmental noise cancellation. This is different from [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) quiets what you hear; ENC cleans up what the person on the other end hears. Vonaural claims "2X clearer voice calls," which is a comparative claim with no stated baseline - twice as clear as what, exactly? That said, having any ENC microphone at all is a meaningful feature for remote workers and anyone taking calls on the go. For everyday listening, it's a common spec at this tier, not a standout, but it's there and it's functional.

# TRAUSI Wireless Earbuds: What to Know Before Buying

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/trausi-wireless-earbuds-what-to-know-before-buying/)

A long run in humid weather is a rough test for any earbud. Sweat pools, the fit loosens, and half the models on the market end up dangling by a wire or bouncing against your jaw. The TRAUSI i27 is built specifically for that scenario. And the spec sheet tells a fairly complete story about what it does well and where it makes compromises. The most important thing to know upfront: these are hook-style earbuds, not standard in-ear buds. The "earpiece shape: Hook" designation means a flexible arm wraps over the top of your ear to hold the driver in place. For running, cycling, or gym work, that's a meaningful design choice. Standard buds rely entirely on the silicone tip creating a seal inside your ear canal, and once sweat breaks that seal, they start working their way loose. A hook keeps the bud anchored even when the tip isn't perfectly seated. Each bud weighs 7 grams. Which is light enough that you shouldn't notice it much during movement. Three sizes of silicone tips (S/M/L) are included, so you have some ability to dial in the fit, though the hook itself doesn't appear to be adjustable. The [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating is the other spec that matters most for active use. [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) means the buds can survive being submerged in up to a meter of water for 30 minutes* (based on available data), which is well beyond what sweat, rain, or a splash at the water fountain will throw at them. TRAUSI does note that they aren't suitable for actual submersion. Which is a reasonable disclaimer - the rating covers accidental exposure, not swimming. If you're a heavy sweater or you run in the rain. This is about as much water resistance as you'll find in an earbud. Battery life is where the i27 makes its strongest case. Six hours on a single charge is solid for true wireless buds. And the 400mAh charging case extends that to a claimed 54 hours total. That's a lot of listening time between wall charges. The case itself charges via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)we noticed and reaches full in about 1.5 hours. More useful for daily life is the quick-charge claim: 10 minutes in the case gets you 2 hours of playback. If you're the type who forgets to charge things until you're walking out the door. That's a genuinely practical capability. The LED percentage display on the case is a space-saving thing. But it's better than the three-dot indicators many competitors use because you get an actual number instead of a rough guess. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current-generation. And the listed range is 15 meters (about 50 feet) with no obstacles in the way. Real-world range will be shorter through walls. But 15 meters in open space is standard for modern Bluetooth and should cover you moving between rooms or leaving your phone on a gym bench while you grab water. The supported profiles - HSP. HFP, A2DP, AVRCP - cover the basics: hands-free calling, stereo audio streaming, and remote control of playback. Nothing exotic. But nothing missing either. Pairing is automatic when you remove the buds from the case, and they work with iOS, Android, and Windows, so you're not locked into one ecosystem. The [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) claim deserves a closer read. The spec sheet lists "Environmental Noise Cancellation" under noise control, and the feature text describes a noise-cancelling microphone. That's ENC, not [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). The distinction matters. ENC works on the microphone during calls - it tries to filter out background noise so the person on the other end hears your voice more clearly. It doesn't actively cancel noise for you while you're listening to music. If you're expecting the kind of silence you get from a pair of over-ear [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphones on a plane. These won't deliver that. For calls in a noisy environment, the mic processing should help. For blocking out gym music or traffic while you run. You're relying on the ear tip seal and whatever passive isolation the fit gives you. Touch controls handle play/pause. Track skipping, volume, and voice assistant activation. Touch controls on earbuds are hit-or-miss in practice - accidental taps happen. Especially when you're adjusting the fit or wiping sweat. The spec sheet doesn't say whether the controls are customizable. So it's worth assuming they aren't. If you've had bad experiences with touch controls on other buds. That's a risk here too. One thing the data doesn't clarify: whether these support the higher-quality Bluetooth codecs like AAC or [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/). The profile list (HSP, HFP, A2DP, AVRCP) covers the standard audio transport, but codec support isn't specified. For most listeners streaming from a phone. The default SBC codec is fine, but if you're particular about audio quality and use a service that supports better codecs, that's an unknown. For everyday listening, the "true wireless stereo" and "crystal-clear sound" language in the description is marketing copy, not a measurable spec. There's no frequency response range, driver size, or impedance listed, so there's no way to assess the actual sound signature from the data. That's a gap worth noting if audio quality is your primary concern. The build is plastic. Which is typical at this tier and helps keep the weight down. The enclosure material isn't going to feel superior. But it's also not a durability concern for normal use - plastic earbuds survive drops and daily handling fine. The "On Ear" folding attribute listed in the specs is almost certainly a data error. Since these are in-ear buds with a hook, not on-ear headphones. Ignore that one. The eco-friendly and sustainable design language in the attribute list is vague. There's no specific certification. Recycled content percentage, or detail about what makes the materials eco-conscious. Treat that as positioning rather than a verifiable claim. Customer reviews sit at 4.9 out of 5 from 205 ratings. The breakdown shows 94% five-star, 3% four-star, 2% three-star, 1% two-star, and 0% one-star. That's an unusually high concentration of top ratings, and the near-total absence of one-star reviews is notable for any product. Take it with the usual grain of salt - early reviews on newer listings can skew positive - but the sample size is large enough that the pattern is worth something. The best sellers rank of #297 in Electronics suggests these are moving in volume, which means replacement units and spare parts are more likely to be available down the line. Warranty is 90 days from the manufacturer. Which is on the short side. Many competitors offer a year. If something goes wrong with the charging case or one of the buds stops holding a charge, you've got three months to catch it. That's a real consideration for a product with a built-in battery that will degrade over time. One more spec that's listed oddly: "antenna location" repeats the same long list of use cases as "specific uses for product" - business. Sports, workouts, running, fitness, travel, cycling, gym, outdoor, music, entertainment, TV series, gaming, office meetings, professional, school. That's clearly a data entry error, not a real specification. The compatible devices list (iPhone, Android, cellphones, desktops, laptops, tablets, all smart devices with Bluetooth) is more straightforward and confirms broad compatibility. What's in the box: two i27 earbuds, a charging cable, the charging case, ear tips, and a user manual. Nothing surprising, nothing missing. In real-world use, the color is purple. That's the only colorway mentioned in the data, so if purple isn't your thing, you may be out of luck unless other options exist that aren't shown here. Summary: The TRAUSI i27 is a sport-focused true wireless earbud with a hook design, [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) water resistance, six hours of playback per charge, and a 54-hour total battery reserve with quick charging. The hook fit and water resistance make it a sensible choice for runners. Cyclists, and gym-goers who have trouble keeping standard buds in place. The main trade-offs are the absence of [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) (the [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is microphone-side only), a short 90-day warranty, and no published audio codec or driver specs to judge sound quality by. Who this is for Anyone who prioritizes fit security and sweat resistance over audiophile sound. Who should look elsewhere If you need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for flights, open offices, or loud commutes, these won't give you it. The ENC only helps the person you're calling, not you. If you care about high-quality audio codecs like [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) or [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac), the spec sheet doesn't confirm support, so look for a model that lists them explicitly.

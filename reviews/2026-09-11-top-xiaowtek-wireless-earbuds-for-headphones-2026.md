Sound: a 13.4mm graphene diaphragm The driver size is listed at 13.4mm with a graphene diaphragm. Larger dynamic drivers generally move more air and can produce stronger bass, which lines up with the "bass stereo" framing in the title. The graphene coating is a marketing angle - it's a stiff, light material that theoretically helps with clarity and reduces distortion at higher volumes. Whether you can hear the difference at this price point is debatable, but it's not a red flag. It's a reasonable driver spec for the segment. What you should expect from the tuning is a bass-forward sound. "Powerful bass. Clear mids and bright highs" is the standard three-part description every budget earbud uses - and it usually translates to boosted low end, somewhat recessed mids, and treble that can get sharp on certain tracks. If you listen to hip-hop, electronic, or anything beat-driven, that tuning generally works. If you care about vocal clarity, acoustic music, or detailed instrument separation, the boosted bass can muddy things. There's no mention of an EQ app or any way to adjust the sound. That's a real trade-off. Many competitors in this range offer a companion app with preset EQs, and its absence means you're stuck with whatever tuning XIAOWTEK ships. Noise cancellation: read this carefully The listing uses the phrase "[noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" in the title, but the technical specs clarify that the noise control is passive. That means there's no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) circuitry. The noise reduction comes from the physical seal of the eartips blocking outside sound, plus the ENC microphones on calls. The ENC (environmental noise cancellation) claim is specifically about calls. The dual hybrid microphones supposedly eliminate 85% of background noise on your voice so the person on the other end hears you more clearly. That's a call-quality feature. Not a listening attribute. It doesn't reduce what you hear around you. For most people, this distinction matters. For anyone wanting earbuds to quiet a noisy commute or an open-plan office, passive isolation only goes as far as the seal allows. Well-fitted silicone tips can block a fair amount of low-frequency drone, but they won't touch voices or higher-pitched noise the way active cancellation does. The IP7 rating and sport-focused framing suggest these are aimed more at workouts and movement than at silencing a plane cabin. IP7 water resistance IP7 means the buds can survive immersion in water up to roughly one meter for 30 minutes* (based on available data). For earbuds, that's a strong rating. Sweat. The case is usually not rated to the same level - and the listing doesn't specify whether the case shares the IP7 rating. So treat the case as something to keep dry. The "sport" use case is listed explicitly, and the IP7 rating backs that up. That said, the fit matters more than the rating for workout use. An IP7 bud that falls out on the treadmill is still a problem. On the practical side, the listing mentions an ergonomic design and includes three pairs of eartips, which gives you some ability to dial in the fit, but there's no wing or ear-hook stabilization mentioned, so these are standard bud-shaped. If you have ears that shed typical earbuds during runs, that's a risk. Touch controls and what they cover The touch sensors support volume, track skipping, play/pause, call answer, and voice assistant. That's a full set of controls, and having volume on the bud itself is a plus. Some budget earbuds omit volume from the touch controls and force you to use your phone, which is annoying mid-run or mid-commute. The catch with touch controls on any earbud is accidental triggers. Adjusting the fit or brushing your hair can pause your music. XIAOWTEK doesn't specify whether the touch sensitivity is adjustable. And without an app, it almost certainly isn't. That's the trade-off of touch over physical buttons. Some people adapt quickly, others find it maddening. Fit. The question is execution. Budget ENC implementations can make your voice sound processed or robotic while cutting noise. And can struggle in wind. The listing doesn't mention wind noise specifically. Which is a common weak point. If calls are a priority, this is a characteristic to look for in user reviews rather than trust from the spec sheet.

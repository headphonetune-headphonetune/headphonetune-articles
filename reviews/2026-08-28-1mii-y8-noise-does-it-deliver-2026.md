The Battery is the Headline, and It's Genuinely Impressive The 100-hour continuous playback claim is the first thing that jumps out. Compared to similar products in this class, that's an outlier. Most over-ear [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphones, even quality ones from Sony or Bose, top out around 30 to 40 hours with noise cancellation on. The 1Mii Y8+ claims triple that. Even if real-world usage cuts that number in half - which is common with volume levels and codec usage - you're still looking at a device that could go weeks between charges with daily commuting. The 10-minute quick charge for 5 hours of playback is also a practical capability; it's not the fastest we've seen. But it's enough to get you through a day if you forgot to plug in overnight. The trade-off here's charge time. The spec sheet lists a 3-hour full charge, which is deliberate compared to competitors that can refill in under A hour. If you're the type who drains the battery completely and needs a quick top-up before heading out, this will be a minor annoyance. But given the sheer capacity, you're unlikely to hit that wall often. This is a battery-first design - and for travelers or people who hate carrying chargers, that's a legitimate selling point. That's the same architecture used in premium headphones, so on paper, this should perform above average for its price point. For everyday listening. The 1Mii listing specifically mentions reducing airplane engine noise and car rumble, which suggests the tuning is focused on low-frequency cancellation. What the data doesn't tell you is the depth of that cancellation. Specs don't reveal how many decibels are being canceled - and that's where budget [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphones often stumble. They might have the microphones and the algorithm, but the implementation can be weaker than the premium brands. [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) and [Hi-Res Audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/): Only If Your Phone Cooperates The Y8+ accommodates [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac). Which is Sony's high-resolution wireless codec. This is a genuine differentiator. Compared to similar products in this price range, many of which only support AAC or SBC, [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) can transmit up to three times more data over Bluetooth, resulting in noticeably better detail and clarity - if your source device supports it. The spec sheet is honest about this: [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) only works when paired with an Android device that has [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) built in. iPhones don't support [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) at all; they'll fall back to AAC. Which is fine but not high-res. For most people. The 40mm dynamic drivers and a frequency response of 20 Hz to 40 kHz are standard for this category. The 16-ohm impedance is low. So they should get loud without much power. That's a flexible approach, though it means you might need to fiddle with the app to get the sound profile you want out of the box. One note: the frequency response spec is listed as "40 KHz" in one place and "20 Hz - 40 KHz" in another. On the practical side, the latter is the complete range. That upper limit is beyond human hearing, which is typical for Hi-Res certification, but it doesn't guarantee the quality of the response in that range.

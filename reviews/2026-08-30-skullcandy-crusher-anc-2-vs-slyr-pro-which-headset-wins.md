# Skullcandy Crusher ANC 2 vs SLYR Pro: Which Headset Wins?

### Skullcandy Crusher ANC 2 vs Skullcandy SLYR Pro: Which One Fits Your Head?

Choosing between Skullcandy’s Crusher ANC 2 and the SLYR Pro isn’t about picking a “better” headphone—it’s about picking the right tool for the job. One is built for bass-heavy immersion on the go, the other is a gaming-focused headset with a very different set of priorities. Here’s how they actually compare.

### The Crusher ANC 2: Bass You Can Feel

The Crusher ANC 2 is Skullcandy’s flagship for music lovers who want physical, tactile bass. The signature feature is the adjustable sensory bass slider, which uses haptic drivers to vibrate the earcups. At low settings it adds a subtle thump; crank it up and it genuinely shakes your head. That’s not a gimmick—it’s a completely different way to experience bass-heavy genres like hip-hop, EDM, and dubstep. If you’ve never tried it, it’s worth a listen before you judge.

Beyond the bass, you get active noise cancellation that’s effective but not class-leading. It handles airplane hum and office chatter well, though it won’t completely silence louder noises like a vacuum. The ANC is adjustable, which is nice if you want to stay aware of your surroundings on a walk. Battery life sits around 50 hours with ANC off, and about 40 with it on—solid numbers for long trips. The build is mostly plastic, but it feels sturdy enough for daily commuting.

One thing to note: the Crusher ANC 2 is not a neutral headphone. The bass slider changes the tuning significantly, and even at the lowest setting, the low end is boosted. If you’re after a flat, analytical sound for mixing or critical listening, this isn’t it. It’s a fun, immersive headphone, not a studio tool.

### The SLYR Pro: Built for Gaming, Not Music

The SLYR Pro takes a completely different approach. It’s a wired gaming headset with a detachable noise-cancelling microphone, designed for consoles, PC, and mobile. There’s no Bluetooth, no ANC, and no haptic bass. What you get instead is a lightweight, comfortable frame that’s built for long sessions, plus a microphone that makes your voice sound clear to teammates.

Sound-wise, the SLYR Pro leans toward clarity in the mids and highs, which helps with footsteps and directional audio in games. It’s not a bass-heavy headset—if you’re playing single-player games with lots of explosions, it might feel a bit thin compared to the Crusher. But for competitive shooters where positional audio matters more than rumble, it does the job well. The ear cushions are plush and breathable, which makes a real difference during a four-hour raid.

The wired connection means no latency and no battery anxiety, but it also tethers you to your device. There’s no wireless option at all, so if you want to move around the room while listening, this isn’t the one. The microphone is a clear win here though—it’s better than any built-in mic on a Bluetooth headphone, and the noise cancellation on the mic side helps cut out keyboard clatter.

### Which One Should You Buy?

Ask yourself what you’ll use the headset for most. If you’re primarily listening to music on a commute, on a plane, or at your desk, the Crusher ANC 2 is the obvious choice. The haptic bass is something you won’t find elsewhere, and the wireless freedom plus ANC make it a better all-rounder for everyday life. If you also game casually, it works fine as a wired headset with a mediocre built-in mic—just don’t expect great voice quality in party chat.

If gaming is your main use case, the SLYR Pro is the smarter pick. The microphone quality is dramatically better, the sound signature suits competitive play, and the comfort is excellent for long sessions. You lose wireless and ANC, but you gain a tool that does its specific job well. ### Practical Buying Tips

If you’re leaning toward the Crusher ANC 2, try the bass slider in person before committing. Some people find the haptic effect distracting after a few hours, and it’s not something you can judge from reviews alone. Also, check whether the ANC hiss bothers you—some units have a faint white noise when ANC is enabled, which can be annoying in quiet rooms.

For the SLYR Pro, consider your platform. It works with most consoles, but check compatibility with your specific setup—some older controllers might need an adapter. Also, since it’s wired, think about cable length and whether your gaming spot is close enough to your console or PC.

### Final Thoughts

Neither headset is a jack-of-all-trades. The Crusher ANC 2 is a bass-forward, wireless, noise-cancelling headphone that happens to have a mic. The SLYR Pro is a wired gaming headset with a great mic and a balanced sound. Choose based on your primary activity, not on marketing. If you mostly listen to music and want something fun, get the Crusher. If you mostly game and want clear comms, get the SLYR Pro. Simple as that.

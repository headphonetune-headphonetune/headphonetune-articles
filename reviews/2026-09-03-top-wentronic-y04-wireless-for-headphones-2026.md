# Top Wentronic Y04 Wireless for Headphones (2026)

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/top-wentronic-y04-wireless-for-headphones-2026/)

The Wentronic Y04 is a budget-oriented, foldable over-ear headphone with [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and a very long battery life. Based on the specs, it's aimed at travelers and commuters who want basic [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)I found that and all-day playback without spending flagship money. The 48-hour battery claim is the headline characteristic, and the foldable design with a detachable cable adds real versatility. But the data also reveals some trade-offs: no water resistance, a modest 33-foot Bluetooth range, and a 40ms latency figure that suggests it's not built for gaming or video editing. Here's what the details actually mean. Battery and charging The 48-hour playtime is the standout spec. That's roughly double what many mid-range wireless headphones offer, and it translates to about a full workweek of daily commutes without reaching for a charger. If you fly long-haul or forget to charge things regularly, this is a genuine practical advantage. The charge time is listed at 2 hours, which is reasonable for a battery that size - you're not getting fast-charge tech like some premium models, but a full top-up overnight or during a layover is fine. The spec sheet doesn't mention whether that 48 hours is with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)I would say that we found that on or off, which is a meaningful omission. Many manufacturers quote battery life with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) disabled; if you run noise cancellation continuously, expect that number to drop, possibly to the 30-hour range. Don't plan around the full 48 hours unless you're willing to test it yourself. [Active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) The [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is described as blocking ambient noise for an immersive experience. That's the marketing language. The technical data doesn't specify the depth of cancellation in decibels, which is common at this price point. What you should expect is that it will dull consistent low-frequency sounds - engine hum, air conditioning, the rumble of a train - but it won't silence voices or sudden noises the way superior [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) from Sony or Bose does. For a product in this tier, that's acceptable. The real question is whether the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) adds noticeable hiss or pressure, which budget implementations sometimes do. For everyday listening, the 36 customer reviews at 4.3 stars suggest most buyers are satisfied, but that's a compact sample size. If [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) quality is your top priority, this isn't the category to shop in; if you just want to take the edge off a noisy commute, it'll likely do the job. Sound quality and drivers The 40mm dynamic drivers are standard for over-ear headphones. Impedance is 32 ohms, which is low enough to be driven easily by a phone or laptop without an external amp. Sensitivity is listed at 105dB, which is on the higher side - these will get loud without much power. Frequency response is 20Hz to 20kHz, which covers the full human hearing range, though that spec alone tells you nothing about tuning. In real-world use, the marketing claims "rich, clear, and detailed audio," but with no frequency response curve provided, you can't verify bass emphasis or treble roll-off. What you can infer is that the 40mm drivers in a closed-back, over-ear design typically deliver decent bass presence and reasonable separation for the price. Don't expect audiophile-grade resolution, but for podcasts, pop, rock, and calls, it should be more than adequate. The 40ms latency is worth noting. That's high for Bluetooth - most modern codecs aim for under 30ms - and it means audio-visual sync could be noticeably off when watching video or gaming. If you're primarily listening to music or taking calls, latency won't matter. If you watch a lot of YouTube or Netflix on your phone, you might notice lips moving slightly ahead of dialogue. Some devices compensate for this in software, but not all do. Bluetooth and connectivity [Bluetooth 5](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is listed, which is a solid, current version. It offers better range and stability than older iterations, though the 33-foot range is conservative - that's about 10 meters, which is typical for Bluetooth in real-world conditions with walls and interference. The compatible devices list includes cellphones, laptops, PCs, tablets, and televisions, so pairing flexibility is broad. There's no mention of multipoint connectivity, which would let you connect to two devices simultaneously and switch between them. That's a attribute often absent at this price, so if you frequently jump between a laptop and phone, you'll need to manually disconnect and reconnect. The detachable 3.5mm audio cable is a genuinely useful addition. It means you can use these wired if the battery dies or if you're on a plane with a seatback entertainment system that doesn't support Bluetooth. That's a practical fallback that many wireless headphones in this range omit. Comfort and build The ear cushions are faux leather over memory foam, with a plastic enclosure. Faux leather is common at this price - it's less breathable than real leather or fabric, so your ears may get warm during long sessions, but it's also easier to wipe clean. Memory foam is a decent sign for comfort; it conforms to your ears better than basic foam padding. The foldable design is a major plus for travel - these will collapse into a more space-saving shape for packing, though the listing doesn't specify whether a carrying case is included. The "what's in the box" section only mentions ear cushions, which is ambiguous. It might mean spare cushions are included, or it might be a listing error. That said, the weight isn't specified, which is frustrating because over-ear comfort depends heavily on weight and clamping force. At this price point, plastic construction usually keeps weight manageable, but without a number, you're gambling on whether these feel light or heavy after A hour. For most people, the enclosure materials - faux leather, memory foam, and plastic - suggest a budget build. That's not inherently bad, but it means the hinges and folding mechanism are the most likely failure points over time. Treat the folding joints gently. Controls and calls Control type is listed as button control, which is actually a positive. Physical buttons are easier to use blindly than touch controls - you can find them by feel without fumbling. The specific button layout isn't detailed, but typical implementations offer volume, play/pause, call answer, and [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) toggle. On the practical side, the product is listed for hands-free calling, and with a built-in microphone, it should handle phone calls adequately. Budget headphone mics often sound thin and pick up background noise, so don't expect crystal-clear conference calls. For casual phone conversations in a quiet room, it'll be fine. In a noisy street or open office, the person on the other end may struggle to hear you clearly. Durability and environment There's no water resistance rating - the spec explicitly says "Not Water Resistant." That means no rain, no heavy sweat, no gym use. If you're a runner or you work out with headphones, these are the wrong choice. For comparison, they're designed for commuting, office use, and travel - sedentary or lightly active scenarios. The EU spare part availability duration of 2 years is a petite but useful detail. It suggests the manufacturer plans to support repairs or replacements for at least two years, which is better than many budget brands that disappear after a year. The warranty is listed as 1 year, which is standard but not generous. Value and positioning With a best sellers rank of #553 in over-ear headphones, this is a niche product from a less-known brand. Wentronic isn't a household name like Sony or Sennheiser, which means you're relying on the 36 customer reviews for real-world validation. A 4.3-star average from that small sample is mildly encouraging but not statistically robust. The absence of detailed specs on [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) depth, weight, and battery life with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) active means you're buying on faith to some degree. What you're getting for the money is a foldable, long-battery-life headphone with fundamental [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and a wired fallback. That combination is genuinely useful for frequent travelers. What you're not getting is premium sound, strong water resistance, low-latency performance, or the polish of established brands. The 40ms latency alone disqualifies these for serious gaming or video editing. In daily use, the lack of water resistance disqualifies them for fitness use. If your use case is sitting on a train, plane, or office chair listening to music or podcasts, they fit the bill. Summary: The Wentronic Y04 is a reasonable for travelers and commuters who prioritize battery life and foldability over sound precision or ruggedness. The 48-hour playtime, detachable cable, and physical buttons are practical strengths, while the 40ms latency, missing water resistance, and unspecified [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) performance are clear limitations. It's worth considering if you need a long-lasting, packable headphone for casual listening and calls, but it's not the right pick for gym use, gaming, or anyone who demands top-tier noise cancellation.

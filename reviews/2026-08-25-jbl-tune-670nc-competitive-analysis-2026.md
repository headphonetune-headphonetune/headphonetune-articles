# JBL Tune 670NC: Competitive Analysis (2026)

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/jbl-tune-670nc-competitive-analysis-2026/)

Let's talk about the JBL Tune 670NC. These are on-ear wireless headphones with adaptive [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we found , and they sit in a very crowded part of the market. The headline numbers are a 70-hour battery claim, [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/)we could see . And JBL's signature bass-heavy sound. But what does that actually mean if you're standing in a noisy kitchen trying to focus, or packing for a flight? First, the battery. JBL claims up to 70 hours with Bluetooth on and [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off. That's a generous number. And it's worth being clear about the asterisk. The fine print says 44 hours with Bluetooth on and [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on. So your real-world figure depends on how much you use the [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). If you're on a long-haul flight with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) active the whole time. Expect closer to two full days of heavy use, not three. The 70-hour figure is for when you're just streaming music without the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) engaged. Either way. This is a long-lasting pair. For comparison. Many competitors in this price bracket struggle to hit 30 hours* (based on available data) with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on. The quick charge is also practical: a 5-minute top-up gives you 3 hours of playback. That's genuinely useful when you forgot to charge overnight. The full recharge takes about 2 hours via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c), which is standard. The [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) itself is described as "adaptive." That means the headphones adjust the level of cancellation based on your environment. It's not the same as premium over-ear models from Sony or Bose, but it's a step above fundamental [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). There are two ambient modes: Ambient Aware lets outside sound in, and TalkThru lowers the music and boosts voices so you can chat without removing the headphones. Both are controlled through the JBL Headphones app. For a commute. This is handy-you can keep an ear on your stop announcement or have a quick conversation with a cashier without fumbling with the earcups. The catch is that the on-ear headphones don't seal as well as over-ear models, so passive isolation is weaker. The [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) has to work harder. And you'll still hear some high-frequency noise like chatter or keyboard clatter. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're buying these specifically for a noisy office, manage your expectations. [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current and supports LE Audio, but note the fine print: LE Audio is coming via an over-the-air update at a later stage. So right out of the box, you're getting standard [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/), which is still solid for connection stability and range. The spec sheet lists a 10-meter range, which is typical. Multipoint pairing is included. So you can connect to your laptop and phone simultaneously. That's a practical feature for anyone who takes calls on a computer but listens to music on a phone. The app also has a Smart Audio & Video mode that lets you prioritize sound quality or reduce latency for gaming and video. That's a pleasant touch-it acknowledges that Bluetooth audio often has a slight delay, and gives you a toggle to address it. Sound quality is where JBL leans on its brand name. The Pure Bass Sound is exactly what it says: bass-forward. If you listen to hip-hop, electronic, or pop, these will sound energetic and punchy. The 32-ohm impedance and 98 dB sensitivity mean they're straightforward to drive from any phone or laptop-you won't need an amp. The frequency response is listed as 20 Hz to 20. 000 Hz. Which is the standard human hearing range and doesn't tell you much about actual tuning. What matters is the driver size and tuning, and the spec sheet doesn't give driver dimensions. Based on the sound signature JBL is known for. Expect the low end to be prominent, mids to be slightly recessed, and highs to be present but not piercing. For a casual listener, that's fine. For someone who wants neutral, accurate monitoring, this isn't it. The build is all plastic, and the weight is 174 grams. That's light. On-ear headphones with this kind of weight are easy to wear for extended wear, and the padded headband and soft ear cushions back that up. The foldable design means they flatten down for storage, which is a plus for travel. But the plastic construction and the fact that they're not water resistant (the spec sheet explicitly says "Not Water Resistant") means you shouldn't use these for intense workouts. Sweat could damage them over time. They're fine for a casual walk or commute, but not for a run. The ear placement is on-ear. Which is a key trade-off. On-ear headphones rest on your ears rather than enclosing them. For comparison, this makes them lighter and cooler, but they press on your ears. After a couple of hours, some people find that pressure uncomfortable. The clamping force here isn't extreme. But if you have sensitive ears or wear glasses, you might want to try them before committing. The 3.5mm jack and detachable audio cable are included. So you can use them wired if the battery dies or if you're on a plane with a wired entertainment system. That's a practical fallback that not all wireless headphones offer. Call quality is covered by VoiceAware, which lets you hear your own voice during calls. This is a attribute that sounds minor but matters-without it, you tend to shout because you can't hear yourself. The built-in microphone and hands-free calls are standard. But there's no mention of noise reduction for the mic, so expect decent but not stellar call clarity in noisy environments. The controls are buttons, not touch, which is actually a plus for many people-buttons are easier to use blind, and you're less likely to accidentally pause music when adjusting the fit. Voice control works with Google Assistant and Siri. But that's a standard integration, not a standout aspect. The app is where customization happens. There's an integrated equalizer. So you can tweak the sound profile if the default bass-heavy tuning isn't to your taste. That's a significant point. The out-of-the-box sound is very JBL. But the EQ lets you pull back the bass and boost mids if you want. The app also handles the ambient modes and the Smart Audio & Video toggle. It's not a different app. But it does what you need. The downside is that you have to use the app to access some of the features-there's no physical button for ambient mode on the headphones, based on the spec sheet. That means if you want to switch between [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and Ambient Aware. You need to pull out your phone. That's a minor annoyance in daily use. The included accessories are minimal: the headphones, a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) cable, a detachable audio cable, a quick start guide, and a warranty card. There's no carrying case in the box, despite the spec sheet mentioning a carrying case material. That's a notable omission. The foldable design helps, but you'll want to buy a pouch if you plan to toss these in a bag. For everyday listening. The warranty is 2 years, which is decent for the price bracket. Now, the numbers. The customer reviews are 4.5 out of 5 stars from over 2,200 ratings. That's a solid score. And it aligns with what the specs suggest-a consistent, no-frills product that does what it promises. The best sellers rank puts it at #62 in on-ear headphones. Which indicates strong sales but not dominance. There's no pricing data here, so I can't comment on value relative to cost, but based on the attribute set, this is positioned as a mid-range option. In real-world use. It's not competing with top-tier [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphones. And it's not a budget impulse buy either. The 70-hour battery claim is the biggest differentiator. If you travel a lot and hate carrying chargers, this is a strong argument. But remember, that's with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off. With [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on, it's 44 hours, which is still excellent. The adaptive [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and ambient modes are useful, but the on-ear form factor limits how effective the [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) can be. For most people, the sound is fun and energetic, but it's not accurate. The build is light and portable. But plastic and not sweat-resistant. The lack of a carrying case is a real oversight. Especially for a product aimed at travel. Summary: The JBL Tune 670NC is a solid mid-range option for someone who wants long battery life. A bass-forward sound. And the convenience of wireless with a wired fallback. The adaptive [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is effective enough for commutes and casual use. But the on-ear design and plastic build mean it's not a quality noise-cancelling experience. If you need precise sound. Heavy sweat resistance, or the strongest [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) available, look elsewhere. But for everyday listening, travel, and calls, this is a practical, dependable choice. Who this is for: This is for the person who commutes, listens to music for hours, and wants a pair of headphones that won't die on a long trip. It suits casual listeners who enjoy a bass-heavy sound and don't need studio accuracy. It's also good for someone who wants multipoint pairing to switch between a laptop and phone. And who appreciates the option to go wired when needed. If you prioritize battery life and portability over high-end materials and modern [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), this fits. Who should look elsewhere: If you're an audiophile who demands neutral, detailed sound, these aren't for you. If you work out heavily and need sweat resistance, skip these. If you're in a very noisy environment and need maximum [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). An over-ear model with stronger [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) will serve you better. And if you hate using apps to change fundamental settings like ambient mode.

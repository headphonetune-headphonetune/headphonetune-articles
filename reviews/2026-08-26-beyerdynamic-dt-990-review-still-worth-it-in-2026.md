# Beyerdynamic DT 990 Review: Still Worth It in 2026?

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/beyerdynamic-dt-990-headphones-review-2026/)

### The DT 990 in 2026: Still Worth Your Attention?

Some headphones age like milk. Others age like a good pair of leather boots — they get more comfortable, more familiar, and you forget how much you rely on them until they’re gone. The beyerdynamic DT 990 falls firmly into that second camp. It’s been around for years, and it’s still one of the most talked-about open-back headphones for good reason. But is it the right pick for you in 2026? Let’s talk about what it actually does well, where it stumbles, and who should be looking elsewhere.

### What the DT 990 Gets Right

The first thing you notice is the soundstage. It’s wide, airy, and genuinely immersive — the kind of presentation that makes you forget you’re wearing headphones. Instruments get their own space, and there’s a sense of depth that closed-back models rarely match. If you’re into classical, jazz, or live recordings, this is a real treat.

The treble is another story. It’s bright. Very bright. Some call it “sparkly,” others call it “piercing.” The DT 990 has a famous V-shaped tuning — boosted bass and boosted highs with a scooped midrange. That makes it exciting for rock, electronic, and metal, where cymbals and guitar harmonics get extra energy. But if you’re sensitive to sibilance (that harsh “s” sound in vocals), you’ll want to audition these before committing. The treble peak around 8-9 kHz can be fatiguing over long sessions, especially at higher volumes.

Bass response is solid for an open-back. It’s not a basshead’s dream — the sub-bass rolls off noticeably — but the mid-bass punch is satisfying. Kick drums have weight, and bass lines are clear without muddying the mids. For a 250-ohm model, it’s surprisingly dynamic.

### The Comfort Question

Here’s where the DT 990 shines. The velour earpads are plush, the clamping force is moderate, and the headband distributes weight well. You can wear these for hours without feeling the need to take them off. That’s rare. The build is all-plastic, but it’s the good kind of plastic — sturdy, flexible, and designed to survive daily use. They’re not going to win any beauty contests, but they’ll outlast many fancier-looking competitors.

### Who Should Skip These?

If you’re planning to use the DT 990 with a phone or laptop directly, think again. The 250-ohm version needs an amplifier to sound its best. Without one, they’ll play — but they’ll sound thin, lifeless, and quiet. You need at least a decent USB DAC/amp or a dedicated headphone amp to unlock their potential. That’s an extra cost and an extra cable on your desk.

Also, they leak sound. A lot. Open-back design means everyone around you hears what you’re listening to, and you hear everything around you. Not ideal for an office or a shared room. If you need isolation, look at closed-back alternatives instead.

### The 2026 Context

The market has changed a lot since the DT 990 first appeared. There are now excellent budget closed-backs, wireless models with ANC, and even planar magnetic options at similar price points. The DT 990’s value depends on what you already own and what you’re after. If you’re building a dedicated listening setup — a quiet room, a good amp, a music library you care about — it’s still a fantastic choice. If you need something portable, private, or plug-and-play, it’s not.

### The Bottom Line

The DT 990 is not a beginner’s headphone. It demands an amp, it demands a quiet room, and it demands a tolerance for bright treble. But if you meet those demands, it rewards you with a soundstage and clarity that few headphones at its level can match. It’s a classic for a reason — just make sure you know what you’re getting into before you buy.

The LC-dolida Sleep Headphones are a sleep mask with flat speakers sewn into the fabric - and that combination is the whole point. You get light blocking and audio in one item, no earbuds pressing into your ear canal, no headband plus mask stacked on your face. Anyone who has tried to sleep on their side with earbuds knows the concern: the bud jams into the ear and the seal breaks the moment you turn your head. A flat speaker inside a fabric mask avoids that entirely. The battery claim deserves scrutiny. The listing says 8-10 hours of playing time and describes it as enough to "support a whole night." For most people, a full night is seven to nine hours, so 8-10 hours covers it with a little margin. But that's a best-case figure. Bluetooth audio at low volume draws less power than at high volume, and battery capacity fades over time. If you sleep eight hours and like your audio loud, you're likely running the battery to the edge or past it. A 1-2 hour charge time is reasonable for a mask this size, and it means a lunchtime top-up can get you through another night if you forget to plug it in. It's not the newest version, but the practical difference between 5.2 and later revisions for a sleep mask is negligible. What matters more is whether the connection holds when your phone is on a nightstand a few feet away, and 5.2 handles that fine. The built-in microphone means you can take a call without taking the mask off, which is a agreeable-to-have if you wear it for meditation or yoga, less useful if you're asleep. The listing mentions using it for calls, but a microphone buried in a fabric mask against your face is not going to sound as clear as a phone held to your ear or a dedicated headset. Treat call quality as a convenience, not a strength. The light-blocking claims are specific: full coverage, a nose area designed to prevent leakage, and total blackout. Nose gaps are the usual failure point on sleep masks. And the listing addresses it directly, which suggests the design at least attempts to solve a known problem. The 4.2 rating across tens of thousands of reviews suggests most buyers find the fit workable. But fit is the one thing you can't evaluate from a spec sheet.

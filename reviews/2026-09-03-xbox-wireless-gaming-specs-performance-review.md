# Xbox Wireless Gaming: Specs &amp; Performance Review

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/xbox-wireless-gaming-specs-performance-review/)

The Xbox Wireless Headset is Microsoft's own attempt at a first-party gaming headset - and the specs show a product designed to slot cleanly into the Xbox ecosystem. It isn't trying to be a do-everything audiophile device. And you shouldn't buy it expecting one. What it offers is a tightly integrated wireless experience for Xbox owners who want decent sound. Dependable chat, and no cable clutter. Build & Materials The earcups are wrapped in polyurethane (PU) leather, which is the standard for this price tier. it's soft enough for long sessions, but PU doesn't breathe like fabric or real leather. If you game in a warm room or tend to run hot, expect your ears to get sweaty after a couple of hours. For everyday listening, the headband is adjustable and the All things considered, weight is listed at 11.36 ounces, which is light enough that you shouldn't feel constant pressure on your head. Microsoft calls the design "flexible," which usually means the headband has some give rather than being a rigid metal clamp. that's capable for durability in a casual sense. But don't mistake it for a rugged travel headset. This is a home-use device, not something you toss into a backpack daily. The ear placement is over-ear with an oval shape. Which is the right call for gaming. On-ear headsets press against the cartilage and get uncomfortable quickly. Over-ear cups surround the ear entirely, which helps with passive noise isolation. The spec sheet lists "Sound Isolation" as the noise control method, and that's accurate - this isn't [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)our analysis shows . The PU foam and closed-back design will block out some ambient noise like a humming fridge or a keyboard clatter, but a loud TV in the same room will still bleed through. If you need silence, this headset won't deliver it. Performance Expectations The audio driver is 40mm, which is the typical size for a gaming headset. That isn't large - and it isn't small - it's the industry default because it balances clarity with enough bass presence for explosions and gunfire. In real-world use, the frequency response is listed as 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, which covers the full human hearing range. That spec is meaningless for marketing purposes since nearly every headphone claims this. But it does mean you won't miss low-end rumble or high-frequency details like footsteps. The impedance is 32 ohms, which is low enough to be driven easily by a console controller or a phone. Sensitivity is rated at 90 dB, which is on the lower side. Most gaming headsets sit around 100 dB or higher. What that means in practice is that this headset may need more volume steps to reach the same loudness as a competitor. On A Xbox. You will likely be fine, but if you connect it to a phone or a laptop with a weak headphone amp - which is most laptops - you might find yourself maxing out the volume slider during quiet game sections. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a real limitation worth noting. The real selling point here's spatial audio. The headset accommodates [Dolby Atmos](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) at no extra cost, which is unusual. [Dolby Atmos](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/)we found for headphones usually requires a license fee or a subscription on Xbox. Microsoft is bundling it in. Which saves you roughly fifteen dollars and removes the friction of setting it up. For most people, it also works with Windows Sonic and DTS Headphone:X. All three are virtual surround technologies that process positional audio. In games that support them, footsteps, gunfire - and environmental cues get directional placement. You will hear an enemy approaching from behind-left versus behind-right. Which is a genuine competitive advantage in shooters. The 40mm drivers aren't going to give you the pinpoint imaging of a high-end open-back headphone. But for a closed-back wireless unit, the spatial processing does a lot of heavy lifting. If you play games like Call of Duty, Apex Legends, or Fortnite, this is the attribute that justifies the buy. On the practical side. The microphone installation is dual boom mics with voice isolation and auto-mute. The "dual" part isn't two visible mics - it's a mic array that picks up your voice and filters out background noise. The LED indicator on the mic lights up when you're muted, which is a small quality-of-life detail that prevents you from yelling at your squad while muted. Auto-mute is a capability that cuts your mic when you are not speaking. Which keeps your teammates from hearing your breathing or your mechanical keyboard. The sensitivity of that auto-mute is adjustable in the Xbox Accessories app. Which is satisfactory because overly aggressive auto-mute can clip the start of your sentences if you speak softly. The mic isn't detachable. So this headset isn't well-suited for someone who wants a clean look for music listening outside of gaming. Battery and Charging Battery life is rated at up to 20 hours. That is average for a wireless gaming headset. Some competitors push 30 hours* (based on available data), but 20 hours is enough for a full day of heavy gaming or a weekend of moderate use. The charging time is listed as "2E+1 hours. " which is a formatting error in the data. Reading it as 20 hours would be absurd, and the more logical interpretation is 2 hours for a full charge. That aligns with most [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) headsets in this category. For comparison, the cable is [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)what caught our attention for charging, which is the modern standard, so you can use any phone charger you already own. there's no 3.5mm* (based on available data) headphone jack listed, which means this headset is entirely wireless. If the battery dies mid-session. You can't plug in a cable and keep playing. You will need to stop and charge. that's a notable limitation for players who forget to charge their gear. The battery is internal and rechargeable, and there's no mention of a removable battery, so when the cell degrades after a couple of years, you can't swap it out easily. Connectivity This is where the headset shines. It uses Xbox Wireless radio to connect directly to Xbox consoles without a dongle or base station. That is a clean setup - you power on the headset and it pairs with the console instantly. It also has [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) for connecting to PCs. Phones - and tablets. The killer feature is simultaneous dual connectivity. You can be connected to your Xbox via the wireless radio and your phone via Bluetooth at the same time. That means you can be in a game party chat on your console while taking a phone call, or listen to a podcast from your phone while gaming. The [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) range is listed at 10 meters, which is standard. You can walk to another room and keep the connection, but walls will degrade it quickly. [Bluetooth LE](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) Audio is also supported, which is a newer standard that offers lower latency and better battery efficiency on compatible Windows 11 PCs and mobile devices. The shared audio attribute lets you stream to a second pair of Bluetooth headphones so a friend can listen in, though that requires the second device to also support LE Audio, which is still not widespread. Software and Customization The Xbox Accessories app handles EQ tuning, bass boost, auto-mute sensitivity, mute light brightness, and mic monitoring levels. Mic monitoring is the feature that plays your own voice back into your ears so you do not shout when wearing a closed-back headset. Having adjustable levels is better than a fixed implementation. Since some people find mic monitoring distracting while others need it to gauge their speaking volume. The EQ customization is standard - presets and custom bands. This isn't a niche audiophile tuning tool, but it's enough to adjust the sound signature to your preference. If you find the default sound too bass-heavy or too flat, you can tweak it. The app is only available on Xbox and Windows, so if you primarily use this headset with a phone or a Mac, you're stuck with the default tuning. What Is Included The box contains the headset and a user manual. That is it. There is no carrying case, no [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging cable is explicitly listed in the included items, which is odd since the cable features are listed as "[USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) Charging." The product data suggests a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) cable is involved. But the "What's in the Box" section only mentions the manual. You should assume you may need to supply your own charging cable. The warranty is 90 days from the manufacturer, which is short. Most electronics offer at least a year. In daily use, this is a low-confidence signal for long-term durability. The product isn't water resistant. So keep it away from drinks and don't wear it while exercising. The folding feature is listed as "On Ear," which is a data error - the headset is over-ear, and the folding description does not match. It likely doesn't fold flat for storage, which makes it bulkier to travel with. What isn't Clear from the Data The data doesn't specify whether the earcups are replaceable. PU leather degrades over time, and if you can't swap the pads, the headset becomes less ergonomic as the material flakes. For typical buyers. The Bluetooth codec support isn't listed beyond LE Audio. It doesn't mention whether it works with [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) or AAC. Which matters if you plan to use it for music on a phone. Standard Bluetooth SBC is likely the fallback. Which is fine for gaming but not great for music fidelity. The sensitivity rating of 90 dB is low, but the data does not clarify whether that is measured at a specific frequency or under a specific load. It is a single data point that suggests the headset is not particularly efficient. But it is not a definitive measure of quality. The weight of 11.36 ounces is listed. But without the headband clamping force spec, you can't fully predict comfort. A light headset with a tight clamp is worse than a heavier one with a gentle fit. Summary: The Xbox Wireless Headset is a solid first-party option for Xbox Series X|S and Xbox One owners who want a no-fuss wireless connection, [Dolby Atmos](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) included, and simultaneous Bluetooth for phone calls or music. It delivers good positional audio for competitive gaming. A decent mic with useful auto-mute features, and enough battery life for long sessions. The build is average. The sensitivity is low, and the lack of a wired backup connection means a dead battery ends your session. If you are primarily A Xbox player. This is a straightforward recommendation. If you are a PC gamer, a mobile user, or someone who needs a headset for music and travel, you can find better value elsewhere. Who this is for: This is for the Xbox player who wants a wireless headset that pairs instantly with the console, supports [Dolby Atmos](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) without an extra fee, and lets them take a phone call mid-game without taking the headset off. It suits gamers who play shooters and want spatial audio for positional awareness, and who don't mind the closed-back PU leather design. it's also for someone who wants a portable headset that doesn't need a dongle cluttering the console's USB ports. Who should look elsewhere: Skip this if you're a PC-only gamer - you can find headsets with better mic quality and lower latency for the same money. And you don't benefit from the Xbox Wireless radio. Skip it if you want a headset for music listening. Because the low sensitivity and gaming-tuned sound signature are not well-suited for critical listening. Skip it if you need a wired backup option or a detachable mic. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the Xbox Wireless Gaming
### Can I use this headset with my Xbox console and phone at the same time?
Yes. It pairs directly to an Xbox console via Xbox Wireless and to a PC or mobile device via Bluetooth 5.3, and you can connect to both simultaneously.

 I spent some time going through the specs on the Xbox Wireless Gaming, and here is what stood out to me.### Does the headset support Dolby Atmos, and do I have to pay extra for it?
It supports Dolby Atmos, Windows Sonic - and DTS Headphone:X. Dolby Atmos is included at no extra cost.

### How long does the battery last. And how long does it take to charge?
Battery life is up to 20 hours. The charge time is listed as 20 hours, which likely refers to a full charge cycle, but the spec is ambiguous-expect a full charge to take several hours via the USB-C cable.

### Can I adjust the volume and game/chat balance without taking the headset off?
Yes. Rotate the earcup dials to quickly adjust volume and game/chat balance, and use the push buttons for power, pairing, mute, and balance controls.

### Is this headset wired or wireless only?
It is wireless only-there is no headphone jack. It connects via Xbox Wireless or Bluetooth 5.3. And charges through a USB-C cable.

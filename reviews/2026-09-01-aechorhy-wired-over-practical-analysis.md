Noise Control and Isolation The technical specs list "noise control: None." This is a significant point. These aren't noise-canceling headphones. And the listing doesn't claim they're. However, the lack of [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)what caught our attention is expected at this price. What matters more is passive isolation-how well the earcups seal around your ears to block out external sound. The listing mentions "over-ear" placement and "breathable materials," but it doesn't specify the clamping force or the depth of the ear cushions. If the ear pads are shallow or the clamping force is weak, you'll get poor passive isolation. That means in a noisy environment-a coffee shop, a room with a loud air conditioner-you'll hear a lot of ambient noise bleeding through. For a studio setting, this is fine; you're typically in a quiet room. But if you're planning to use these for tracking vocals in a room with other musicians, the lack of isolation means you'll hear them playing through the headphones, which can bleed into your mic if you're recording. This is a standard limitation of open-back or poorly sealed closed-back headphones. The data suggests these are closed-back (since they're marketed for DJ use), but the isolation quality is unverified.

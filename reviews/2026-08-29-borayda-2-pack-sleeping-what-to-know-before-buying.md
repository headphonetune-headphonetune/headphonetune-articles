# BORAYDA 2-Pack Sleeping: What to Know Before Buying

These are flat. Flexible Bluetooth headphones built into a fabric headband, marketed primarily for sleeping but also for lounging and light activity. You get two of them in a pack, one black and one grey. Before you buy, it's worth understanding what you're actually getting, because this is a niche product with specific strengths and clear limitations. What's in the Box and Build Quality The package includes two complete headband units. That's the whole product. The headbands are made from a mix of spandex. Nylon - and elastic. Which means they will stretch to fit most head sizes comfortably. For everyday listening. The fabric is soft, and the double-layer lining is designed for warmth, which makes sense for a product intended for sleep. The detachable and washable design is a significant practical attribute. You can pull the electronics out and launder the fabric band. Which is almost essential for something you'll wear while sleeping and sweating in. If that weren't the case, the headband would quickly become unhygienic. The fact that it's washable adds real long-term value. There's no water resistance rating here. In real-world use, the listing explicitly states "Not Water Resistant." That means you should not wear these for intense workouts where you'll sweat heavily, and definitely not in the rain. They're for low-moisture activities like sleeping. Reading, or a casual walk. The lack of water resistance is a hard limit on the "sport" use case mentioned in the specs. Comfort and Fit The "Over Ear" placement and earpiece shape mean the speakers sit directly on your ears, not around them. This is a key detail. Because the speakers are flat and embedded in the fabric, they press against your ears. For side sleepers, this can be a problem. The pressure on the ear cartilage can become uncomfortable after a while, even with a soft fabric covering. The design is better for back sleepers or people who don't move much. The elastic fabric will keep the band in place. But the on-ear pressure is something you should expect. It's not a circumaural design that encloses your ear; it's a supra-aural design that sits on top of it. The warmth aspect is a double-edged sword. For most people. The double-layer lining will keep your ears warm in a cold room, but it could also make your ears feel hot and stuffy during warmer nights or in a warm climate. There's no active cooling or breathability mesh mentioned beyond "great breathability," which is a marketing claim. In practice that a thick fabric headband will trap heat. If you run hot at night. This could be uncomfortable. Battery and Charging The built-in 250mAh battery is rated for 18 hours of continuous playback or talk time. That's a solid figure for this class of product. On the practical side. It means you can wear them for a full night's sleep for several nights in a row before needing to charge. The standby time of 120 hours is also capable; if you leave them on your nightstand for a week. They'll still hold a charge when you pick them up. Charging takes 1.5 to 2 hours, which is reasonable. You plug them in before you go to bed. And they're ready for the next night. The battery life is one of the stronger points here. For a device meant to be worn for hours at a time. 18 hours is enough for a full week of nightly use on a single charge, assuming you sleep for about eight hours a night. That's a practical, usable number. Bluetooth and Connectivity [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/)we noticed is the current standard, and it's decent to see it here. For comparison, the practical reality is that the any device with standard Bluetooth audio output will work. The lack of a headphone jack means you're entirely dependent on Bluetooth. If your device doesn't support Bluetooth audio, these won't work for you. The controls are three physical buttons on the headband panel. They handle uncomplicated functions: power, pairing, play/pause, answer calls, skip tracks, and volume. The LED indicator light shows connection status. In daily use. This is a minimal, functional control scheme. There's no app support. No equalizer. And no custom button mapping. What you get is what you get. For a sleep product, that's fine. You set it and forget it. Sound Quality This is where you need to temper your expectations. For typical buyers, the drivers are a 30mm dynamic driver with a 12.5mm ring neodymium magnet motor. The sensitivity is rated at 112 dB, which is high. Impedance is 32 ohms, which is standard. The marketing claims "deep. Punchy bass" and "pure and comfortable sound." Here's the truth: these are flat speakers pressed against your ears through fabric. The sound will be acceptable for podcasts. Audiobooks, white noise, and ambient music. It will not be great for key music listening. For everyday listening. The high sensitivity means they can get loud, but loudness isn't the issue. The issue is that the physical design doesn't create any soundstage. There's no ear cup to create an acoustic chamber. The bass will be present but not tight. The highs will be rolled off. The sound is best described as "adequate." For a sleep device, that's acceptable. You don't need audiophile quality to listen to rain sounds or a sleep meditation track. But if you're expecting the kind of sound you'd get from a pair of over-ear headphones from a dedicated audio brand, you'll be disappointed. The "superior sound quality" claim is marketing. In practice that the sound is fine for its intended purpose, but it's not a selling point for music lovers. Microphone and Calls There's a built-in microphone. And the specs mention talk time. The mic is embedded in the fabric headband. In practice. This means call quality will be mediocre. The person on the other end will hear you. But the audio will likely sound muffled or distant because the mic isn't positioned close to your mouth. It's fine for a quick call, but not something you'd want for a long business conversation. For a sleep product. This is a minor capability. You might use it to take a quick call before bed, but you won't rely on it. What's Missing The spec sheet doesn't list a weight. Given the materials. It's likely light, but there's no official number. There's no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we noticed , and the "noise control" spec is listed as "None." The headband gives you passive noise blocking from the fabric, but it's minimal. If you need to block out a snoring partner or street noise. These won't do much. You'd need a dedicated noise-canceling product or earplugs. The lack of [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is a significant consideration for a sleep product. The fabric will muffle some sound, but it's not designed for isolation. Value and Practicality You're getting two units for the price of one, which is a decent. Having two means you can wash one while using the other, or keep one at home and one for travel. The washable design is the standout feature here because it directly addresses the hygiene issue that plagues fabric headphones. The product is a 3-in-1: earmuffs. Sleeping headphones, and a Bluetooth headband. The earmuff function works well because the fabric is warm. The sleeping headphone function works if you can tolerate the on-ear pressure. In real-world use, the Bluetooth headband function is fine for casual listening around the house. Summary: The BORAYDA 2-Pack Sleeping Headphones are a practical, budget-oriented solution for listening to audio while lying down or lounging.

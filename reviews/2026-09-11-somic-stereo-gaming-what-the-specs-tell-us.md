Each ear houses a 40 mm dynamic driver. that's the standard size for gaming headsets across nearly every price tier, from entry-level to mid-range, so this isn't a differentiator. What matters more is the frequency response, listed at 22 KHz. That number is odd. A 22 KHz ceiling is below the 20 Hz to 20 KHz range that human hearing can technically reach, and it is below the 20 KHz to 40 KHz range that many gaming headsets advertise. A 22 KHz top end means the headset will not reproduce the highest overtones that give cymbals and certain sound effects their air and sparkle. For footsteps and gunfire in a game, that largely doesn't matter. For music, it may sound slightly closed-in compared to a headset that reaches 40 KHz. The spec sheet doesn't list a frequency floor, so there's no way to know how far down the bass extends. You are left trusting the 40 mm driver to handle low-end without knowing whether it reaches the sub-bass region that makes explosions feel physical. Impedance is 32 ohms. that's a good number to see on a wired gaming headset. For everyday listening, it is low enough that a phone, tablet, or controller headphone jack can drive it to reasonable volume without a separate amplifier. If you're plugging into a DualSense controller, a laptop, or an iPhone with a dongle, the headset should get loud enough. It also means the headset isn't picky about sources. You can move it between devices without worrying about whether one can power it. Compared to the 16-ohm headsets that are common in the budget gaming space, 32 ohms is slightly more demanding but still well within the range of standard consumer electronics. It won't reproduce the kind of volume headroom you get from a 250-ohm desktop headphone paired with an amp, but that isn't what this product is for. Connectivity is wired only, through a 3.5 mm jack. The spec sheet lists a fixed cable, meaning you cannot detach the cable and replace it if it breaks. That is a meaningful durability concern. Detachable cables are common on headsets above the entry level because the cable is the first thing to fail. With a fixed cable, a single snag on a chair leg or a sharp bend at the jack can render the whole headset useless. The cable does terminate in a 3.5 mm plug that the manufacturer says works with Xbox One, Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4, PC, laptop, iPad, and iPhone. In real-world use, the included splitter cable is for devices that have separate headphone and microphone jacks, which is most desktop PCs.

# SHOKZ OpenFit 2+: Evaluating the Features

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/shokz-openfit-2-evaluating-the-features/)

The SHOKZ OpenFit 2+ is an open-ear earbud. Which means it doesn't seal your ear canal. Instead, it rests on the outer ear with a hook, leaving your ears open to ambient sound. That design choice drives almost everything about this product, so let's start there. The core appeal is situational awareness. You can hear traffic. Conversations, a train announcement, or a colleague calling your name while music plays. That's a safety attribute for runners and cyclists. And a practical one for office workers who need to stay reachable. For everyday listening, the trade-off is acoustic: without a seal, bass response is physically weaker than what you get from in-ear buds, and you'll hear more environmental noise competing with your audio. SHOKZ has addressed this with a dual-driver setup - a 17.3mm dynamic driver for lows and a separate tweeter for highs - plus a technology called DirectPitch 2.0 that aims sound more directly at your ear canal. That helps. But it doesn't replicate the isolation of a sealed earbud. If you want to block out a noisy commute or a loud gym, this is the wrong tool. Weight and Fit Each earbud weighs 9.4g. That's light enough that you'll forget you're wearing them after a few minutes. In real-world use. The ear hooks are made of nickel-titanium, a shape-memory alloy that returns to its original form after bending. That means the hook should hold its shape over time and keep a consistent grip on your ear. Which is a genuine advantage over plastic hooks that can loosen or snap. The contact points use SHOKZ's Ultra-Soft Silicone 2.0. Which is exactly what it sounds like - a soft-touch material that cushions where the bud meets your ear. The total item weight is 74.8g, which includes the case and earbuds. The case alone is 53g. And it's compact at 15.2cm long, 7.6cm wide, and 2.3cm tall. That's pocketable. Though not as compact as some in-ear cases. For most people, the case is made of polycarbonate, so it's rigid and should protect the buds, but don't expect a top-tier, soft-touch finish. Sound Quality The specs list a 19-ohm impedance and 96.5dB sensitivity. Those are reasonable numbers for a Bluetooth earbud. The 19-ohm impedance is low, which means the internal amplifier doesn't need to push hard to drive the drivers - that's productive for battery life. The 96.5dB sensitivity is on the higher side. Meaning the buds can get loud without excessive power draw. In practice. You won't struggle to hear these in a quiet room, and they should have enough headroom for outdoor use. On the practical side. The audio architecture is more detailed than typical open-ear buds. Most use a single full-range driver. The OpenFit 2+ uses two: a 17.3mm low-frequency driver and an independent tweeter. That's a meaningful difference. It allows the crossover to send bass to the larger driver and highs to the tweeter, which reduces distortion at higher volumes and gives the sound more separation. The OpenBass 2.0 AI algorithm further shapes the low end. Combined with [Dolby Audio](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/)in our experience support - which you activate in the SHOKZ app - the sound profile leans toward clarity and detail rather than raw, thumping bass. The spec sheet mentions "thumping bass. " but be skeptical of that claim in an open-ear design. Physics limits how much low-end you can feel without an ear seal. What you'll get is a surprisingly full sound for an open-ear bud, with crisp highs and a decent low-mid presence, but it won't match the bass impact of a sealed in-ear or over-ear headphone. Battery and Charging The earbuds deliver up to 11 hours of continuous playback on a full charge. That's strong for any earbud, and especially good for an open-ear model. With the charging case. Total playtime reaches 48 hours. That's A remarkable number - most earbuds in this category offer 24-36 hours* (based on available data) total. The case has a 600mAh battery. Which is solid for a case this size. A 10-minute quick charge gives you 2 hours of playback. Which is useful for a quick top-up before a workout or a commute. Full charge time for the earbuds is about 1 hour. And the case takes about 1 hour to recharge as well. The case accommodates both [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)in our experience and wireless charging. For comparison. The wireless option is a convenience that's still not standard across all earbuds at this level. If you already have a [Qi wireless](https://www.wirelesspowerconsortium.com/)our analysis shows charger on your desk or nightstand. You can just drop the case on it and not think about cables. The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port is there for when you need a faster, wired top-up. This is a well-rounded charging installation - no complaints here. Water Resistance and Durability The [IP55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating covers two things: the first digit (5) means dust-resistant - not fully dustproof. But protected against most particles that could cause harm. The second digit (5) means protected against low-pressure water jets from any direction. In real terms. This handles sweat, rain, and splashes without issue. You can run in the rain, work out heavily, or get caught in a downpour without worrying about the buds. What it doesn't cover is submersion. Don't drop them in a pool or a sink. Also. Note that the charging case is not IP-rated - the spec sheet doesn't list a rating for it, so treat it as less protected. Keep the case dry. That said, connectivity and Controls [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current-generation, offering stable connections and better power efficiency than older versions. The stated range is 33 feet. Which is standard for Bluetooth - expect that to drop if walls or your body are between the buds and your phone. MultiPoint Pairing is included. So you can connect to two devices simultaneously, like a phone and a laptop. That's a practical feature for anyone who switches between a work computer and a personal phone throughout the day. The transition between devices is handled by the buds - when a call comes in on your phone while music plays from your laptop, the buds switch to the call automatically. Controls are a mix of physical buttons and app customization. In daily use, the buds have physical multifunction buttons, which is actually a preference for many people - touch controls can be finicky, especially during exercise when you're sweaty or wearing gloves. Physical buttons give tactile feedback, so you know you've pressed them. The SHOKZ app lets you remap what those buttons do, and it also includes EQ customization with four presets plus custom settings. There's also a "Find My Earbuds" feature that makes the buds ring so you can locate them when they're lost in a couch cushion or a gym bag. That's a small but genuinely useful feature. Microphones and Calls There are four AI-enhanced microphones for call quality. For typical buyers. The spec sheet says they filter background noise for clear communication in noisy environments. That's a common claim, and the reality varies. Four mics with AI processing is a solid unboxing on paper - the multiple mics allow the system to isolate your voice from ambient noise more effectively than a single-mic design. For calls on a busy street or in a windy area, expect your voice to come through, but don't expect studio-grade clarity. The open-ear design actually helps here: since your ears aren't blocked. You'll hear the person you're talking to clearly, and you'll also hear your own voice more naturally, which prevents the "talking in a tunnel" sensation that some in-ear buds cause. Compatibility The buds work with Android, iPhone, Mac, PC, and smartwatches. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) ensures broad compatibility with modern devices. There's no headphone jack - these are wireless-only, which is expected for this product category. The included cable is presumably for charging the case via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c). There's no 3.5mm* (based on available data) cable option, so if your audio source lacks Bluetooth, these won't work. What's in the Box The package includes the OpenFit 2+ earbuds, the wireless charging case, a cable, a carrying bag, and a user manual. The carrying bag is a fine touch - it protects the case from scratches when you toss it in a backpack or purse. For everyday listening, the cable is for the case's [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging port. No extra ear tips are included. But that's because these don't use ear tips - they're hook-style, so sizing is handled by the hook shape and the silicone contact points. The 48-Hour Claim Let's look at that 48-hour number more carefully. That's the combined total of earbud battery (11 hours) plus the case's recharging capacity (roughly 37 additional hours). That assumes you're starting with a fully charged case and fully charged earbuds. And that you're recharging the buds each time they drain. Real-world usage - with volume variations. Call time, and features like [Dolby Audio](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) enabled - will reduce that. Still, even if you get 70% of the claimed total, that's around 33 hours, which is more than most people need for a week of commuting and workouts. The 11-hour single-charge figure is more important for daily use. And it's genuinely good - you can wear these for a full workday plus a workout without reaching for the case. The Noise Control Question The spec sheet lists "[Active Noise Cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" under noise control. But this is misleading. Open-ear earbuds by design can't give you [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) in the traditional sense - there's no ear seal to create a quiet zone. What the OpenFit 2+ likely offers is some form of call noise reduction or environmental noise processing for the microphones, not [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for your listening experience. If you're expecting to block out the world with these. You'll be disappointed. If you want to stay aware of the world. That's the entire point. The "noise control" here is about managing what your microphone picks up, not what you hear. Build Quality and Materials The enclosure is polycarbonate and silicone. Polycarbonate is a rugged. Impact-resistant plastic that's common in electronics. The silicone is at the contact points for comfort. The ear hooks are nickel-titanium. Which is a premium choice - it's the same material used in some medical devices and high-end eyeglass frames because of its flexibility and memory properties. In real-world use, this suggests the hooks will maintain their grip over years of use, not just months. The case is polycarbonate. Which is fine - it's light and protective, though it won't feel as top-tier as a metal or fabric-covered case. Warranty and Support The warranty is 2 years. Which is above average for earbuds - many offer 1 year. That's a meaningful signal of confidence in the product's durability. The brand. SHOKZ, is well-established in the open-ear space; they've been making bone-conduction and open-ear audio products for years. The customer review rating is 4.5 out of 5 stars from over 2. 000 reviews, which is a solid sample size. That's not a guarantee of perfection. But it suggests the product delivers on its core promises Who this is for This is for people who prioritize situational awareness and comfort over audio isolation. Runners and cyclists who need to hear traffic, office workers who want to listen to music while staying available to colleagues, and anyone who finds in-ear buds uncomfortable or isolating. The 11-hour battery life covers a full workday plus a commute. The [IP55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating handles sweat and rain. The physical buttons are a plus for gym-goers who don't want to fiddle with touch controls. The MultiPoint pairing is useful for anyone juggling a phone and a laptop. Who should look elsewhere If you need to block out noise - a loud commute. A busy office, a noisy gym - these are not the right choice. No open-ear earbud can give you the isolation of a sealed in-ear or over-ear headphone. Bass lovers will also be disappointed; the dual-driver unboxing helps. But you can't overcome the physics of an open design. If you frequently take calls in very noisy environments, the four-mic array helps, but a headset with a boom mic will still perform better. And if you prefer touch controls or want a smaller charging case. Other options may fit your habits better. Summary: The SHOKZ OpenFit 2+ is a well-executed open-ear earbud with exceptional battery life. A solid build, and a comfortable fit. For most people. It's a strong choice for anyone who calls for to stay aware of their surroundings while listening to audio - runners, cyclists, office workers, and commuters. The 48-hour total battery, wireless charging, physical buttons, and 2-year warranty are standout features. The trade-offs are the lack of noise isolation and limited bass response, which are inherent to the open-ear design. If you need to block out the world. Look at sealed in-ear or over-ear options.

### Rockpapa L22 Wired and Comfort+ Kids: What Actually Changed

Rockpapa built a small following by making kids' headphones that parents could hand over without much worry. The L22 Wired was one of the brand's simpler options, and the Comfort+ Kids is the follow-up that seems meant to replace it in the lineup. The two share a family resemblance, but the differences are worth understanding before you buy one over the other. Here's how they compare on the things that matter for daily use.

### The Core Difference: Wired vs Wireless

This is the split that defines both models. The L22 is a wired headphone, which means a physical cable running from the earcups to whatever device is playing audio. The Comfort+ Kids leans into wireless convenience, pairing over Bluetooth so there's no cord to snag on a backpack strap or tangle in a pocket.

For a young kid, that distinction matters more than it sounds. A wired connection means no charging, no pairing, and no dead battery halfway through a car ride. You plug in and it works. Wireless, on the other hand, trades that simplicity for freedom of movement and one less cable to babysit. Neither is objectively better — it depends on where and how the headphones get used.

### Build and Comfort

Both are built around the same basic idea: on-ear cups with a headband sized for smaller heads. The Comfort+ name signals that Rockpapa put effort into padding and fit, and the design reflects that priority. If your child complains about pressure after twenty minutes, that's usually where on-ear models fail, and it's the first thing to test in person if you can.

Weight is another factor that rarely shows up in spec sheets but shows up fast in real use. Lighter headphones stay on during longer sessions. Heavier ones get pushed off or adjusted constantly. Without a hands-on measurement, it's hard to say which is lighter, but the Comfort+ naming suggests Rockpapa treated comfort as the headline feature rather than an afterthought.

### Volume Limiting and Safety

Kids' headphones live or die on volume limiting. The whole point of buying a dedicated kids' pair instead of handing over adult headphones is protecting young ears from levels that cause damage over time. Rockpapa markets these as safe for children, and both models follow that playbook. What's harder to verify without testing is whether the limiting is consistent across sources — some implementations clamp loud inputs well, others let peaks through.

### What Changed From the L22 to the Comfort+

The short version: Rockpapa moved from a straightforward wired design to something aimed at everyday family use with fewer cables. The Comfort+ name points at fit and padding improvements, and the wireless feature set is the real headline. If you already own the L22 and it's working fine, there's no urgent reason to switch. If you're buying fresh, the Comfort+ is the more current choice — provided you're ready to manage charging and pairing.

That trade-off is the crux. Wireless headphones for kids introduce a maintenance routine: charge them, pair them, re-pair them when they connect to the wrong device. For older kids who can handle that, it's fine. For younger ones, a wired pair is often the path of least resistance.

### Buying Advice

- **Choose wired** if the headphones stay with one device, battery anxiety is real in your house, or the user is young enough that pairing is a hassle.

- **Choose wireless** if the headphones move between devices, get used while moving around, or the kid is old enough to manage charging.

- **Check the fit** regardless of which you pick. Kids' heads vary a lot, and a headphone that pinches gets abandoned fast.

- **Verify volume limiting** before trusting any kids' model with long listening sessions.

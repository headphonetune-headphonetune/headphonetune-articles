Let's get one thing straight right away: these aren't headphones for your commute, your gym bag, or your phone. The Sony WHRF400 is a dedicated home-theater accessory, built for one specific job - letting you watch TV without bothering anyone else in the house. If that's your use case, the specs tell a pretty clear story. If you were hoping for something more adaptable, you'll want to keep reading, because there are some real limitations baked into this design. What This Thing Actually Is The WHRF400 is a wireless over-ear headphone system that uses RF (radio frequency) rather than Bluetooth. That's the single most important detail here. RF transmission is what gives you the 150-foot range and the ability to pass through walls, but it also means the transmitter has to physically connect to your TV. There's no pairing with a phone, no multipoint connection, no app. The base station plugs into your television's RCA audio outputs or a 3.5mm headphone jack, and the headphones sync with that dock. That's the whole setup. The listing includes the transmitter dock and A RCA cable in the box. So you won't need to buy anything extra to get started - assuming your TV has one of those two outputs. Performance Specs: What the Numbers Mean The driver size is listed as 40mm, which is standard for over-ear headphones. For everyday listening. The frequency response is 10 Hz to 22,000 Hz, which covers the full audible range and then some. For TV dialogue, music, and movie soundtracks, this is more than adequate. The impedance is 32 ohms and sensitivity is 100 dB, which tells you these are easy to drive and reasonably productive - you won't need to crank the volume to hear clearly, and the built-in amplifier in the transmitter handles the heavy lifting anyway. The "noise reduction system" mentioned in the features isn't [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we noticed . It's a voice-enhancing circuit that boosts mid-range frequencies so dialogue cuts through background noise in the mix. That's a smart choice for a TV headphone. Because muffled speech is the most common complaint with home theater audio. In real-world use, it won't block out the sound of your spouse vacuuming, but it will make actors' lines easier to understand without turning up the volume. The 150-foot wireless range is generous. But be realistic about what RF signals travel through walls, so you could theoretically walk to the kitchen or bathroom and keep listening. In practice, interference from other RF devices, thick walls, and the quality of your home's construction will all play a role. The spec sheet says Bluetooth range is 45.72 meters (about 150 feet). Which seems to be a copy-paste error, since these are RF headphones - not Bluetooth. Either way. The stated range is more than enough for a typical living room or even a petite house. Compatibility: The Large Catch This is the most important limitation to understand. The WHRF400 requires your TV to have either RCA audio outputs (the red and white jacks) or a 3.5mm headphone jack. Many modern flat-screen TVs have dropped RCA outputs entirely, and some don't have headphone jacks either. If your TV only has optical audio out or HDMI ARC, this system won't work without an additional adapter or converter box, which isn't included. The listing is upfront about this requirement, so it's not a hidden gotcha - but it's a real compatibility hurdle you need to check before buying. The wireless technology is RF, which means it won't interfere with your Wi-Fi or Bluetooth devices, and it won't be affected by them either. That's a plus for reliability. But it also means these headphones are locked to the included transmitter dock. You can't use them with your phone, tablet, or computer unless you plug the dock into those devices, which is possible but clunky. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the Sony RF400 Wireless
### How do I connect these headphones to my TV?
You connect the included transmitter dock to your TV using either the RCA audio out or the headphone jack. Then the headphones sync with the dock automatically. The required cable is supplied in the box.

### How far can I move from the TV before the signal drops?
I think we noticed the wireless range is up to 150 feet, so you can move around your home freely. The listed Bluetooth range of 45.72 meters (about 150 feet) matches this, but walls and interference may reduce it.

### How long does the battery last and how long does it take to charge?
You get up to 20 hours of listening time on a full charge. Recharging takes about 7 hours. So plan to charge them overnight or while you're not watching.

### Do these headphones have noise cancellation?
No, they don't have active noise cancellation. They do have a noise reduction system that clarifies sound. But they won't block out ambient noise like ANC headphones would.

### Are these headphones water-resistant?
No. They aren't water resistant. Keep them away from moisture and use them only indoors for TV watching.

In real-world use, the full case charge takes about 2 hours, which is standard. Fit and Physical Design These are compact. The listing says 15% smaller and 40% lighter than the previous Go Air model. The case is described as smaller than a credit card, which is a useful reference point. It's genuinely pocketable, which is more than you can say for some bulky charging cases on the market. If you're carrying these in a pocket while running or at the gym, that matters. There are three sets of gel eartips included. That's standard, but it's also significant. A proper seal is the difference between decent sound and good sound. And it's also the difference between earbuds that fall out and earbuds that stay put. The fit is described as "snug," and the earpiece shape is "rounded tip." Without trying them, you can't know if they'll fit your ears perfectly, but the inclusion of multiple tip sizes gives you a fighting chance. The earbuds are rated as water-resistant and sweatproof, which aligns with the gym/running use case they're pushing. It keeps the weight down, which contributes to the "40% lighter" claim. They won't feel high-end in the hand, but they're not supposed to. Sound Quality and EQ Here's where you need to manage expectations. The driver is a 6mm dynamic driver. And the frequency response is listed as 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. That's the standard human hearing range, but it doesn't tell you much about how they actually sound. A 6mm driver is petite, which means bass won't be thumping like a pair of over-ear headphones with larger drivers. That's physics, not a flaw. What JLab does offer is EQ3 Sound, which gives you three presets: JLab Signature, Balanced, and Bass Boost. You can cycle through these via the touch controls, or you can use the JLab app to customize further. This is a significant feature at this price. Many budget earbuds just have one sound signature and you're stuck with it. Here, you can at least tune it to your preference. If you like bass-heavy music, the Bass Boost preset will help compensate for the space-saving driver. If you want a more neutral sound, Balanced is there. The app also lets you adjust touch controls and Be Aware settings. The impedance is 16 ohms, which is low. That means they're straightforward to drive, which is capable for a phone. They won't need a headphone amp, and they'll be plenty loud from a standard smartphone output. Bluetooth and Connectivity [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is the current standard, and it's satisfactory to see it here. It offers better range and stability than older versions. The range is listed as 10 meters. Which is standard for Bluetooth. The "Auto On & Connect" feature means they pair with each other when you take them out of the case, and then connect to your phone. That's a convenience characteristic that should work seamlessly, though it's not specific to JLab. Dual Connect is notable - you can use either earbud independently. That's useful for calls or if you want to keep one ear open to your surroundings. The microphone is a MEMS mic in each bud, which is standard. Call quality on budget earbuds is often mediocre, and without testing, it's hard to say if these are better or worse than average. The spec sheet doesn't give you any special call enhancement features, so expect average performance. There's also Google Fast Pair support for Android devices. That's a agreeable touch - it makes pairing with Android phones nearly instant, and it also enables the "find my earbuds" feature. If you're an iPhone user. You won't get that, but standard Bluetooth pairing will still work. What's Missing There's no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) ([ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)). The listing says "noise control: None." That's a key omission. What you get instead is "Be Aware Mode. " which is the opposite of [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) - it lets ambient noise in so you can hear your surroundings. That's useful for running or walking in traffic. But it doesn't replace [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for blocking out airplane noise or office chatter. You'll be relying on the passive isolation from the gel eartips, which is decent but not [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)-level. There's no wireless charging for the case. You'll need to plug it in via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c). Not a dealbreaker. But it's a feature that's becoming more common even at budget prices. There's no mention of multipoint connectivity, which would let you connect to two devices simultaneously (like a phone and laptop). If that's a feature you need, you'll have to look elsewhere. The controls are touch-based. That's fine, but touch controls on budget earbuds can be finicky. Accidental touches when adjusting the earbuds are common. The listing doesn't specify what gestures control what, so you'll need to read the manual or figure it out in the app. The App and Ecosystem The JLab app is a differentiator.

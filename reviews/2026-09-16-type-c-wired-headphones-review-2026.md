Practical trade-offs The core trade-off with any wired [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) earbud is convenience versus reliability. You never worry about battery life, pairing, or lag. But you're tethered. And you're dependent on a single port. If your phone's [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port is also your charging port, you can't charge and listen at the same time unless you have a splitter. That's a real limitation for long flights or workdays. The second trade-off is device compatibility. [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio isn't as standardized as it should be. Some Android phones handle [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio natively; others require the earbuds to have their own DAC. The listing says "plug-and-play connectivity without any adapters," which implies an onboard DAC, but it doesn't state that explicitly. If your device doesn't support [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio output, these won't work, and the listing doesn't give you a compatibility list to check against. The third trade-off is the lack of any meaningful performance data. No frequency response. No impedance, no driver size, no weight, no cable length, no IP rating.

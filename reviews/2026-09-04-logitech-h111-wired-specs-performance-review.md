# Logitech H111 Wired: Specs &amp; Performance Review

my view is the Logitech H111 is a wired headset with a 3.5mm jack, and the most notable thing about it isn't any single spec - it's the sheer simplicity of the entire package. There's no battery, no Bluetooth pairing, no software to install, and no controls on the cable to fumble with. You plug it into anything with a headphone jack, and it works. That's the whole pitch, and for a lot of people, that's genuinely enough. Let's start with the connectivity because that defines what this thing is. The H111 uses a standard 3.5mm audio jack, which means it will work with a huge range of devices - PCs, Macs, laptops, smartphones, and tablets - provided those devices actually have a physical jack. For everyday listening, the listing says it's compatible with Windows 10 or later, Windows 8, Windows 7, Windows Vista, Mac OS X 10.5 or later, Chrome OS, Linux Kernel 2.6 plus, and iOS or Android devices. That's basically every mainstream operating system from the last decade and a half. The practical takeaway: if you're using a modern ultrabook or a recent smartphone that has ditched the headphone jack, this headset won't work without an adapter. If you're on an older laptop, a desktop, or a device that still has the port, you're fine. The cable is 7.71 feet (235 cm) long. That's a notable detail. Most wired headsets in this price range give you around four to six feet, which is fine for sitting at a desk but restrictive if you want to stand up or move around during a call. The H111's longer cable gives you the freedom to pace around a bit while on a conference call or stretch your legs during a long webinar. It's a petite thing, but it's clearly been thought about. The downside is that you'll have a lot of cable to manage when you're sitting at your desk. You'll probably want to bundle or route it somehow, otherwise it'll drape across your keyboard and mouse area. The microphone is where this headset earns its keep. In real-world use, it's a noise-cancelling mic, which in this context means it filters out background room noise so your voice comes through more clearly on the other end. The boom rotates 180 degrees, so you can wear it on your left or right side, and it's flexible enough to bend into position near your mouth. When you're not using it, you can tuck the boom up and out of the way, which is handy if you're just listening to music or watching a video. This is a practical design choice for someone who spends a lot of time on voice calls - Skype, Zoom, Teams, webinars, that sort of thing. The mic isn't detachable, so you're stuck with the boom attached to the left earcup, but the rotating mechanism mitigates that annoyance. One thing to be careful about: the listing includes a spec that says "[Active Noise Cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" under noise control, but that's misleading. For most people, the actual attribute is a noise-cancelling microphone, which reduces background noise on your voice transmission. That isn't the same as [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on the headphones themselves, which would reduce ambient noise you hear. The H111 has no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for your ears. If you're in a loud coffee shop and you want to block out chatter so you can focus on your music, this headset won't do that for you. The ear cushions are foam and the earcups sit on your ears - they'll block a little passive sound, but not much. The "[Active Noise Cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" label in the specs appears to be a data error or a mislabeled field. Trust the feature list, not that stray spec line. Sound quality is no-frills but serviceable. The H111 uses dynamic drivers with a frequency response of 20 Hz to 20 kHz and an impedance of 32 ohms. That frequency range covers the full audible spectrum for human hearing, but the actual sound quality will be shaped by the driver size and the materials used - and the listing doesn't specify driver size, which is a bit of a gap. In practice, you should expect this to sound like a decent budget headset: clear enough for voice calls, acceptable for music and games, but not something that will impress you with deep bass or sparkling highs. The 32-ohm impedance is low, which means it's uncomplicated to drive from a phone, laptop, or tablet without needing a separate amplifier. That's a satisfactory thing for a device meant to plug into anything. The ear placement is listed as "Over Ear" in one field and "On Ear" in another, with the style name saying "Foam Cushion." The description mentions "ultra-soft foam ear cushions." Looking at the actual product, the H111 is an on-ear headset - the earcups rest on your ears rather than fully enclosing them. That distinction matters for comfort over long sessions. On-ear headphones can start to feel uncomfortable after A hour or two because the pressure is concentrated on your ears. The foam cushions help, and the adjustable headband lets you customize the fit, but this isn't a headset you'll want to wear for an eight-hour gaming marathon. It's better suited for shorter calls, meetings, or casual listening sessions. On the practical side, the earpiece shape is described as "around the ear," which suggests the cushions are shaped to contour around your ear, but they're still on-ear, not over-ear. The enclosure material is listed as faux leather and plastic, though the style name says foam cushion - the ear pads themselves are foam, while the headband and earcup exteriors are plastic. The weight is listed at 0.07 kg, which is about 70 grams* (based on available data). That's very light. You'll barely notice it on your head, which helps with comfort, but it also means the build is mostly thin plastic. This isn't a rugged headset. If you toss it in a backpack and sit on it, it will likely crack. There's no carrying case included - the "carrying case material: Ballistic" spec appears to be a data artifact, not an actual included accessory. The H111 is a desk headset. Treat it accordingly. The controls situation is worth noting. The listing says "No controls" and "control type: No controls." That means there's no inline volume wheel, no mute button, no play/pause button on the cable. You control everything from the source device. If you're on a laptop and someone calls you, you'll need to reach for your keyboard or screen to adjust volume or mute yourself. That's a minor inconvenience, but it's also a reflection of the price point. The H111 is a bare-bones headset, and the lack of controls keeps it simple and keeps the cost down. If you need inline controls, look elsewhere. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the Logitech H111 Wired
### Which devices can I use this headset with?
It works with any device that has a 3.5mm audio jack, including PCs, Macs, laptops, smartphones, and tablets. Operating system support covers Windows 7 and later, Mac OS X 10.5 or later, Chrome OS, Linux Kernel 2.6+, and iOS or Android.

### Can I wear the microphone on either side?
Yes, the microphone boom rotates 180 degrees, so you can position it on your left or right side. The flexible mic can also be tucked away when not in use.

### Does the noise-cancelling feature apply to the microphone or the headphones?
The noise-cancelling is on the microphone, not the headphones. It reduces background room noise during calls, but the headphones themselves don't offer active noise cancellation for your listening environment.

### How long is the cable, and does it have any special features?
The cable is 7.71 feet (235 cm) long, giving you room to move during calls. it's retractable, though the listing doesn't specify whether that means the cable itself coils or if it's just a storage feature.

### What is the sound quality like for music and games?
The headset uses dynamic drivers with a frequency range of 20 Hz to 20 kHz and 32 ohm impedance, which is standard for full stereo sound in calls, music, and games. It's not audiophile-grade, but it covers the typical audible range for everyday use.

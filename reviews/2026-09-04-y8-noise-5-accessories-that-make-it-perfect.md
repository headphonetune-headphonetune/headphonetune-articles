# Y8+ Noise? 5 Accessories That Make It Perfect

### Why Your 1Mii Y8+ Noise Deserves a Proper Kit

The 1Mii Y8+ Noise is a solid pair of over-ear ANC headphones, but like most modern wireless cans, they only reach their full potential with a few thoughtful extras. The stock cable is fine, the carry case does its job, but after a few weeks of daily use, you’ll start noticing the gaps. Here’s what actually matters to buy alongside them in 2026.

### 1. A Right-Angle 3.5mm Cable

The Y8+ ships with a straight plug, which is fine at a desk. But if you listen on the move—especially with your phone in a jeans pocket—a straight jack puts constant stress on both the headphone’s socket and the cable’s strain relief. A right-angle cable, ideally one with a braided jacket, keeps the connection snug against the housing and avoids that annoying bend-and-crack over time. Look for one with a mic and inline remote if you plan to take calls in wired mode; the Y8+’s own controls don’t work when the cable is plugged in.

### 2. A Hard-Shell Case (Not the Pouch)

If you’re using the included soft pouch, you’re risking the plastic hinges on the Y8+’s folding mechanism. These headphones are light and fold flat, which makes them easy to toss into a bag—but that’s exactly when they get crushed by a laptop or a water bottle. A hard-shell case with a rigid interior divider keeps the ear cups from pressing against each other, which over time can flatten the memory foam. It’s a boring purchase, but it’s the one that prevents a $100+ repair later.

### 3. A USB-C to 3.5mm DAC Dongle

Here’s a quirk of the Y8+: when you use the included USB-C cable for wired listening, the headphones’ internal DAC handles the conversion. That’s fine for podcasts, but the output can sound slightly veiled compared to what the headphone drivers are actually capable of. A small USB-C dongle with a dedicated DAC chip bypasses that internal processing. On the Y8+, this yields cleaner treble and a tighter low end—especially noticeable on acoustic tracks or poorly mastered rock. Just make sure the dongle supports at least 24-bit/96kHz; anything less and you’re not gaining much.

### 4. Replacement Earpads (After 12 Months)

The stock pads on the Y8+ are comfortable out of the box, but the protein leather starts flaking after roughly a year of regular wear—sooner if you live in a humid climate. Replacing them with a third-party set of memory foam pads isn’t just about comfort. It also restores the seal that ANC relies on. A worn-out pad that’s lost its shape will let ambient noise leak in, and the ANC will have to work harder, which drains battery faster. Buy a spare set early, even if you don’t need them yet, because pad manufacturers often discontinue models without notice.

### 5. A Bluetooth Transmitter for Non-Bluetooth Sources

The Y8+ supports aptX Adaptive, which is great for Android phones and modern laptops. But if you want to use these headphones with an older TV, a desktop amp, or an airplane seatback entertainment system, you’re stuck with the 3.5mm cable. A small Bluetooth 5.3 transmitter with a 3.5mm input solves that. Plug it into the source, pair it with the Y8+, and you get wireless freedom plus the low-latency codec support. For movies, this matters a lot—standard Bluetooth lag can make dialogue feel off by half a second.

### What to Skip

Don’t bother with a headphone stand unless you have a dedicated desk setup. The Y8+ folds flat, so hanging them on a stand actually stretches the headband over time. Also skip any “premium” replacement cables unless you’re hearing actual static or cutouts—the stock cable is fine for 90% of listeners. And while a portable DAC/amp might seem tempting, the Y8+ is already sensitive enough that an external amp adds nothing but bulk.

### Final Thoughts

Start with the right-angle cable and the hard case—those are immediate quality-of-life wins. The DAC dongle is worth it if you’re picky about wired sound, and the earpads are a long-term investment you’ll thank yourself for later.

The audio section is where the listing gets ambitious and where you should read carefully. Triple-layer 13mm dynamic drivers with a polymer diaphragm is a legitimate configuration for bass-forward tuning, and 13mm is on the larger side for in-ear buds, which usually translates to stronger low-end response at the expense of some precision in the mids. For most people, the claim of "sufficiently strong bass that naturally connects to the mid and high frequencies" is vague, but the driver size accommodates the idea that this is a bass-leaning sound signature. The codec support is more interesting: AAC, SBC, and Qualcomm [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) Adaptive are all listed. [AptX](https://www.aptx.com/)in our experience adaptive is a genuine step up from SBC for Android users with compatible devices. Offering better bandwidth and lower latency. But here's the catch - [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) Adaptive requires a Qualcomm chipset on both ends. If your phone uses a different chipset. You'll fall back to AAC or SBC, and the difference in sound quality will be noticeable to anyone listening critically. The listing doesn't specify which Bluetooth chipset is inside the earbuds, so whether [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) is actually available to you depends on your source device. That's a real limitation worth knowing before you buy. The noise cancellation claim deserves scrutiny. The features section says "4 ENC Mics" and "ENC [noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) technology that picks up your voice precisely and blocks out 80% background noise." ENC stands for Environmental Noise Cancellation. And it's relevant to understand what that means: ENC is about the microphones filtering noise on your voice during calls, not about silencing the world around you for your music. The technical specifications list "[Active Noise Cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" as a feature. Which is a different thing entirely, and the listing never actually describes an [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) system - no mention of feedforward or feedback mics. No transparency mode, no adjustable levels. Taken together. The most likely reading is that these earbuds have ENC for calls but do not have true [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for listening. The phrase "[noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)" in the title is doing a lot of work that the specs don't fully back up. If blocking out a noisy commute or a loud office is your main reason for buying, this is a significant caveat. The passive isolation from the in-ear silicone tips will help, but that's a different mechanism from [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). The "dustproof" claim is included in the same sentence, but [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) doesn't actually cover dust - the X in [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) means dust protection isn't rated. If dust resistance matters to you. The listing is overstating what the rating guarantees.

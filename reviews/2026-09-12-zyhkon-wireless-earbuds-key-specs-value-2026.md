# ZYHKON Wireless Earbuds: Key Specs &amp; Value (2026)

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/zyhkon-wireless-earbuds-key-specs-value-2026/)

You're on a packed train, someone's on a call two seats over, and the announcement system is crackling overhead. That's the scenario ZYHKON is targeting with the Z9pro - a set of wireless earbuds with a touchscreen charging case, hybrid noise cancellation, and a Rosa colorway. Here's what the specs actually tell you. How much noise can these actually cancel? The Z9pro lists hybrid [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)based on our review rated at up to 38dB of ambient reduction. Hybrid means the system uses microphones both inside and outside the ear to sample noise, which generally handles a wider range of frequencies than a single-mic setup. 38dB is a solid figure - not the deepest [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in my opinion you'll find, but enough to take the edge off engine rumble, air conditioning hum, and general transit noise. It won't erase a crying baby or a loud conversation entirely, and the spec sheet doesn't break down performance by frequency, so treat 38dB as a ceiling rather than a guarantee across all environments. For calls, there's ENC (environmental noise cancellation) on the microphone side. ZYHKON claims it filters out 90% of background voices during calls. That's a bold number, and the data doesn't explain how it was measured or in what conditions. What it means practically: the person on the other end should hear less of what's behind you, but you should still expect some bleed in genuinely loud places. ENC helps; it doesn't create a studio booth. There's also a transparency mode, which pipes outside sound in so you can hear announcements or order a coffee without pulling the buds out. That's a standard feature now, and its inclusion here's expected rather than superior. What's the deal with the touchscreen case? This is the Z9pro's headline attribute and its biggest differentiator. The charging case has a touch display that lets you control volume, skip tracks, answer calls, toggle [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we found that and transparency, set alarms, and trigger a locate-earbuds function - all without touching your phone. You can also switch between the five EQ modes from the case. Whether that's genuinely useful depends on your habits. If you're running or your phone is buried in a bag, having track and volume control on the case is practical. If your phone is usually in your hand, the case screen is a novelty you'll use for a week and then forget. The specs don't say whether the case screen stays on, how bright it gets, or whether it drains the case battery faster - those are real questions the listing doesn't answer. One thing worth noting: the case is described as having a "smart touch display," and the features list mentions alarms and find-my-earbuds. Those are unusual for earbuds at any price and suggest a fairly ambitious firmware. The data doesn't tell us how responsive the screen is or whether it works with gloves, so there's some unknown here. How long does The battery lasts? The earbuds are rated at 6-8 hours per charge, with up to 48 hours total when you count recharges from the case. The "battery average life" spec lists 6 hours, which is the conservative end of that range - assume 6 hours with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on and volume at moderate levels, and closer to 8 if you're going straightforward on both. 48 hours total is a strong figure for this category. For everyday listening, it means you could take these on a week-long trip without packing a charger, assuming you're listening a few hours a day. The touchscreen case displays real-time battery levels for both the buds and the case, which removes the guesswork - you'll know exactly when you need to plug in rather than waiting for a low-battery chime. The case charges via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c), and the cable is included. There's no mention of wireless charging, so don't expect to drop the case on a pad. What do they sound like? The Z9pro uses 14.2mm dynamic drivers. That's a larger driver than you'll find in many earbuds - most sit between 6mm and 12mm* (based on available data). A bigger driver generally moves more air, which typically translates to stronger bass and a fuller All things considered, sound. ZYHKON describes the tuning as deep bass, balanced mids, and crisp highs. That's the standard marketing triad, and without independent measurements, you should treat it as a starting point rather than a verified sound signature. There are five EQ presets: Standard, Rock, Jazz, Pop, and Classic. You switch between them from the case, not an app. That's a meaningful detail - no app means no firmware updates through your phone, no custom EQ curves, and no account unboxing. It also means you're locked into those five presets. If none of them suits your taste, you have no way to fine-tune further. For most listeners, one of the five will be fine. For anyone who likes to dial in their own sound, the lack of app support is a real limitation. Will they stay in during a workout? In real-world use, the Z9pro is [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rated. That's a meaningful level of water resistance - [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) means the buds can survive being submerged in up to a meter of water for 30 minutes* (based on available data). In practice, that covers sweat, rain, and splashes without issue. You could rinse them off after a muddy run. It doesn't mean you should swim with them, and the rating applies to the earbuds, not necessarily the charging case. The fit is in-ear with three sizes of silicone tips (S/M/L) included. In-ear designs seal in the canal, which helps both noise isolation and stability. The specs describe the buds as portable and ergonomic, but no weight figure is given, so there's no way to quantify how light they actually are. For sport use, the combination of an in-ear seal, multiple tip sizes, and [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) is a sensible package - but fit is personal, and the only way to know if they'll stay put during a sprint is to try them. What about connectivity? [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is the current version and brings real benefits: faster pairing, better power efficiency, and more stable connections in crowded wireless environments. The Z9pro supports one-tap auto-connect after the initial setup, and it's compatible with iOS, Android, tablets, laptops, and smart TVs. For most people, the listing specifically calls out iPhone, Samsung, and mainstream devices. There's no mention of multipoint pairing - the ability to connect to two devices at once, like a laptop and a phone. If that matters to you, the data doesn't confirm it's there. Assume single-device connection unless you confirm otherwise. No headphone jack, obviously. These are wireless-only. There's no wired fallback option if the battery dies. What's in the box? Two earbuds, a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging cable, the charging case, and a user manual. That's it. No extra ear tips beyond the three sizes already on or with the buds, no carrying pouch, no adapter. The essentials are covered, but don't expect extras. What about the warranty and brand reputation? ZYHKON offers a 24-month warranty. That's longer than the standard 12 months and worth factoring in - it suggests the company is willing to stand behind the product, or at least wants you to believe it's. The brand itself isn't a household name, and the product has 178 ratings averaging 4.3 out of 5 stars. That's a decent sample size and a solid average, though not enough to draw sweeping conclusions. The earbuds rank #108 in Earbud & In-Ear Headphones and #885 in Electronics All things considered, which indicates they're selling reasonably well. Where the specs get thin Several things a buyer would want to know simply aren't in the data. There's no weight listed for the buds or the case. No driver impedance or sensitivity figures. No codec support details - we don't know if these handle AAC, [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/), or just SBC. No latency figure, which matters if you plan to game or watch video. No charging time for the case or buds. No information on how many charge cycles the case holds before the battery degrades. The touchscreen case also raises questions the specs don't answer: Does the screen scratch easily? Does It has a always-on mode? How much battery does the display itself consume? These aren't dealbreakers, but they're unknowns. The color The color is listed as "Rosa," which is pink. On the practical side, the images and product name confirm this. If you want black or white, this listing is for the pink version. That's not a flaw, but it's worth being explicit about since color options aren't mentioned. The practical trade-offs The Z9pro's appeal is clear: a touchscreen case that genuinely reduces phone interaction, a large driver for a full sound, strong battery life with a clear display of remaining power, and [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) protection for workouts and weather. The hybrid [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) at 38dB is competitive, and [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current. The trade-offs are equally clear. No app means no custom EQ, no firmware updates, and no find-my-earbuds through your phone (the case handles locating, but that's a different thing). The touchscreen case is a single point of failure - if the screen cracks or the touch layer stops responding, you lose a lot of the product's functionality. No multipoint pairing is confirmed. No wired option. And the brand, while offering a two-year warranty, doesn't have the track record of established audio companies, so long-term durability is an open question. Summary: The ZYHKON Z9pro is a attribute-dense set of wireless earbuds with a genuinely unusual touchscreen case, solid 48-hour total battery life, 38dB hybrid [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) waterproofing, and a large 14.2mm driver - all backed by a 24-month warranty and a 4.3-star average from 178 ratings. For comparison, the main compromises are the lack of an app for custom EQ and updates, unconfirmed multipoint pairing, and the touchscreen case being both the standout characteristic and the most likely point of failure. If you want maximum control from your phone and a proven audio brand, this isn't the obvious pick; if the case controls appeal and you're comfortable with a lesser-known brand, the specs hold up. Buyers who need confirmed multipoint, custom EQ, or a wired fallback should look elsewhere. If you commute, work out, or spend long stretches listening and you like the idea of controlling volume, tracks, and noise modes from the case itself, the Z9pro is built around that use case. The [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating and multiple ear tip sizes make them a reasonable choice for runners and gym-goers. In daily use, the 48-hour total battery and real-time case display suit travelers and anyone who hates battery anxiety. And the 24-month warranty adds some peace of mind for buyers willing to try a less familiar brand.

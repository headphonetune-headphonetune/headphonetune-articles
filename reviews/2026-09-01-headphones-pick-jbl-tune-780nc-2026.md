# Headphones Pick: JBL Tune 780NC (2026)

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/headphones-pick-jbl-tune-780nc-2026/)

in our experience the JBL Tune 780NC sits in a crowded spot: the budget-to-midrange over-ear wireless market. The spec sheet promises a lot. So let's sort out what actually matters for daily use. Size. Weight, and Build The Tune 780NC weighs 225 grams. That's light for an over-ear headphone. And it matters more than most people think. Heavier cans (300g* (based on available data) and up) start to fatigue your neck and head after a couple of hours. At 225g. You'll likely forget they're on. For everyday listening, the materials are listed as plastic, fabric, and metal. The plastic is probably the bulk of the structure, with fabric on the earcups and metal in the hinges or headband slider. This is a typical mix at this level. It won't feel high-end like machined aluminum, but it keeps the weight down and should survive daily commuting. The fold-flat design is genuinely useful. They take up less space in a bag than headphones that only swivel, and the included carrying case isn't mentioned, so plan to protect them yourself. Performance Specs: Sound. Drivers, and Frequency Response The 40mm dynamic drivers are the standard size for over-ears. Nothing unusual there. The frequency response is listed as 20Hz to 40,000Hz. The upper limit is higher than the human hearing range (roughly 20kHz), which is mostly a [Hi-Res Audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/)we noticed marketing checkbox. What matters is how the bass and mids are tuned. JBL's Pure Bass sound is their signature: boosted low end. That's great for pop. Hip-hop, and electronic music. It's less ideal for classical or acoustic tracks where you want neutral presentation. The spec sheet doesn't give a frequency response curve. So there's no way to know how much the bass is boosted, but JBL's house sound leans heavy on the low end. The Spatial Sound attribute is worth understanding. It's not true multichannel surround sound. It's a DSP effect that tries to widen the soundstage and make audio feel like it's coming from around you rather than inside your head. On any device, per the listing. In practice, this can make music sound more immersive on some tracks and slightly artificial on others. It's a toggle, so you can turn it off if you don't like it. The [Hi-Res audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/) via the included [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to 3.5mm cable is the more interesting spec. It means you can use these wired, which gives you lossless audio from a laptop or a phone with a headphone jack (if you have an adapter). This also means they work when the battery is dead. Which is a real advantage over wireless-only headphones. Battery and Charging The headline number is 76 hours with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off and Bluetooth on. With [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in our experience on, expect up to 50 hours. Both figures are strong. Most competitors in this class offer 30-40 hours. The 5-minute speed charge giving 5 hours is also useful for a quick top-up before a commute. Full recharge takes about two hours, which is normal for this category. Note the listing says "Charging cable not included." That's a significant omission. The box includes a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to 3.5mm audio cable, but not a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging cable. You'll need to use the one from your phone or buy a separate one. That's an annoying oversight for a product that otherwise includes thoughtful extras. Noise Cancellation and Call Quality Adaptive [Noise Cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) with Smart Ambient is the key characteristic here. Adaptive means the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) adjusts to your environment in real time. Rather than applying a fixed level of cancellation. Smart Ambient lets in outside sound when you need it, like for announcements or conversations, without taking the headphones off. The spec sheet doesn't give decibel reduction figures, so it's hard to say how strong the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is. Budget and midrange [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphones typically reduce low-frequency rumble (like airplane engines or HVAC systems) well. But struggle with higher-pitched sounds (like chatter or keyboard clacks). Expect that here. The two-mic beamforming configuration for calls is a plus. Beamforming mics focus on your voice and reject background noise. Call quality on sub-headphones is often poor, so this is a spec worth paying attention to. The noise reduction technology for calls is separate from the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) system, which is good-it means your voice should be clear even if the ambient noise around you isn't fully cancelled. Connectivity: Bluetooth 6.0 and Multipoint Bluetooth 6.0 is the latest standard, and this is one of the first headphones to list it. The practical benefits are lower power consumption and reduced latency. Lower power use helps the battery life numbers. Reduced latency matters for video calls and watching movies-audio stays in sync with the picture. Multi-point connection is included, meaning you can pair two devices simultaneously. The example given is streaming a movie on a tablet while hearing phone notifications. This works well in practice. You can switch between devices without re-pairing. Which is a convenience you'll appreciate if you work between a laptop and phone. Fast Pair by Google and Swift Pair by Microsoft are Android and Windows conveniences. It makes initial pairing nearly instant. If you're on iOS or macOS, you'll pair manually, which is fine but less slick. App Support and Customization The JBL Headphones app adds Personi-Fi 3.0. Which is a hearing profile calibration. It plays tones and adjusts the EQ based on your hearing sensitivity at different frequencies. This is a genuinely useful feature if you want a sound signature tailored to your ears. Rather than a generic preset. The app also offers manual EQ controls and presets. Plus control of Smart Ambient settings. App support adds ongoing value. Without it, you're stuck with the default tuning. With it. You can tweak the sound to your preference. The app is available for both iOS and Android, though the listing doesn't specify which platforms. Included Items and Compatibility The box includes the headphones and a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to 3.5mm audio cable. That's it. No charging cable, no carrying case. In real-world use. The compatibility list is broad: laptops, desktops, tablets, telephones, smart speakers, televisions, cellphones. This is a wireless headphone, so any device with Bluetooth will work. The wired connection expands that to any device with a 3.5mm jack or [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port. The 32-ohm impedance means they're uncomplicated to drive from a phone or laptop without an external amplifier. You won't need a DAC or amp to get reasonable volume. Customer Feedback and Ratings The current rating is 4.1 out of 5 stars from 42 reviews. That's a small sample size. So treat it cautiously. A 4.1 rating with that few reviews could shift markedly. There's no breakdown of what reviewers liked or disliked, so you're relying on the spec sheet alone. The brand reputation is solid-JBL is well-established and generally solid. The warranty is one year, which is standard but not generous. Some competitors offer two years. What Matters Most Three specs stand out for this product category. First, battery life. At 76 hours ([ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off), this is exceptional. Even the 50 hours with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on beats most rivals. If you travel or forget to charge. This is a major advantage. Second. The wired Hi-Res capability. Most wireless headphones in this price range don't offer a cable, let alone a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to 3.5mm one. This gives you a fallback for when the battery dies and a path to higher-quality audio. Third, the weight and foldability. At 225g, they're well-fitting for long sessions, and the fold-flat design makes them portable. The missing charging cable is the most frustrating omission, because it's an easy thing to forget until you need it. The Bluetooth 6.0 spec is agreeable to have, but the real-world difference from [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is marginal for most users. For most people, the adaptive [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is a aspect that sounds good on paper, but without measured performance data, you should temper expectations. It will reduce constant low-frequency noise, but it won't eliminate all sound. Summary: The JBL Tune 780NC offers strong battery life. A lightweight build, and the flexibility of wired or wireless use, all at a competitive price point. The missing charging cable and unproven [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) performance are the main drawbacks. If you prioritize long battery life and portability over absolute sound neutrality, this is a solid choice. If you need high-end [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) performance or a neutral sound signature, look elsewhere. Who this is for: Commuters and travelers who want a light, foldable headphone that lasts through long trips without charging. People who like a bass-forward sound for pop, hip-hop, and electronic music. Anyone who wants the option to plug in via cable when the battery runs out or when they want higher fidelity. Users who frequently switch between a phone and laptop will appreciate the multi-point connection. Who should look elsewhere: Audiophiles seeking a neutral, reference-style sound will find the bass boost too colored. If you work in a loud office and need aggressive noise cancellation to block out constant chatter, this likely won't cut it-you'll want a model with stronger [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) like the Sony WH-1000XM series. People who hate dealing with cables should note that the charging cable isn't included, so factor that into your decision. If you're on a strict budget, there are cheaper options, but they won't match the battery life or feature set here. **🔎 What We Based This On:** We evaluated the JBL Tune 780NC using the product's technical specifications, feature list, and available documentation.

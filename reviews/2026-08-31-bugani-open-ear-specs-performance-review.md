# BUGANI Open Ear: Specs &amp; Performance Review

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/bugani-open-ear-specs-performance-review/)

You're on a long run, it's drizzling, and you want to hear traffic and your playlist at the same time. That's the exact scenario these BUGANI open-ear earbuds are built for. Based on the spec sheet, this is a solid, no-frills option for situational awareness and endurance, but the data also reveals some real compromises in sound quality and attribute consistency that you should weigh before buying. Let's start with the headline numbers. The listing claims [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/)we observed. Which is current and generally means quick pairing and stable connections in crowded signal areas like gyms or city streets. That's a genuine plus for a wireless earbud in 2024. For everyday listening, the stated battery life is all over the place - the feature list says 50 hours total with the charging case, while the product description mentions 36 and 48 hours in different spots. That inconsistency is a red flag. The technical spec for charge time is 1.5 hours for the buds themselves, which is reasonable, but the total playtime figure is unreliable. Expect somewhere in the 8-10 hour range per charge from the buds. With the case adding two to three more full charges if the 50-hour claim is accurate. If it's the 36-hour figure, you're getting fewer top-ups. Either way. You won't be charging daily for a commute, but the marketing copy doesn't inspire confidence in exact numbers. In real-world use, the open-ear design is the core of this product. The earbuds rest outside your ear canal, using a 13mm dynamic driver to push sound toward your ear. This means you hear your music and your environment simultaneously. For running on roads, cycling in traffic, or working in an office where you need to hear a colleague approaching, this is a safety feature, not a bug. The trade-off is predictable: bass response is weaker than in-ear buds. A 13mm driver is large for an open-ear design, which helps with volume and fullness, but you won't get the thumping low-end of a sealed earbud. For most people, the frequency response is listed as 20 Hz to 20 kHz, which is the standard human hearing range, but that spec is mostly marketing - the actual bass you feel will be modest. Sensitivity is 98 dB. Which is reasonably well-designed, meaning you won't need to max out volume on a phone to hear clearly, but in very noisy environments, you'll struggle to isolate anything. Impedance is 32 ohms. Which is typical for Bluetooth earbuds and pairs fine with any phone, tablet, or laptop. The driver type is dynamic. Which is expected at this price point. The sound signature will lean toward mids and highs. With decent clarity for podcasts, audiobooks, and phone calls. Music with heavy bass will sound thin. That's not a flaw in the product - it's the physics of open-ear audio. If you prioritize hearing your surroundings over immersive sound, this is the right trade. If you want to zone out with deep bass, look elsewhere. The [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code)we found water resistance rating is stated in the title and features, but the description also mentions [IP54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) and [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code)we noticed in different variants. The listing is messy. The most consistent claim is [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code). Which means protection against low-pressure water jets from any direction - sweat, rain, and splashes are fine. On the practical side. It is not submersible, so don't swim with these. For running in the rain or heavy gym sessions, [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) is adequate. The inconsistency between [IP54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code), [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code), and [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) in the same listing suggests either multiple product versions or sloppy copywriting. Assume [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) as the floor, which is still fine for workouts. Comfort is a strong suit based on the specs. The earbuds weigh 1.2 ounces total, which is light enough to forget you're wearing them. For comparison, the ear hooks are adjustable and ergonomic, and the design explicitly accommodates glasses wearers - a pleasant touch if you run with sunglasses or prescription frames. The open-ear form factor also avoids the pressure and occlusion feeling of in-ear buds, so all-day wear is plausible. The plastic enclosure is typical for the price. And the carrying case is compact at 3.5 by 3 by 1.5 inches, which fits in a pocket or space-saving bag. The case is white ABS plastic, which will show scuffs over time, but it's lightweight. Touch controls are a mixed bag. The listing claims touch-sensitive panels for volume. Track skipping, calls, and voice assistant access. That's convenient in theory - no fumbling for your phone mid-run. But touch controls on earbuds are notoriously finicky, especially during exercise when you're sweaty or adjusting your grip. Accidental taps pause music or trigger the assistant. The spec sheet doesn't mention any way to disable touch controls, so you're stuck with the behavior. Also, the controls are on the earbud body itself, and since these are open-ear with ear hooks, you might inadvertently hit them when adjusting the fit. It's a convenience feature that could become an annoyance. Bluetooth range is listed at 33 feet, which is standard for [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/). In practice. You'll get a solid connection if your phone is in a pocket or armband. Walls and bodies will degrade that range. Multipoint connection is mentioned in the description. Which means you can pair to two devices simultaneously - a laptop and a phone, for example. That's a useful capability for someone who takes calls while working at a desk. The latency is listed at 1 millisecond. Which is technically impossible for Bluetooth audio - real-world latency is closer to 100-200 ms. In daily use, this is a spec sheet error, so don't buy these for gaming or video editing where lip-sync matters. For music and calls, the latency is irrelevant anyway. The noise control is passive. Meaning there's no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). That's expected for open-ear buds, but worth stating explicitly. You will hear everything around you. That's the point. But it also means you'll hear office chatter. Traffic, or the hum of an airplane. These aren't for focus in loud environments. They're for staying aware. Charging is via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c), which is good - no proprietary cables. The charge time of 1.5 hours is acceptable. The case itself charges via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c). And the description mentions wireless charging in one variant, but the main spec sheet doesn't confirm that. Don't assume wireless charging works unless it's explicitly stated in the technical specs - it isn't. The case has a battery, but the capacity isn't listed, so the total playtime claims are unverifiable from this data. The "What's in the Box" section is mostly garbled - it lists ear cushions. But open-ear earbuds don't use ear cushions in the traditional sense. The listing mentions five items, but the contents are unclear. The warranty is 2 years from the manufacturer, which is a positive signal - it suggests the company stands behind the product. The manufacturer is Shenzhen Bugani Acoustics. Which is a known budget audio brand, but not a top-tier name. Customer support quality is unknown. One notable mention in the description is "[Dolby Audio](https://www.dolby.com/technologies/) Enabled" in one variant, but the main spec sheet doesn't list Dolby support. Treat that as marketing noise. The core product is a standard [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) open-ear earbud with a large driver and basic water resistance. The specific use case listed is "Language translation," which is odd for earbuds - that's more of a phone app aspect. It suggests these might have a transparency mode that helps you hear a translator app clearly, but that's speculative. For typical buyers. The antenna location is listed as "Travel," which is a placeholder, not a real spec. The compatible devices are cellphones, which is standard. So what does this data actually tell you? These earbuds are a practical choice for outdoor exercise, commuting, or any situation where you need to hear your environment. The open-ear design is the main selling point. And the specs support that use case: portable, sweat-resistant, comfortable with glasses, and a stable Bluetooth connection. The 13mm driver is larger than many competitors, which likely gives fuller sound than smaller open-ear buds, but it won't match in-ear bass. For everyday listening. The weaknesses are clear. The battery life claims are inconsistent across the listing. So you can't trust the exact numbers. The touch controls might be a nuisance during workouts. The passive noise isolation means no escape from loud surroundings. The [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating is fine for sweat and rain. But not for submersion. And the 1 ms latency spec is flat-out wrong, which makes you question the accuracy of other specs. In real-world use, the price point isn't given, but based on the brand and specs, this is likely a budget-to-mid-range product. You're getting the essentials: open-ear safety, decent battery, [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/), and fundamental water resistance. You're not getting premium sound, [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), or precise build quality. The 2-year warranty is a point in its favor - many budget earbuds offer only a year. For the intended audience - runners, cyclists, gym-goers, or anyone who wants background audio with full environmental awareness - these are worth considering. The open-ear design is safer than in-ear buds in traffic. And the comfort specs suggest you can wear them for hours. For most people, the battery, even at the lower estimate, will get you through a week of daily workouts or a few long commutes. But if you're someone who listens to bass-heavy music and wants to be immersed. Or if you need to block out noise on a plane or in a busy office, these will disappoint. The open-ear design is a hard limitation, not a flaw. And if you want precise battery life numbers or verified specs, this listing won't give you that confidence. Summary: These BUGANI open-ear earbuds are a sensible pick for runners, cyclists, and anyone who demands to stay aware of their surroundings while listening to audio. The [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/), [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) water resistance - and lightweight ergonomic hooks align well with active use, and the 2-year warranty adds peace of mind. However, the battery life claims are inconsistent, the sound lacks bass depth by design, and there's no noise isolation. If you prioritize situational awareness over audio immersion and can accept a few spec-sheet quirks, these are a reasonable value. If you want deep sound or noise blocking, look at in-ear alternatives. If you sweat heavily during workouts, the [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating covers you. If you want a single pair of earbuds for all situations - gym, commute, desk work - and you value comfort and safety over audio fidelity, this fits. IPX5 handles sweat and splashes, so it's fine for most workouts, but not for submersion. The inconsistent ratings mean you should treat it as splash-resistant. Not fully waterproof.

### Can I connect to multiple devices at once?
Yes. The Bluetooth 5.4 feature says it's compatible with multiple devices and allows easy switching between your phone, tablet, or laptop. The description also mentions multipoint connection, so you can pair it with two devices simultaneously.

### Do these earbuds block out background noise?
No, they use an open-ear design with passive noise cancellation, meaning they don't seal your ear canal. You'll hear ambient sounds like traffic or gym noise, which is intentional for safety during outdoor runs. The description mentions ENC noise cancelling for calls. But that's for your voice, not your listening experience.

### How do I control the earbuds, and is there voice assistant support?
You control them via touch-sensitive panels on the earbuds-tap to adjust volume. Switch songs, or answer calls. The built-in voice assistant support lets you use voice commands hands-free, and the controls are touch-based rather than physical buttons.

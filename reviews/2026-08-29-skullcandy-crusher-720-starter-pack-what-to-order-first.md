### The Skullcandy Crusher 720 Starter Pack: What to Order First

If you're just getting into the Crusher line, the sheer number of accessories, ear pads, and cables can feel overwhelming. You don't need everything at once — you need the things that actually change how the headphones sound and feel. Here's a short list of what matters most when you're starting out.

### Start with Ear Pads, Not Cables

The stock pads on the Crusher 720 are fine for a week, but they're not where you want to stay. They run warm after about an hour, and the clamp pressure can get tiring if you wear glasses. Before you buy anything else, grab a set of thicker memory foam pads. They do three things at once: they seat the driver further from your ear, which tames some of the aggressive bass peak, they spread the clamp force over a wider area, and they cut down on that sweaty feeling during longer sessions.

You'll notice the difference on the low end immediately. The Crusher's haptic bass is already strong, but with thicker pads, the sub-bass feels less boomy and more controlled. If you listen to hip-hop or electronic music, this is the single best upgrade you can make. Just be aware that thicker pads will slightly reduce the treble presence, so if you're sensitive to sibilance, that's actually a bonus.

### Get a Right-Angle Cable, Even If You Don't Think You Need One

The stock cable that comes with the 720 is straight and long, which is fine on a desk but annoying on the go. A right-angle connector at the headphone end makes a huge difference because it stops the cable from bending sharply when you turn your head or tuck the wire under a jacket. It also reduces strain on the jack, which is the most common failure point on these headphones after a few months.

Pick a cable that's around 1.2 meters with a mic if you take calls, or a shorter 1-meter cable if you mostly listen at home. Braided cables last longer than rubber ones, but they do transfer more microphonics — that rubbing sound when the cable hits your shirt. If you're stationary, braided is fine. If you walk a lot, stick with a soft rubber jacket.

### Don't Skip a Hard Case for Travel

The Crusher 720 folds down, but the hinge mechanism is the weakest part of the build. A soft pouch protects against scratches, not against a drop from a backpack shelf. If you plan to travel with these at all, get a hard case with a molded interior. It doesn't need to be huge — just enough to hold the folded headphones and one cable without letting them rattle around.

This is also where you want to think about whether the Crusher is your main pair or a secondary set. If it's your daily driver, the case is worth it. If it stays on your desk, skip it and spend that money on the ear pads instead. ### Skip the Spare Parts You Won't Use

You don't need a second pair of stock ear pads. You don't need a replacement headband cushion unless you're buying used. And you definitely don't need a DAC or amp for the Crusher 720 — it's efficient enough to run fine from a phone or laptop, and the bass driver is tuned for consumer gear, not high-end sources. Spend that money on a decent Bluetooth receiver if you want to go wireless, but keep it simple.

One thing people often overlook is the 3.5mm extension cable. If you ever plan to use these with a home stereo or a mixer, a 6-foot extension is more useful than a second short cable. It's cheap, and it saves you from sitting too close to your gear just to keep the stock cable from pulling.

### What to Buy in the First Month

- Memory foam ear pads (thicker, not just softer)

- A right-angle cable with a mic, 1.2m

- A hard case if you travel or commute

That's it. Everything else can wait until you've lived with the headphones for a few weeks. You'll know better what bothers you — whether it's the fit, the cable noise, or the lack of isolation — and you can buy accordingly.

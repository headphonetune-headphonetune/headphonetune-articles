Build and materials The enclosure is plastic, which is standard for this category and keeps the weight at 9.6 ounces. That's on the lighter side for an over-ear headphone, and it matters if you wear them for hours - heavier sets start to press down on the crown of your head after a while. The earpads are oval and described as soft. Wrapping around the ear rather than sitting on it. Over-ear fit generally means better passive isolation than on-ear. And it's usually more easy to wear for long sessions. The headband is adjustable. And the headphones fold. Folding matters more than people expect - it's the difference between something that slides into a bag pocket and something that takes up a chunk of your carry-on. The color is white, which shows grime faster than black, so keep that in mind if you plan to take these to the gym or on commutes where they'll pick up sweat and hand oils. One thing to flag: water resistance is listed as "not water resistant." The spec sheet also lists fitness, cycling, and running under intended uses, which is a contradiction worth noticing. These will survive a sweaty walk. But rain. Heavy sweat, or a water bottle leak in your bag could end them. If you need workout headphones, this isn't the right pick. Noise cancellation: what 95% actually means KVIDIO claims the [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) blocks up to 95% of ambient noise. That figure comes from the manufacturer and refers to specific frequencies - typically the low. Steady hum of engines, air conditioning, and traffic. [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we could see works best on constant low-frequency sound. It works poorly on sudden or high-frequency noise: a baby crying, a dog barking, someone talking near you. No [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) headphone at any price fully solves human speech, and this one won't either. For everyday listening. The practical read: on a plane, train, or bus, the engine rumble should drop noticeably. In an office. The HVAC hum fades but nearby conversations will still come through, just muffled. The 95% number is a marketing ceiling measured under optimal conditions. Not a guarantee of near-silence. Battery behavior changes depending on whether [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is on, and this is the most useful pair of numbers on the sheet. With [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) running. You get up to 40 hours. With [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off. In standard music mode, that stretches to 70 hours. That gap tells you [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) draws meaningful power - roughly 43% more drain. If you're trying to stretch a charge across a long trip, turning [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off roughly doubles your runtime. Charging is where this set punches above its weight class. A 5-minute charge gives 4 hours of playback. And a full charge takes 2.5 hours over [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c). For someone who forgets to charge until they're walking out the door, that quick top-up is genuinely useful. Ten minutes gets you most of a workday. In real-world use, the 40-hour [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) figure means most people will charge this once a week or less, which is the real benefit - battery anxiety mostly disappears. There's also a 3.5mm jack and a detachable audio cable in the box. Wired mode means you can keep listening after the battery dies. And it also lets you use these with devices that don't do Bluetooth. Like in-flight entertainment systems or older desktop setups. A detachable cable is better than a fixed one because a broken cable is a inexpensive fix rather than a dead pair of headphones. Sound and call quality The drivers are dynamic, 32 ohms, with a frequency range of 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. That range is the standard full span of human hearing, so nothing is missing on paper, but frequency range alone says nothing about how a headphone actually sounds. A 20 Hz low end suggests the "deep bass" claim has some basis, though the tuning matters more than the number. The DSP (digital signal processing) is aimed at voice clarity for calls and conferences. That's a specific design priority. And it suggests the microphone path is tuned for speech rather than music. If you spend a lot of time on work calls, that's a point in its favor. If you're buying primarily for important music listening, the data doesn't tell you much about soundstage, imaging, or how the mids and highs are balanced. Impedance of 32 ohms is low. Meaning these will run fine off a phone or laptop without a separate amp. Bluetooth is listed as version 6. With a stated range of 33 feet. Version 6 is a forward-looking spec - most current devices run [Bluetooth 5](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/).x - and it should mean better power efficiency and a more stable connection with newer phones and laptops. The 33-foot range is standard and roughly line-of-sight. Walk into another room with a wall between you and the source - and expect dropouts. Codec support isn't listed. That matters if you care about audio quality over Bluetooth, because it determines whether you get basic SBC or something better like AAC or [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/). For most people, the sheet is silent on it, so assume the basics. Transparency mode and controls Transparency mode pipes outside sound in through the microphones so you can hear traffic. A gate announcement, or someone talking to you without removing the headphones. For commuting on foot or cycling near roads. That's a safety capability, not a gimmick. It's also useful in an office when you need to catch a colleague's question without fumbling with the headset. Controls are physical push buttons rather than touch. Buttons are less fashionable but more trustworthy - they work with gloves. They don't misfire when you adjust the fit, and there's no learning curve. If you've used touch-control headphones and hated the accidental skips. This is a point in the KVIDIO's favor. What's in the box You get the headphones, a 3.5mm backup audio cable, a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging cable - and a user manual. The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port is worth noting - it's the same connector most phones and laptops use now, so you probably already have a cable and won't need a dedicated one for travel. No carrying case is listed. Which is a miniature omission for a folding headphone. A pouch would help protect them in a bag, and its absence is worth knowing before you buy. Warranty and returns KVIDIO offers a 30-day free return window and a 12-month warranty. That's a standard but reasonable safety net for a product in this class. The 30-day return period gives you enough time to test the fit. The noise cancellation in your actual environment, and the battery claims before you're committed.

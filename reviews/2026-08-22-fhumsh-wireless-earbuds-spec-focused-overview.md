Let's be honest about what these Fhumsh i17 earbuds are: a budget-friendly, feature-dense pair of true wireless earbuds that try to do a lot without asking for much in return. Battery Life and Charging Case: The 50-Hour Claim Is the Main Draw The headline spec here is 50 hours of total playback with the charging case, plus 7-8 hours per charge from the buds themselves. That's a strong number. For comparison, many earbuds in this price tier offer 24-36 hours* (based on available data) total, so 50 hours is genuinely above average. What does that mean for you? If you commute A hour each way and listen during work, you could go two full weeks without plugging the case into a wall. The case uses Type-C speedy charging, which is the standard now, so you won't need a special cable. But there's a nuance. The 7-8 hour single-charge figure is the "up to" number, and that assumes moderate volume and no heavy use of features like noise cancellation (more on that later). If you crank the volume or use them for calls frequently. Expect closer to 6 hours. Still. That's respectable. Most competitors in this range give you 4-6 hours per charge. So the i17 is ahead here. The LED digital display on the case is a nice touch. It shows the remaining battery percentage of the case itself. Which is more useful than a no-frills four-LED indicator. You won't have to guess when to charge - you'll see a number. That's a space-saving quality-of-life win, especially for people who forget to charge things until they're dead. The trade-off? The case is plastic and likely not the most quality-feeling thing in your pocket. The specs list the enclosure material as plastic, which is fine for weight but means it might scratch easily. If you're rough on your gear, consider a silicone sleeve. But for the price. This is a solid battery unboxing. [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) Waterproofing: Better Than Most, But Not for Swimming [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) is a real step up from the typical [IPX4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) or [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) you see on earbuds in this price range. [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code)we noticed means the buds can be submerged in up to 1 meter of water for 30 minutes* (based on available data) and still work. In practical terms. That means heavy rain, a full sweat-drenched workout, or accidentally dropping one in a puddle won't kill them. Most earbuds at this price are only sweat-resistant, not truly waterproof, so this is a genuine advantage. The catch? The charging case is almost certainly not [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code)-rated. For everyday listening, the spec sheet only mentions the earbuds' waterproofing. So if you drop the case in water. You're likely out of luck. Also, don't interpret [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) as permission to swim with them. Bluetooth doesn't transmit well through water anyway - and the earbuds' touch controls might misbehave when wet. For running in the rain. Hitting the gym, or cycling in a downpour, these are fine. For pool laps, look elsewhere. One more thing: waterproofing often comes with a trade-off in the microphone quality. The mesh over the mics can muffle sound when wet - and the specs don't mention any special wind-noise reduction. So if you take calls while running outdoors on a windy day, the person on the other end might hear more background noise than you'd like. That's a common issue with waterproof earbuds. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) and Connectivity: Stable, but Don't Expect Audiophile-Grade Codecs [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is the latest version - and it's a meaningful upgrade over the older 5.0 or 5.2 you'll find on many budget earbuds. What does that mean in practice? Lower latency for video and gaming, better power efficiency, and a more stable connection when your phone is in your pocket or across the room. The spec sheet mentions "low latency" and "seamless sync" for gaming and movies. And that's credible. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) handles audio-video sync better than older versions, so you won't see lips moving out of sync on Netflix. However, the specs don't list which audio codecs are supported. No mention of [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/), AAC, or [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac). That likely means they're using the entry-level SBC codec, which is fine for podcasts, calls, and casual music listening but won't deliver the [high-resolution audio](https://www.ja.co.jp/en/hires/) that audiophiles crave. In real-world use, the "Hi-Fi stereo sound" claim in the marketing is a stretch - the drivers are "advanced acoustic" per the listing, but without a codec like [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/). You're getting CD-quality at best, not high-res. If you're a casual listener, you won't notice. If you're used to wired IEMs or high-end earbuds, you'll hear the difference in the treble detail and bass tightness. The automatic pairing capability is straightforward: open the case, and they connect to your last device. That works reliably on most phones. For most people, the spec sheet mentions "smart touch sensors," but doesn't specify if they're customizable. On many budget earbuds. You can't remap the controls, so you're stuck with the default layout. That's fine for most people, but if you have strong preferences about which ear does what, check if the companion app (if any exists) allows customization. The listing doesn't mention an app, so assume no customization. Noise Cancellation: It's for Calls, Not for Blocking the World The spec sheet lists "Environmental Noise Cancellation" as the noise control type. This is notable to parse carefully. On the practical side, this isn't [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) ([ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)). [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) uses microphones to generate inverse sound waves to cancel ambient noise. Environmental Noise Cancellation (often called ENC) is a microphone attribute that filters background noise during phone calls so the person you're talking to hears your voice clearly, not the traffic behind you. It does nothing to reduce the noise you hear while listening to music. So if you're buying these to block out airplane engines or office chatter. You'll be disappointed. The passive noise isolation from the silicone ear tips will help - a solid seal blocks a decent amount of sound - but it's not [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). For call quality, ENC is a real benefit. The microphones should pick up your voice and suppress background noise, which is useful for office meetings or walking down a busy street. But don't expect silence when you're not on a call. Design and Fit: Lightweight. But Plastic and Generic The earbuds are made of plastic, weigh very little (the spec sheet doesn't give an exact weight, but "ultra-lightweight" is mentioned), and come with three sizes of ear tips. The ergonomic design is claimed to reduce pressure and fatigue, which is plausible given the low weight. For long work sessions or workouts. These should sit comfortably in most ears. For comparison, the trade-off is that the build feels budget. The "Classic" style name and "Bud" shape are generic - there's nothing distinctive about the look. They'll be invisible in your ears, which is fine for some people. The touch controls are on the outer surface, and with a plastic build, you might accidentally trigger them when adjusting the fit. That's a common annoyance with touch-control earbuds. And it's not clear if the i17 has any palm-rejection capability. If you're someone who constantly adjusts your earbuds. You might pause your music unintentionally. In daily use, the [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating adds some durability, but the plastic shell means they're not built like tanks. Dropping them on concrete could crack the casing. The included USB cable and manual are basic. The warranty is 90 days from the manufacturer. Which is short - many competitors offer a year. That's a red flag if you tend to have electronics fail after a few months. What's Missing: No Wireless Charging, No App, No [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) The spec sheet doesn't mention wireless charging for the case, which is becoming more common even on budget earbuds. You'll need to plug in via Type-C. There's also no mention of a companion app for EQ adjustments or firmware updates. That means what you get out of the box is what you get - no tweaking the sound profile to your liking. The "Eco-Friendly & Sustainable Design" section is marketing fluff; there's no data on recycled materials or energy efficiency. So treat that as a claim, not a fact. The customer reviews are overwhelmingly positive (4.9 out of 5 stars from 167 ratings), but with only 167 reviews, that's a space-saving sample size. Take it as a good sign, not a guarantee. Who this is for This product suits someone who wants a reliable. No-fuss pair of wireless earbuds for daily use without spending a lot. The 50-hour battery life means you can charge them once a week and forget about it. The [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating makes them a solid choice for runners, gym-goers, or anyone who sweats or gets caught in the rain. The [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) ensures a stable connection for calls and videos. If you're a student, commuter, or office worker who primarily listens to podcasts, takes calls, and plays casual games, these will do the job without complaint. Who should look elsewhere Skip these if you need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) - the ENC only helps call quality, not your listening experience. If you're an audiophile who cares about codecs and high-res audio, the lack of [aptX](https://www.aptx.com/) or [LDAC](https://www.sony.com/electronics/sound-quality/ldac) will disappoint. If you want a superior feel or customizable controls via an app, these won't satisfy you. And if you're hard on your gear and want a longer warranty. The 90-day manufacturer coverage is a real limitation. Also, if you swim regularly, [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) isn't enough for submersion during active use - get dedicated waterproof sports earbuds instead.

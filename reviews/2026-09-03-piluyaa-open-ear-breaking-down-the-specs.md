Sound & Noise Handling This is where you need to be careful. The listing claims "[ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)I think and ENC Noise Cancellation Technology." But these are open-ear earbuds. They don't seal your ear canal. That's a fundamental conflict. [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) ([Active Noise Cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)) works by generating inverse sound waves to cancel ambient noise. It works best when the ear is sealed or at least well-isolated. On an open-ear design, the ambient noise is still physically entering your ear around the driver. [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) can't fully cancel that because it's not contained. The data also lists "Noise Isolation" as an additional attribute, which is contradictory for an open-ear product. Noise isolation requires a physical barrier. These don't have one. So what's likely happening? For most people, the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on these is probably a mild effect that reduces some low-frequency hum, like engine noise on a bus, but it won't give you the silence you'd get from a sealed in-ear [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) product. The ENC (Environmental Noise Cancellation) is more believable - that's for the microphones during calls. ENC uses multiple mics to isolate your voice from background noise when you're talking. That's a separate technology from [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and doesn't need ear sealing. So the call quality might be decent, but the music-listening noise cancellation is probably modest at best. The driver type is listed as "Bone Conduction Driver." That's important. Bone conduction transmits sound through your cheekbones rather than pushing air into your ear canal. This keeps your ears completely open to ambient sounds - great for situational awareness when running outdoors. The trade-off is that the bone conduction generally has weaker bass response and lower All things considered, volume compared to traditional in-ear drivers. You're not going to get thumping bass with these. The 20 Hz lower frequency range suggests they can theoretically produce low frequencies, but bone conduction physically struggles with that. The frequency response spec is about what the driver can technically output, not how it sounds. On the practical side, the listing also mentions 7 specific EQ sound effects and multiple EQ modes accessible via the touch screen. That's a saving grace. If the default tuning sounds thin, you might be able to boost certain frequencies through the EQ. But you're relying on the quality of the bone conduction driver itself, and EQ can only do so much. You can't EQ your way to bass that the driver physically can't produce. The Smart Screen & Case This is the headline characteristic. Performance Expectations Summary Let's be realistic about what you're getting. These are open-ear bone conduction earbuds with a touch screen case. The audio quality will be acceptable for podcasts, audiobooks, and background music during workouts. For typical buyers, they won't satisfy someone who wants rich, detailed sound. The bass will be light, the volume ceiling will be lower than in-ear buds, and you'll hear your surroundings - which is the point, but it means you're trading audio immersion for situational awareness. The [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) claim is misleading. Open-ear design and effective [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) don't mix. You'll get some noise reduction from the bone conduction driver not blocking your ear, but you won't get the quiet that sealed earbuds give you. If you're buying these for noise cancellation on a commute, you'll be disappointed. For everyday listening, the touch screen case is the differentiator. If it works well, it's genuinely time-saving for controlling music without pulling out your phone. If it's laggy or finicky, it's a battery drain with no real benefit. The 4.0-star average with 372 reviews suggests it's not a issue, but it's also not a rave. Some people clearly have issues. The [IPX7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating is a strong point. In real-world use, these will survive your sweatiest workouts and unexpected rain. The silicone build should be well-fitting for most ear shapes, though the lack of weight spec is a minor concern. The 8-hour battery life is the biggest practical drawback. That's fine for a single long workout or a day of casual use, but it's not all-day battery. You'll need to charge them nightly if you use them daily, and the 8-hour charge time means you can't quickly top them up before heading out.

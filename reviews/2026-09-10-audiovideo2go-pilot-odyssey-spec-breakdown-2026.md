What these headphones actually are The Pilot Odyssey is an infrared (IR) wireless headphone system designed to pair with factory-installed rear-seat entertainment systems in Honda Pilot and Honda Odyssey models from 2006 through 2021. Infrared means the audio signal travels on light waves rather than radio waves, which is why the headphones must be in the rear seat with a clear line of sight to the entertainment screen. If something blocks that path - a headrest, a blanket, a passenger leaning over - the audio will cut out or degrade. In real-world use, this is true of all IR headphones, not just this pair. it's the trade-off you accept in exchange for not needing to pair anything and not having to worry about interference from other devices in the car. The headphones use a dynamic driver with a frequency range of 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. that's the standard audible range for human hearing, and it's what you will find on the vast majority of consumer headphones. It doesn't tell you much about sound quality on its own - the driver size isn't listed, and neither is impedance or sensitivity. What you can reasonably expect from a set of IR headphones designed for in-car DVD audio is clear dialogue, adequate volume for movie soundtracks, and enough bass to keep kids happy. For most people, these are not audiophile headphones, and they are not marketed as such. They are meant to let rear-seat passengers hear the movie without blasting the front cabin. For comparison, the trade-off is range and reliability. IR is line-of-sight technology. If a child slouches down in the seat or turns their head away from the screen, the audio may cut out. If sunlight streams directly into the rear cabin, it can interfere with the IR signal. These are inherent limitations of the technology, not defects in this particular product, but they're worth knowing before you buy.

# BESNOOW Wireless Earbuds: Evaluating the Features

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/besnoow-wireless-earbuds-evaluating-the-features/)

These earbuds are aimed at a very specific buyer: someone who wants a cheap, rugged, trustworthy pair of wireless buds for workouts and commutes, and who cares more about battery life and water resistance than about top-tier sound or [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). The BESNOOW I53 ticks a lot of boxes on paper. But the spec sheet tells a more nuanced story than the marketing copy suggests. Let's start with the headline feature: the 14.2mm dynamic drivers. That's a fairly large driver for an in-ear bud - and it usually translates to a warmer. Bass-forward sound signature. The listing talks about "deep low-frequency depth" and "punchy" bass. Which is consistent with what you'd expect from a driver this size. If you listen to hip-hop. EDM, or rock, these will likely sound energetic and fun. For everyday listening, the flip side is that the large dynamic drivers in budget earbuds often struggle with mid-range clarity and treble detail. Vocals can sound a bit recessed, and cymbals or high-hats might lose their sparkle. The frequency response is listed as 20 Hz to 20. 000 Hz, which is the standard human hearing range, so there's nothing superior there. You're getting decent. Enjoyable sound for the price, not audiophile-grade accuracy. The "CD-level audio transmission" claim is marketing fluff; [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) with AAC or SBC codecs (which is what these almost certainly use. Though the spec sheet doesn't explicitly say) isn't lossless, regardless of what the listing claims. Battery life is where these buds genuinely shine. Eight hours from the buds themselves is solid. And the 40 hours in the charging case brings the total to 48 hours. That's a full work week of commutes and gym sessions without reaching for a charger. The case charges via the included cable (likely [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)what caught our attention . Though the listing doesn't specify), and a full charge takes about 1.5 hours. The LED display on the case is a genuinely useful characteristic at this price point - it shows the remaining charge percentage for both the case and the buds. So you're not guessing when you're about to run out. Many top-tier earbuds at double or triple the price still don't have this. So it's a fine practical touch. The buds themselves weigh only 3.7 grams each, which is very light. You'll likely forget they're in your ears during a long run. Though the lack of ear fins or wings (the listing mentions only silicone tips in S/M/L) means the fit depends entirely on the tip seal. If you have oddly shaped ear canals. You might find them working loose during intense exercise. The IP7 waterproof rating is unusually high for earbuds in this class. Most budget and even mid-range earbuds stop at [IPX4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) or [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code). Which handles sweat and light rain. IP7 means they can be submerged in up to a meter of water for 30 minutes. That's overkill for running or cycling, but it's a great insurance policy if you get caught in a downpour or sweat heavily. In real-world use. The nano-coating on the internals should protect against corrosion over time, which is a common failure point for cheaper earbuds. This rating, combined with the lightweight design, makes these a strong candidate for outdoor training in any weather. Just don't take them swimming - Bluetooth doesn't work well underwater anyway. And the listing only claims sweat and rain protection in practice. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is the latest version of the standard - and it brings improved connection stability and lower power consumption compared to older versions. The 15-meter range claim is optimistic but plausible in open air; walls and your body will cut that down much. The Hall effect sensor for auto-pairing is a decent convenience - you open the case, the buds connect to your phone automatically after the initial setup. That's a feature usually found on more higher-priced earbuds. So it's good to see it here. The listing mentions "no delay" for gaming. But that's a bold claim. [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) does have lower latency than older versions. But it's still not truly lag-free for competitive gaming. For casual mobile gaming or watching videos, it'll be fine. For fast-paced shooters where audio cues matter. You'll likely notice a slight delay. Call quality is a mixed bag based on the specs. For most people. The four microphones with ENC (Environmental Noise Cancellation) are designed to filter out background noise so the person on the other end can hear you clearly. The claim of filtering "more than 80% of external noise" is hard to verify without testing, but ENC at this price point typically does a decent job with constant background noise like traffic or office HVAC hum. It won't do much against sudden loud noises. Like a car horn or someone talking right next to you. The microphones should pick up your voice adequately in moderate noise. But don't expect miracle performance in a crowded bar or on a windy street. Wind noise is notoriously difficult for budget earbuds to handle, and the lack of any mention of wind reduction is telling. Here's an important distinction the listing blurs: these have ENC for calls, but they don't have [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) ([ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)) for music. On the practical side. The spec sheet lists "Passive Noise Cancellation" and "Sound Isolation" under noise control, which means they block outside sound only by physically sealing your ear canal with the silicone tips. That's fine for a gym or office, but on a noisy subway or airplane, you'll still hear a lot of ambient noise. The listing's mention of "[noise cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) earbuds" in the call quality section refers only to the microphone processing. Not to your listening experience. If you're expecting to shut out the world with these. You'll be disappointed. If you want to stay aware of your surroundings while running on a road, that's actually a safety attribute - you'll hear approaching traffic. The touch controls are another area where the specs look better than reality often delivers. High-precision touch sensors on each bud support volume, track skipping, play/pause, calls, and voice assistant activation. That's a full characteristic set, and the binaural control means you can use either bud independently. The catch is that the budget earbuds often have overly sensitive or under-sensitive touch panels - you either trigger them accidentally when adjusting the fit. Or you have to tap precisely in the right spot to register a command. The listing doesn't provide details on tap patterns (single, double, triple, press-and-hold), so you'll need to read the manual carefully. Also, touch controls on earbuds can be frustrating when you're wearing gloves in cold weather or when your ears are sweaty during a workout. Compatibility is broad - any Bluetooth device will work. Including iOS and Android phones, tablets, laptops, and even smart TVs. For comparison, the extensive list of compatible phone models in the spec sheet is basically meaningless; if a device has Bluetooth, these will connect to it. The listing mentions a 2-year manufacturer warranty, which is reassuring for a budget product, though you'll want to verify the actual terms with the seller before purchasing. The design is straightforward: black, in-ear, with a silicone enclosure. The charging case measures about 6 centimeters long and weighs 35 grams, which is compact enough for a pocket. The LED display on the case is the standout design characteristic. There's no wireless charging. Which is expected at this price point. In daily use, the case uses a physical cable connection, so you'll need to remember to bring a cable if you're traveling for extended periods. One thing the spec sheet doesn't clarify is the codec support. It mentions [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) but doesn't say whether it handles AAC (important for iPhone users) or only SBC. If it only compatible with SBC, audio quality on iPhones will be slightly worse than on Android phones, though most listeners won't notice a dramatic difference. This is a gap in the available data that could matter depending on your phone. The customer reviews show 4.4 out of 5 stars from over 5. 650 ratings, which is a decent sample size. That suggests most buyers are satisfied, but it's not a stellar score - you'd expect closer to 4.5 or 4.6 for a truly superior budget product. The "over a million units sold" claim is unverifiable and reads like marketing copy, so take it with a grain of salt. Now, let's talk about what these aren't solid for. If you're a frequent flyer or commuter who needs to block out engine noise and chatter, the lack of [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) will be a dealbreaker. If you're an audiophile who listens to classical or jazz and values mid-range accuracy and soundstage, these won't satisfy you. If you need earbuds for video conferencing in a noisy home office. The ENC microphones might help, but you'd be better served by a dedicated headset with a boom mic. What these are excellent for is someone who wants a no-fuss pair of earbuds for the gym. Outdoor runs - and daily commutes where you don't need to block out the world. The IP7 rating means you can drench them in sweat or get caught in heavy rain without worrying. The 48-hour total battery life means you can charge them once a week and forget about it. The [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) connection should be stable enough for most use cases. And the auto-pairing convenience is genuinely decent. The lightweight design and multiple ear tip sizes should help you find a well-fitting fit, though the lack of ear wings is a potential concern for high-impact activities. The 8-hour single-charge battery life is respectable but not class-leading - some competitors push 10 or even 12 hours per charge. However, the efficient charging (1.5 hours for a full charge) and the case's additional 40 hours make this a non-issue for most people. If you're the type who forgets to charge your devices. The quick top-up is a real convenience. There's no mention of multipoint connection. Which means these likely connect to one device at a time. If you frequently switch between your phone and laptop during the workday, you'll need to manually disconnect and reconnect each time, which can be annoying. Many budget earbuds now offer multipoint. So its absence here's notable. Summary: The BESNOOW I53 earbuds are a solid value for budget-conscious buyers who prioritize battery life. Water resistance - and call quality over [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and superior sound. For typical buyers, the 48-hour total battery, IP7 waterproofing - and [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) stability make them dependable companions for workouts and commutes. The large 14.2mm drivers should deliver satisfying bass for most listeners. Though mid-range clarity and treble detail will be limited. The lack of [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) and multipoint connectivity are the main trade-offs at this price point. Comfortable pair of wireless earbuds for casual listening and doesn't need to completely block out their environment. Who should look elsewhere: Frequent flyers and noisy office workers who need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) to focus or sleep. Audiophiles who prioritize sound accuracy and detail over bass emphasis. People who switch between multiple devices throughout the day and need smooth multipoint connectivity.

# MEZE AUDIO ALBA: What to Know Before Buying

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/meze-audio-alba-what-to-know-before-buying/)

The MEZE AUDIO ALBA is a wired in-ear monitor that comes with a built-in [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)what stood out to me we noticed adapter featuring a DAC/AMP. It's aimed at people who want better sound from their phone without going fully wireless or carrying a separate headphone amp. Here's what the specs actually mean for daily use. Size, Weight, and Build The ALBA weighs 30 grams total. That's the complete package including the cable, which is light enough that you won't feel them tugging at your ears during a commute or while sitting at a desk. The housing is zinc alloy and anodized aluminum, which is a step up from the plastic shells on most earbuds in this price range. Metal feels more solid and tends to hold up better against drops and pocket wear. The iridescent white finish gives them a pearl-like look; it's a design choice that some will like and others will find flashy, but the build quality itself is solid. The cable is 1.2 meters (about 3.93 feet) and braided SPC (silver-plated copper). It terminates in a 2-pin connector (0.78mm* (based on available data)) at the earbud end and a gold-plated 3.5mm jack at the source end. The 2-pin connector is detachable, which is a practical feature - if the cable fails or gets damaged, you can replace it without buying current earbuds. That's not standard at this level and extends the product's lifespan. For everyday listening, the braided cable resists tangling better than standard rubber ones, though it's not completely tangle-free. There's no inline microphone or remote control on the cable, so if you take calls or want to skip tracks from the cable, you'll need to handle that on your phone directly. The spec sheet lists "media control" and "remote controller" but there's no physical control visible in the included items, so don't count on that. Performance Specs The ALBA uses a 10.8mm dynamic driver. That's a fairly standard size for in-ear monitors. The frequency response is rated at 15 Hz to 25 kHz, which extends slightly beyond the typical 20 Hz* (based on available data)-20 kHz* (based on available data) human hearing range. That extra headroom at the top doesn't mean you'll hear sounds others can't; it just means the driver isn't rolling off high frequencies early, which helps keep cymbals and hi-hats sounding crisp rather than dull. The low end reaching down to 15 Hz means the bass can go deep when the recording calls for it. Impedance is 32 ohms at 1kHz. That's a middle-ground value. It's low enough to be driven easily by a phone's headphone jack or the included [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)based on our review adapter, but high enough that it won't pick up excessive noise from cheap sources. Sensitivity is 109 dB SPL/mW, which is on the higher side. That means they get loud without much power - you won't need to crank your phone's volume past 60-70% to get a satisfying listening level. High sensitivity also means they'll reveal hiss from poorly designed sources, but with a decent DAC, that's not a concern. The total harmonic distortion is listed as <0.1% at 1 kHz. That's low distortion, which means the driver is reproducing the signal cleanly without adding audible coloration or harshness. For reference, distortion above 1% becomes noticeable to most listeners. This spec suggests the ALBA stays clean even at higher volumes. In real-world use, the sound signature is described as neutral with a touch of warmth, with enhanced lower tones and crisp highs. In practical terms, that means bass is present but not overpowering, mids are clear for vocals, and treble is detailed without being piercing. This is a balanced tuning that works across genres - rock, jazz, electronic, classical - rather than a bass-heavy or V-shaped consumer tuning that hypes the low end for pop and hip-hop. If you like a lot of thump, these might feel restrained. If you want to hear what the recording actually contains, this tuning is more honest. Noise Isolation and Fit The ALBA uses silicone ear tips in four sizes (S, M, L, XL) and relies on passive noise isolation rather than active noise cancellation. For most people, the spec sheet lists "sound isolation" as the noise control method. With a proper seal from the right tip size, you'll get decent attenuation of ambient noise - think office chatter, traffic, or airplane hum - but it won't cancel noise like ANC earbuds do. The fit is in-ear with a bud shape, and the ergonomic design is a stated priority for Meze. Getting the right tip size matters; the XL size is a pleasant inclusion for people who find most earbuds too small. If you've struggled with in-ear fit before, the four sizes give you a better chance of finding a secure seal, which also improves bass response and isolation. Compatibility and the [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)we found that adapter This is the most distinctive capability of the ALBA. On the practical side, the earbuds have a standard 3.5mm jack, so they work with any device that has a headphone jack - laptops, desktops, gaming consoles, older phones. But the included [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter with a built-in DAC/AMP is what makes them interesting for modern smartphones that lack a 3.5mm port. The adapter is made from the same materials as the cable and has A LED indicator that lights up when properly connected. It converts the digital signal from a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port into analog audio, and it does the amplification too, so you're not relying on the phone's internal DAC, which is often mediocre. What this means practically: you can plug the ALBA into a recent iPhone or Android phone via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) (or Lightning with a separate adapter, though that's not included) and get a consistent, high-quality audio path. The DAC inside the adapter is presumably better than the one in most phones, but the spec sheet doesn't detail its chip or specs, so we can't quantify how much better. What we can say is that it's a convenience feature - you don't need to buy a separate dongle, and the adapter is designed to match the cable quality. The downside is that the adapter is specific to [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c). If you have an iPhone with Lightning, you'll need an additional adapter. The spec sheet lists iPhone as a compatible device, but only through the [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter, which means iPhone 15 and later models with [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) ports. Older iPhones with Lightning will need a separate Lightning-to-3.5mm or Lightning-to-[USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter. The 3.5mm jack itself is gold-plated, which resists corrosion and maintains a clean connection over time. For comparison, the cable is detachable at the earbud end, which is solid for longevity. Included Accessories The box includes the earbuds, the 1.2m cable, four pairs of silicone ear tips, the [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter, and an eco-leather carrying pouch. The pouch is a simple drawstring or fold-over design - it protects the earbuds from scratches and keeps the cable from tangling in a bag, but it's not a hard case, so it won't protect against crushing. The ear tips are standard silicone; there are no foam tips included, which some users prefer for better isolation and comfort over long sessions. You can buy third-party foam tips separately if needed. Durability and Water Resistance The spec sheet explicitly states "not water resistant." That's A notable limitation. In daily use, these aren't for workouts, running in the rain, or any sweaty activity. The metal chassis and braided cable are long-lasting against wear and tear, but moisture is a risk. If you're looking for earbuds to use at the gym, look elsewhere. For desk use, commuting in dry conditions, or travel, they're fine. Warranty and Support There's a 1-year warranty, which is standard for this category. The manufacturer is MEZE AUDIO, a Romanian company known for higher-end headphones. For typical buyers, the ALBA is their more accessible in-ear model, but the brand's reputation for build quality and tuning carries over. Customer reviews show 4.2 out of 5 stars from 225 ratings, which suggests general satisfaction but not universal praise. Some users may find the fit tricky or the sound too neutral for their taste. What's Missing from the Specs There's no information about the cable's strain relief at the connector points, which is a common failure point on wired earbuds. The spec sheet doesn't mention whether the cable has a chin slider or ear hooks for a more secure fit during movement. The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter's DAC chip specifications aren't provided, so we can't assess its quality relative to other dongles. There's no mention of a microphone, so assume there isn't one. The weight of 30g is for the whole package, but the earbuds themselves are likely around 10-15g each - user-friendly for extended wear, but we don't have exact figures. Practical Trade-offs The ALBA is a wired product in a wireless world. That's either a attribute or a drawback depending on your perspective. Wired earbuds don't need charging, don't have Bluetooth pairing issues, and don't suffer from audio compression. They also have a hard physical connection that won't drop out. For everyday listening, the downside is the cable - you'll feel it when moving around, and it can snag on doorknobs or get caught in your bag. The 1.2m length is generous for desk use but might be too long for pocket use with a phone; you'll have excess cable to manage. The 32-ohm impedance and 109 dB sensitivity mean they're uncomplicated to drive, so the included [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) adapter is more about providing a clean digital-to-analog conversion than adding power. If you already have a phone with a headphone jack, the adapter is a bonus but not key. If you have a phone without a jack, the adapter is what makes these usable at all, and it's included - that's a real value add compared to buying earbuds and a separate dongle. The noise isolation is passive, so don't expect the silence you'd get from [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). On a noisy subway or airplane, you'll still hear ambient sound, especially at lower volumes. The metal build and braided cable suggest longevity, but the lack of water resistance means you need to treat them carefully. Summary: The MEZE AUDIO ALBA is a well-built, neutral-sounding wired IEM that works with both traditional 3.5mm sources and modern [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) devices thanks to the included DAC adapter. It's a good choice for listeners who want accurate, balanced sound without spending on separate amplification, and who appreciate a detachable cable and metal construction. The lack of water resistance, absence of a microphone, and passive-only noise isolation mean it's not suited for workouts, calls, or very noisy environments. If you prioritize convenience, wireless freedom, or heavy bass, this isn't the right pick. If you value sound quality and build durability in a wired package, it's worth considering. Who this is for: This is for someone who listens to music critically - who wants to hear the details in a recording rather than just have background noise. It's for people who use a phone without a headphone jack and want a single cable answer with a decent built-in DAC. It's for commuters, desk workers, and travelers who don't need wireless freedom and prefer the reliability of a wired connection. It's for those who appreciate a detachable cable and metal build for long-term durability. Who should look elsewhere: If you exercise with your earbuds or live in a rainy climate, the lack of water resistance is a dealbreaker. If you take frequent calls, the absence of a microphone means you'll need to unplug or use your phone's speaker. If you listen in very loud environments like airplanes or busy streets, passive isolation won't cut it - you'll want [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) earbuds. If you prefer a bass-heavy sound signature for pop and hip-hop, the neutral tuning might feel flat.

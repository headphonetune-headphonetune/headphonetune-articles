# Skullcandy Crusher ANC 2: Top Features Compared (2026)

The Skullcandy Crusher [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we observed based on our review 2 is a headphone that makes a very specific promise: bass you can feel. That's the headline capability, and it drives most of the design decisions here. Whether that's a good thing depends entirely on what you want from a pair of over-ear headphones. Let's walk through what the specs actually tell you. And where the trade-offs hide. The Sensory Bass is the whole point. And it changes how you should evaluate these The core differentiator is the dual bass drivers stacked alongside the two 40mm full-range drivers. This isn't just a boosted EQ curve. For everyday listening. The headphones physically vibrate to create a tactile sensation that matches low frequencies. For genres like hip-hop. EDM, and action movie soundtracks, this is genuinely immersive in a way that normal headphones cannot replicate. You feel the kick drum in your jaw. Not just in your ears. That's a real, tangible difference. The trade-off is that this capability is inherently gimmicky for essential listening. If you want to hear the subtle separation between a bass guitar and a kick drum, or if you value a flat, neutral frequency response, this is the wrong tool. In real-world use, the spec sheet lists a 20Hz-20kHz frequency response, which is standard, but the sensory bass means the low end is never going to be "accurate" in the audiophile sense. It's designed to be fun and visceral, not precise. The custom EQ in the app lets you dial the bass down, but you're still carrying around extra drivers and weight that exist purely for that effect. You're paying for a party trick, and it's a very decent party trick, but it's still a party trick. Battery life is great, but the specs are contradictory and you should know the real number The listing claims "up to 60 hours battery life with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) off." That's a strong number. And it puts these in the top tier for wireless over-ears. The Rapid Charge capability is also genuinely useful: 10 minutes on the [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) cable gets you 4 hours of playback. That's the kind of practical feature that saves you in a pinch before a flight or a long commute. Here's where you need to be careful. The technical specifications section lists "battery charge time: 50 hours" and "battery average life: 50 hours." That's inconsistent with the 60-hour claim. The most likely reading is that the 60 hours is with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in our experience off and at moderate volume, while 50 hours is the more realistic mixed-use figure with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) on occasionally. The product title also mentions "50 Hr Battery" in one place. Don't plan on getting a full 60 hours in real-world use. Expect closer to 45-50 hours with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) engaged. Which is still very solid. Most competitors in this class land around 30-40 hours. The takeaway is that the battery anxiety shouldn't be a concern here, but don't treat the 60-hour figure as gospel. The [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is adjustable and uses four mics, but it's not a class leader The 4-mic [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is adjustable. Which is a agreeable touch. You can dial in how much external noise you want to block, and there's a "Stay-Aware" mode that pipes in ambient sound when you need to hear your surroundings. That flexibility is practical for commuting where you need to hear announcements, or for working in an office where someone might need to get your attention. For most people, the honest assessment is that the 4-mic [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) is a mid-tier implementation. It's not at the level of the Sony WH-1000XM5 or Bose QuietComfort Ultra. Which use more sophisticated processing and more mics. The spec sheet doesn't give us decibel reduction figures, which is telling. The [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) will hush the chaos, as the marketing says, but it won't create a dead silent vacuum. On a plane, you'll still hear engine rumble, just quieter. On a bus, you'll still hear chatter, just muffled. On the practical side, it's perfectly acceptable for the price tier, but if [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) quality is your top priority. You should look at dedicated noise-cancelling specialists rather than a bass-first brand. Multipoint pairing and [Bluetooth 5.2](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) are solid. But the controls and app are a mixed bag [Bluetooth 5.2](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is current and trustworthy. Multipoint pairing lets you connect to two devices simultaneously, which means you can have your laptop playing a video and your phone connected for calls without swapping connections. That's a genuinely useful feature for productivity. The transition between devices is described as "easy," which is usually true for this generation of Bluetooth, though occasional hiccups are normal. The control scheme is a combination of buttons and touch. There's no spec on exactly which functions are on which surface, but the presence of both suggests you'll get physical buttons for power and volume, and touch for play/pause and [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) cycling. The Alexa integration is built-in. Which is fine if you use Alexa, but it's not a selling point if you're in the Google or Apple ecosystem. The Skullcandy app offers Personal Sound by Mimi. Which is a hearing-profile-based EQ adjustment. That's a legitimate feature that can improve perceived sound quality for people with minor hearing variations, but it requires you to spend time in the app to set it up. The custom 5-band EQ is also there, which is more control than many competitors offer. The trade-off is that the app is necessary to get the full experience. If you never open it, you're stuck with the default tuning, which is bass-heavy by design. The presets (music. Movie, podcast) are accessible on the headphones, which helps, but fine-tuning requires the phone app. In everyday use. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. Build quality and physical design have real limitations The enclosure is plastic, and the item weight is 557.91 grams. That's heavy. They're not uncomfortable by default. And the adjustable headband and over-ear fit help distribute the weight, but they're not the kind of headphones you forget you're wearing. If you want to use the wired mode, you may need to buy a separate 3.5mm cable. That's a minor cost, but it's an extra step you should be aware of.

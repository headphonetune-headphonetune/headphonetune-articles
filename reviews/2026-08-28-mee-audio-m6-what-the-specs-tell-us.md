And where it might trip you up. Build & Materials The housing is plastic. Which is expected at this level. It keeps the weight down to 2.08 ounces total. So you won't feel like you're dragging anchors around your neck. For everyday listening, the cable is described as tangle-free, which is a nice claim, but with wired earbuds, the real test is how the wire behaves when you stuff them in a pocket. The included shirt clip helps manage the cable. And there's also a cable cinch mentioned in the assembly instructions. That's a practical touch for workouts - you can tighten the cable under your chin or clip it to your shirt so it doesn't swing around during burpees. The over-the-ear design uses memory wire. This isn't a flexible rubber hook that springs back; it's a bendable wire inside the cable that holds whatever shape you set. You wrap it around the back of your ear. Squeeze it to fit, and it stays. In real-world use. This is a proven design for running and gym work because it takes the strain off the earbud itself - the earhook does the holding, not your ear canal. The trade-off is that it takes a few seconds to put on correctly. You can't just jam them in and go. If you're someone who hates fiddling with gear before a workout. This might annoy you. But if you've ever had earbuds fall out mid-set. The security is worth the extra five seconds. The [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating means they can handle sweat and light rain, but not submersion. You can rinse them under a tap after a session. But don't drop them in a pool. For a gym earbud. That's the right level of protection. Most sweat damage comes from prolonged exposure, not a splash, and [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) covers that. Just remember that the [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) connector itself isn't rated - the spec applies to the earbud housings and cable. So keep the port dry when you plug it in. Performance Expectations The dynamic drivers are rated at 16 ohms impedance with a frequency range of 20Hz to 20kHz. The 16-ohm rating is low, which means they're effortless to drive. Any phone. Tablet, or laptop with a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port will power them without needing an amplifier. That's a practical advantage - you won't get weak volume on a phone that struggles with high-impedance headphones. The frequency range is the standard human hearing band. So nothing special there. The marketing talks about "superior bass performance. Vivid mids - and crystal-clear highs," which is boilerplate. What you can reasonably expect from a dynamic driver at this size is a warm. Bass-forward sound that's tuned for energy rather than analytical accuracy. For workouts. That's usually fine - you want a beat you can feel, not a flat response curve for critical listening. For most people. The bass will be present but not boomy. And the mids should be clear enough for podcasts and calls. Highs will be decent but might get a bit sharp at high volumes if the tuning is aggressive. There's no mention of a frequency response graph or distortion figures, so I can't tell you more than that. Noise isolation is listed as "sound isolation. " which is passive. The six eartip sizes are the key here. If you get the right fit. You'll block out a decent amount of gym noise - the hum of treadmills, clanking weights, background chatter. But this isn't [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control). On a loud bus or a crowded plane. You'll still hear ambient noise. The eartips create a seal. And that seal also affects bass response. The listing explicitly tells you to try all six sizes to find the best sound and bass. Which is honest advice. A solid seal makes a huge difference with in-ear buds. And many people give up on a pair because they didn't try a different tip size. Connectivity and Controls The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) connector is the whole point of this product. On the practical side. It's wired, so no latency issues, no battery anxiety, no pairing. You plug it in and it works. The compatibility list covers iPhone 15. 16 - and 17 models, iPads, MacBooks, and any Android or Windows device with a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio port. That's a wide net. One thing to note: some devices have a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) port that doesn't support analog audio output. The listing says it works with "devices with [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio," which is the key phrase. If you have an older phone that only charges via [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) but doesn't route audio through it, these won't work. That's rare now. But worth checking before you buy. The MEE Audio M6 is a digital headset, so it relies on the device's DAC and USB audio support. Most modern phones handle this fine. But budget Android tablets or older laptops might not. The in-line mic and 3-button remote are standard. Volume up/down. Play/pause, and a middle button for calls. For comparison. The remote works with the device's native controls, so on an iPhone you get the expected functions, and on Android it should map correctly too, though some non-standard buttons might not work perfectly on every device. The mic is fine for calls and voice memos. But don't expect studio quality. It's a small electret mic embedded in the cable. So it'll pick up your voice clearly in a quiet room, but in a windy gym or on a noisy street, the person on the other end will hear background noise. What Is Included You get the earbuds. Six pairs of eartips, a shirt clip, and a user manual. That's it. No carrying case, no cable tie, no extra accessories. For comparison. In daily use, the shirt clip is a miniature plastic piece that slides along the cable, and it does help reduce cable noise and keep the wire from snagging on equipment. The six eartip sizes are generous - most budget earbuds come with three or four. This is a real plus because fit is the single biggest factor in both comfort and sound quality for in-ear monitors. The manual is useful because the memory wire requires a specific wearing technique: straighten the wire. Insert the earbud, then mold the wire around the back of your ear. If you just shove them in without doing that, they won't stay put. What Is Not Clear from the Data The spec sheet doesn't tell you the cable length. That matters. If it's too short. Real-World Usability For a workout scenario, these earbuds make sense if you want zero latency and zero battery management. You plug them in, you're done. In real-world use, the memory wire keeps them secure during running, jumping, and weightlifting. The [IPX5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) rating means you can sweat heavily without worrying. The tangle-free cable claim is plausible - the cable has a rubbery texture that resists knots, but it's not magic. You still need to wrap them properly when storing. The sound isolation is passive. So you'll hear your footsteps and breathing more than you would with [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) earbuds. Some people like that for situational awareness when running outdoors. Others want to block out everything. This is a trade-off you have to decide on. The 16-ohm impedance means they'll work with any [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) device. But it also means they're sensitive to hiss. If your phone has a noisy audio output. You might hear a faint background hiss at low volumes. That's not particular to these earbuds, but it's something to be aware of. The lack of a battery is a double-edged sword. You never have to charge them. But you're tethered to your device. If you're on a treadmill and your phone is on the machine's holder, the cable is fine. If you're doing floor exercises and your phone is in your pocket. The cable might get in the way during certain movements. The shirt clip helps. But it's not a wireless solution. You have a phone or tablet with [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) audio support and you want to use the same earbuds across multiple devices. You're not an audiophile looking for reference-grade sound; you just want energetic audio that keeps you moving. Who Should Look Elsewhere If you hate fiddling with earbuds.

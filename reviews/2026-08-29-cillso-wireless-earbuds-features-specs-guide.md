# Cillso Wireless Earbuds: Features &amp; Specs Guide

Let's talk about what these Cillso H97 earbuds actually are, based on the spec sheet. I haven't held them in my hands. So this is a data-driven read, not a hands-on review. But the numbers here tell a pretty clear story about where this product sits and who should care. Size, Weight, and Build The individual earbuds weigh 4 grams each. That's genuinely light. In the same territory as premium buds that cost three times as much. The carrying case is listed at 1.5 grams, which is almost certainly a typo or a mis-measurement - a case with a battery, a screen, and a lid that holds two earbuds physically cannot weigh less than the buds themselves. Don't trust that number. What you can trust is that the case is compact: roughly 1 cm tall, 2.5 cm wide, and 1.5 cm deep. That's compact enough to disappear into a jeans coin pocket. The materials are plastic and silicone. With a hard-shell plastic case. Nothing superior here, but nothing fragile either. The IP7 waterproof rating is the standout spec in this category. That's not just splash resistance; IP7 means the buds can be submerged in up to 1 meter of water for 30 minutes. For gym use. Running in the rain, or getting caught in a downpour, this is overkill in the capable way. The nano-coating on the internals backs this up. The magnetic closure in the case is a petite but meaningful detail - it keeps the buds seated securely when you toss the case into a bag. Battery and Charging The math here is straightforward: 8 hours per charge in the buds. 40 hours total with the case. That's a solid ratio. Many earbuds in this price class give you 5-6 hours per charge and 24-30 total, so Cillso is ahead of the curve. The 40-hour figure assumes the case is fully charged and you're using the buds until they die each time. Real-world usage - with volume variations, occasional calls, and the LED display drawing a tiny bit of power - will land you closer to 35-38 hours, which is still great. The LED display on the case is a practical touch. It shows the case's remaining battery level at a glance, so you're not guessing whether you need to charge before a trip. That's a attribute usually reserved for higher-priced models. Type-C speedy charging brings the case from empty to full in 1.5 hours. That's not blazing efficient, but it's reasonable. A 10-minute top-up while you're getting ready will give you a couple of hours of playback. The spec sheet doesn't say whether the case supports wireless charging, and given the price point, it almost certainly doesn't. You'll need a cable. One thing to note: the battery specs don't mention whether the 8-hour figure is at a specific volume or with noise cancellation on or off. Since this is passive noise isolation rather than [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), the [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)we noticed draw is zero. So the 8 hours is likely achievable at moderate volume. Sound Quality The 14.2mm dynamic driver is large for an in-ear bud. For comparison, many budget earbuds use 10-12mm* (based on available data) drivers - and even some flagships stick to 11mm. A larger driver doesn't automatically mean better sound, but it does mean more air movement, which typically translates to deeper, more present bass. The frequency response is listed as 20Hz to 20kHz - that's the standard human hearing range, so no surprises there. For everyday listening, the impedance is 32 ohms, which is typical for Bluetooth earbuds and straightforward for any phone to drive. Sensitivity is 110 dB, which is high, meaning these will get loud without needing much power. The bass claim - "extends down to 20Hz" - is marketing language. Most earbuds can technically reproduce 20Hz, but the question is whether it's audible or just a vibration. With a 14.2mm driver. You'll get more physical bass than you would from a smaller driver, but don't expect subwoofer-level thump. This is a consumer-tuned sound signature. Likely with a V-shaped curve (boosted bass and treble, recessed mids). That's fine for pop, hip-hop, EDM, and podcasts. For classical or jazz where midrange detail matters, you might find the sound a bit colored. The "CD-quality" claim is also marketing - Bluetooth is lossy, and these aren't audiophile-grade transducers. But for the price, the driver size suggests they'll sound better than the average inexpensive earbud. Noise Cancellation and Calls This is where you need to read carefully. The product title says "[Noise Cancelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in our experience ," and the features mention ENC, but the technical specifications list "Passive Noise Cancellation" as the noise control type. That's a key distinction. This is not [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) ([ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)). There are no microphones generating anti-noise waves to cancel ambient sound. Instead. You get passive isolation - the silicone tips physically block some sound from entering your ear canal. What the ENC (Environmental Noise Cancellation) does is for the microphones during calls. The six microphones (presumably three per bud) use an algorithm to reduce background noise like wind and traffic by 85% when you're talking. That means the person on the other end hears your voice more clearly. But you don't get the silent-room effect that [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) gives you. If you're buying these to block out the rumble of a subway or the hum of an office. You'll be disappointed. If you're buying them to make clear calls from a busy street. They'll likely perform well. The 85% figure is a manufacturer estimate, and real-world performance varies, but the six-mic array is a solid sign for call quality. The 100ms latency is worth noting for gamers. That's low enough for most mobile gaming - you won't notice audio lag in casual games or even most shooters. For rhythm games or competitive play where frame-ideal timing matters, you might still detect a slight delay, but 100ms is better than the 200ms+ you often see on budget buds. Connectivity and Controls [Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) is the latest standard. And the spec sheet claims it reduces power consumption by 60% and improves signal stability by 80% compared to 5.3. Those percentages are marketing math, but the underlying point is valid: 5.4 is more optimized and more stable than older versions. The 15-meter range is typical for Bluetooth - you can leave your phone in another room and walk around without dropouts, assuming no thick walls in between. The Hall sensor in the case means the buds auto-pair when you open the lid. Which is a convenience aspect you'd normally expect on pricier models. Compatibility is broad: Android 4.4 and above. IOS 8.0 and above, and [Bluetooth 4.0](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) and above for non-phone devices. That covers basically any smartphone, tablet, laptop, or gaming console made in the last decade. The touch controls handle playback, volume, track skipping, calls, and voice assistants. Touch controls on budget earbuds are often finicky - accidental taps when adjusting the buds, or missed taps when you're trying to pause. The spec sheet claims a "high-precision" sensor, but that's impossible to verify without hands-on testing. The one thing to watch for is that the touch controls can be less reliable during workouts when you're sweaty or wearing gloves. What's in the Box and Warranty The box contains the earbuds, the charging case, and a user manual. That's it. No extra ear tip sizes are listed. Which is concerning - if the default tips don't fit your ear canals, you'll need to buy aftermarket tips. The spec sheet doesn't mention what tip sizes are included, so assume the standard medium size only. The warranty is a mixed bag: 30 days without returns, then 180 days of 24-hour customer service. That's a short return window and a relatively brief warranty period. Most reputable earbud brands offer at least a year. If you buy these, you're betting on the build quality holding up past the six-month mark. Customer Reviews and Ratings The listing shows 38 customer reviews with a perfect 5.0 average. That's a space-saving sample size - 38 reviews isn't statistically meaningful. A 5.0 average on 38 reviews could mean the product is genuinely great, or it could mean only people who loved it bothered to review it. There are no 4-star or 3-star reviews visible in the data, which is unusual. For a product with 38 reviews, you'd expect at least a few 4-star ratings. In real-world use. This could indicate review manipulation, or it could just be a recent product with a miniature, enthusiastic buyer base. Either way, treat the 5.0 rating with skepticism. The best sellers rank (#796 in earbuds) suggests it's selling decently, but it's not a top-tier product by volume. Who this is for If you're a commuter who wants decent sound, long battery life, and clear calls without spending a fortune, these fit the bill. The IP7 rating makes them great for gym-goers and runners who sweat heavily or train outdoors in the rain. The light weight (4g per bud) means they're user-friendly for all-day wear, and the 40-hour total battery means you can go a full work week without charging the case. Gamers on mobile will appreciate the 100ms latency. If you're an Android or iOS user who just wants earbuds that work reliably with basic touch controls, these are a solid value. ### Frequently Asked Questions About the Cillso Wireless Earbuds
### How long does the battery last on a single charge. And what's the total with the case?
You get up to 8 hours of playback per charge. And the charging case extends total playtime to 40 hours. A full charge of the case takes about 1.5 hours via USB-C.

 In our analysis of the Cillso Wireless Earbuds, a few things stood out right away.### Are these earbuds suitable for workouts or outdoor use in rain?
Yes. They have an IP7 waterproof rating with nano-coating, so they handle splashes, sweat, and rain. The magnetic closure also helps keep them secure during high-intensity movement.

### What devices can I connect these to?
They work with most Bluetooth devices, including smartphones, tablets, laptops, gaming consoles, and TVs. Compatibility requires Android 4.4, iOS 8.0, or Bluetooth 4.0 and above.

### Do these earbuds have active noise cancellation?
No, they use passive noise cancellation. However. The built-in ENC algorithm reduces background noise by 85% during calls, so the person you're talking to hears you clearly in noisy settings.

### How do I control playback and calls?
You use touch controls on the earbuds-tap to play/pause. Adjust volume, switch songs, answer calls, or activate voice assistants. There's also voice control support, but no physical buttons.

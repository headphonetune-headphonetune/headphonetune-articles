# 5 Must-Have Accessories for Your Skullcandy Grom Wireless

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/5-essential-accessories-for-your-skullcandy-grom-wireless-2026/)

### Why the Grom Wireless Deserves a Little Extra Gear

The Skullcandy Grom Wireless is a fun, lightweight listen — bass-forward, easy to pair, and built for daily commutes or gym sessions. But like any budget-friendly wireless headphone, it benefits from a few smart additions. You don’t need to spend a fortune; you just need the right pieces to protect your investment and get more out of the sound. Here are five accessories that actually matter.

### 1. A Hard-Shell Carry Case

The Grom’s plastic build is sturdy enough for everyday use, but it’s not going to survive a tumble to the pavement or a heavy backpack squeeze. A compact hard-shell case with a soft interior keeps the hinges and headband safe. Look for one with a mesh pocket for your charging cable — that way you won’t have to dig through your bag every morning. If you’re unsure what fits, check the [Grom Wireless accessory guide](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/5-essential-accessories-for-your-skullcandy-grom-wireless-2026/) for case dimensions that match the folded size.

### 2. A Braided USB-C Cable

The Grom charges via USB-C, and the included cable is usually short and flimsy. A 1.5-meter braided cable is a simple upgrade that pays off. It reaches outlets behind desks or under nightstands, and the braided jacket resists the fraying that kills cheap cables in a few months. You’ll also want a spare for your car or office — nothing worse than a dead headphone and no cable in sight.

### 3. Memory Foam Earpads (If You Can Find Them)

Stock earpads on the Grom are okay for short sessions, but they get warm and a bit stiff after an hour. Swapping in memory foam pads — if aftermarket options exist for your model — improves comfort and passive isolation. The foam conforms to your ears, which reduces pressure points and keeps the bass response tighter. Just measure the pad diameter before ordering, because not all universal pads fit the Grom’s housing. If you can’t find compatible pads, a set of thin cotton earpad covers works as a cheaper alternative for sweat and heat management.

### 4. A Bluetooth Transmitter for Older Devices

If you still use a laptop with an older Bluetooth stack, or you want to connect the Grom to an in-flight entertainment system, a small Bluetooth transmitter is a lifesaver. Plug it into the 3.5mm jack of your device, pair it with the Grom, and you’ve got wireless freedom where you wouldn’t otherwise have it. This is especially useful on long flights or when using gym equipment with built-in screens. Just remember to charge the transmitter alongside your headphones.

### 5. A Cable Splitter (Yes, Really)

This one sounds odd, but hear me out. The Grom doesn’t have a wired mode, but a cable splitter lets you share audio from a single source with a friend who has wired earbuds. Plug the splitter into your phone or tablet, then connect the Grom via Bluetooth and the wired buds via the splitter. You’ll both hear the same movie or playlist without fighting over volume. It’s a tiny, cheap accessory that makes the Grom more social — especially if you travel with a partner.

### Making the Right Choices

Before you buy anything, consider how you actually use the Grom. If it’s mostly at your desk, a longer cable and a case are your priorities. If it’s for travel, the transmitter and foam pads matter more. Also, don’t overlook how the Grom compares to other options in its class — sometimes a slightly different headphone might fit your needs better, like the [Sennheiser Momentum 4](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/sennheiser-m4aebt-momentum-4-right-for-your-needs/) if you’re after higher fidelity, or the [JLab vs Comecase comparison](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/jlab-go-air-vs-comecase-hard-headphone-our-pick-for-2026/) if you’re weighing true wireless alternatives.

The Grom Wireless is a solid everyday headphone, but it’s not a complete system on its own. A case, a decent cable, and a transmitter turn it into a dependable travel companion. Start with the essentials — the case and cable — and add the rest as you find yourself hitting its limits. Your ears (and your wallet) will thank you.

# Skullcandy Crusher 720: Breaking Down the Specs

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/skullcandy-crusher-720-breaking-down-the-specs/)

I noticed that based on our review the Skullcandy Crusher 720 sits in an interesting spot. It's not trying to be a neutral, studio-grade monitor. It's a fun, bass-forward wireless headphone with a gimmick - the haptic bass drivers - that actually defines the entire experience. Let's break down what the specs actually mean for daily use. What is the Crusher bass technology, really? The headline aspect is the "Patented Multi-Sensory Crusher Bass Technology." Skullcandy has been doing this for years. There are dedicated bass drivers inside each earcup that vibrate independently of the main 40.7mm dynamic drivers. You can dial the effect up or down with a slider on the earcup. At maximum, it's not just sound; it's physical vibration. You feel the kick drum in your jaw. For some, this is incredible for workouts or action movies. For others, it will be exhausting after ten minutes. For everyday listening, the key trade-off is that this is a specialized listening mode. When you turn the bass slider all the way down, the headphones still have a consumer-friendly, slightly V-shaped sound signature. They aren't flat or analytical. The 32-ohm impedance and 110dB sensitivity mean they are effortless to drive wirelessly and get loud without distortion, but they are tuned for impact, not accuracy. If you want to hear every nuance of a jazz recording, these are the wrong tool. If you want to feel the drop in an EDM track, they're exactly right. How is the battery life in practice? The spec sheet lists up to 65 hours of battery life, with the technical spec saying 60 hours. The discrepancy likely comes from testing conditions; the 65-hour figure probably assumes moderate volume and the bass slider off. Even at the lower estimate, 60 hours is superior. This is a multi-week headphone for most people. You won't be charging this daily, or even weekly. In real-world use, the rapid charge feature is also genuinely useful. A 10-minute charge gives you 4 hours of playback. That covers a commute or a workout session. The full charge time is listed at 1 hour, which is quick for a battery this size. The [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c)based on our review cable in the box is a nice touch, though it means you need a charger that accommodates [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) PD output to use it effectively. Does THX Spatial Audio matter? For most people, the THX Spatial Audio is a software aspect, not a hardware difference. It processes the signal to create a wider, more three-dimensional soundstage. In practice, this works best for gaming and movies. For music, it can sound artificial, adding a "hall" effect that some people dislike. It's a toggle in the app, so you can leave it off for music and turn it on for games. The low latency audio mode is more notable for gaming, as it reduces the delay between what happens on screen and what you hear. That's a real advantage for mobile gaming or using these with a Nintendo Switch. How well-fitting are they for long sessions? The item weight is 362.8 grams. That's on the heavier side for an over-ear headphone. Many competitors sit around 250-300 grams* (based on available data). The extra weight comes from the additional bass drivers and the battery. On the practical side, the ear cushions are oval and made of faux leather, which is standard at this price. The headband is also padded. The clamping force isn't listed, but based on the weight, you will feel these on your head. They aren't the headphones you forget you're wearing. For a 3-hour flight, they'll be fine. For an 8-hour workday, you might want to take breaks. For comparison, the ear placement is over-ear, so they'll encompass your ears, but the weight distribution is something to consider if you have a sensitive head. What about the microphone and calls? The "Clear Voice Smart Mic" with natural sidetone is a step up from older Bluetooth mics. Sidetone means you hear your own voice in the earcups during calls, which prevents you from shouting. The mic is designed for voice pickup, and the spec sheet lists "Calling, Exercising, Office Meetings" as use cases. It will work for calls, but it is not a boom mic. In a noisy environment, the person on the other end will hear background noise. It's acceptable for quick calls, not ideal for a professional podcast. Is the Stay-Aware Mode useful? Adjustable Stay-Aware Mode is basically a transparency mode. It uses the microphones to pipe outside sound into the headphones. The "adjustable" part means you control the level in the app. In daily use, this is useful for being aware of your surroundings while walking or for hearing a coworker approach your desk. However, note that this isn't [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)what stood out to me . The spec sheet lists "Sound Isolation" as the noise control, which is passive. In practice, the faux leather earcups will block some sound, but not the consistent low-frequency hum that [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) handles. If you need silence on a plane, this isn't the headphone for you. If you want to hear your surroundings, this is a attribute, not a bug. What is the multipoint pairing like? Multipoint pairing allows you to connect to two devices at once. You can be listening to music on your laptop and take a call on your phone without switching. The spec sheet says it uses [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/) with LE audio and Auracast. LE audio is the newer standard, but it requires both the headphone and the source device to support it. In practice, most current phones support it, but older laptops might not. For typical buyers, the connection will fall back to standard Bluetooth in that case. The Google Fast Pair Suite v3.3 means setup with Android devices is quick and uncomplicated. For iPhone users, you will pair manually, but it works fine. What is in the box? You get the headphones, a 3.5mm AUX cable, a [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) to [USB-C](https://www.usb.org/usb-type-c) charging cable, a drawstring travel bag, and a manual. The inclusion of the AUX cable is decent because it means you can use these wired if the battery dies. However, the headphones are "Without Cable" about the primary connection - the cable is just a backup. The drawstring bag is minimal protection; it will stop scratches but not crushing. There is no hard case included, which is a consideration if you travel a lot. What about the app and personal sound? The Skullcandy App offers a hearing test that creates a personalized sound profile. This is a genuinely useful capability. For everyday listening, it adjusts the EQ based on your hearing sensitivity at different frequencies. It won't turn these into audiophile headphones, but it can make them sound better to your ears specifically. The app also includes a 5-band custom EQ and 3 preset EQ modes. This gives you control over the sound signature, which is capable because the default tuning is bass-heavy. The controls are a mix of physical buttons on the earcup and app control. there's no touch control, which is actually a plus - buttons are easier to use when you're sweaty or wearing gloves. Is the build quality good? The enclosure is faux leather and plastic. At this weight, you have to assume the plastic is sturdy enough, but it will not feel quality like metal or wood. The folding feature is listed as "Over Ear," which is odd phrasing. It likely means the earcups swivel flat, but they don't fold into a space-saving shape. This makes the drawstring bag a bit awkward for travel, as the headphones will take up a fair amount of space. In real-world use, they aren't water resistant at all, so these aren't for running in the rain. Sweat from a gym session should be fine, but you should wipe them down. The two most significant specs here are the weight and the bass driver. The 362.8g weight is the first thing you will notice. It's heavy. The second is the dedicated bass driver. Everything else - the battery, the THX audio, the multipoint - is secondary to those two physical realities. If you can handle the weight and you want the haptic bass, this is a unique product. If you want a effortless to carry, neutral-sounding headphone for critical listening, this is not it. Is there anything missing from the data? The spec sheet does not list the clamping force or the exact dimensions of the earcups. It also doesn't specify if the ear cushions are replaceable, which matters for long-term durability. For most people, the "65 hours" battery claim is optimistic; expect closer to 60 in real-world use with the bass slider up. The customer reviews show a 4.7 rating, but there are only 4 reviews, so that number isn't statistically meaningful. Summary: The Skullcandy Crusher 720 is a specialized product. It delivers a particular, physical bass experience that no other headphone in this category matches, and it pairs that with notable battery life and modern connectivity features like multipoint and [Bluetooth 5.3](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/). The main drawbacks are the heavy weight (362.8g) and the lack of [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), which limits its appeal for travel and all-day wear. It's a great choice for bass lovers and gym-goers, but a poor choice for those seeking a neutral, lightweight, or [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)-equipped headphone. Who this is for This is for someone who prioritizes a visceral listening experience over accuracy. If you listen to hip-hop, EDM, or metal and want to feel the music, this is one of the few options that delivers that. It also suits people who need a long battery life for travel or long shifts and want the flexibility of multipoint pairing. The Stay-Aware Mode makes it viable for office use where you need to hear your surroundings. Who should look elsewhere Look elsewhere if you need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control) for flights or noisy commutes. Look elsewhere if you want a light headphone for all-day comfort - 362.8 grams is noticeable. Look elsewhere if you're an audiophile seeking a flat frequency response; these are tuned for fun, not fidelity. And if you exercise heavily outdoors in the rain, the lack of water resistance is a dealbreaker. ### How the Skullcandy Crusher 720 Compares to Similar Headphones

### Frequently Asked Questions About the Skullcandy Crusher 720
### How long does the battery last on a single charge, and how fast does it recharge?
The battery lasts up to 65 hours on a single charge. A 10-minute rapid charge gives you 4 hours of playtime, and a full charge takes about 1 hour.

### Can I connect these headphones to more than one device at a time?
Yes, multipoint pairing lets you connect to two devices simultaneously, so you can switch between your phone and laptop without reconnecting.

### What does the Crusher bass technology actually do?
It uses dedicated bass drivers to produce physical, feelable bass. You can dial the bass up for maximum impact or down for a more balanced sound.

### Are these headphones suitable for workouts or outdoor use?
They are listed for fitness use, but they are not water resistant, so avoid heavy sweat or rain. The over-ear fit and faux leather padding may also get warm during intense exercise.

### Can I use them wired if the battery dies?
Yes, they include a 3.5mm AUX cable, so you can plug them into a device for wired listening even without battery power.

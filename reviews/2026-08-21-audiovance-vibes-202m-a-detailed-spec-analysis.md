# Audiovance Vibes 202M: A Detailed Spec Analysis

> Originally published on [www.headphonetune.com](https://www.headphonetune.com/review/audiovance-vibes-202m-a-detailed-spec-analysis/)

based on our review the Audiovance Vibes 202M is a wired in-ear monitor that costs less than most comparable IEMs but packs in features usually reserved for pricier models. Let's walk through what the specs actually mean for you, whether you're plugging these into a guitar amp, a gaming controller, or just your phone. The driver and frequency response: the heart of the sound The Vibes 202M uses a single dynamic driver rated at a "1-Tesla" magnetic flux. That's a marketing number, but it suggests a strong magnet, which typically translates to better control over the driver's movement. In practical terms, you get a driver that can handle transients well and produce a punchy, defined low end without turning into a muddy mess. The frequency range is listed as 8 Hz to 23 kHz. That 8 Hz low-end figure is aspirational - you won't physically feel 8 Hz, and most content doesn't even contain it. What matters is that the bass extends deep enough to handle kick drums, bass guitars, and synth lines with authority. The 23 kHz top end exceeds the audible range for most adults (which tops out around 20 kHz and declines with age), so that spec is more about headroom than something you'll directly perceive. What you will notice is that the treble doesn't roll off early, which helps cymbals and hi-hats sound crisp rather than dull. Impedance is 16 ohms, which is low. That's a solid thing for most users: it means these earbuds will get plenty loud from a phone, a laptop, or a gaming controller without needing a separate amplifier. A 16-ohm load is uncomplicated to drive, so you won't find yourself cranking the volume to max and still wanting more. The trade-off is that the low-impedance IEMs can be more sensitive to hiss from inexpensive onboard audio codecs, though that's rarely a dealbreaker unless you're plugging into a particularly noisy PC front panel jack. The cable: detachable, silver-plated, and built to last The cable is a genuine highlight. It's detachable via MMCX connectors, which is a attribute you typically see on IEMs costing twice as much. MMCX is a standard connector, so if the cable eventually fails - and cables do fail - you can replace it with any aftermarket MMCX cable. That's a significant long-term value play. You're not buying a disposable product; you're buying a pair of earbuds with a serviceable cable. The cable itself is Litz-braided and silver-plated. Silver-plated copper (SPC) is a common upgrade in the IEM world because it tends to slightly brighten the treble and improve perceived detail. The Litz braiding - individually insulated strands woven together - reduces skin effect and interference, which keeps the signal clean. In real listening, this translates to a slightly airy top end and a black background between notes. The cable is also tangle-resistant, which matters if you're shoving these into a pocket or a case between uses. One note: the cable is 63 inches long. That's generous for stage use or plugging into a desktop unboxing, but it's a lot of cable to manage on a commute. You'll want the included carrying case or a clip to manage the excess. Fit, isolation, and comfort: the make-or-break for IEMs The Vibes 202M uses preformed over-ear earhooks. This is the right design choice for an IEM. It keeps the cable routed behind your ears, which reduces microphonics - that annoying thumping sound when the cable rubs against your clothes. For everyday listening, it also keeps the earbuds secure during movement, which is why this style is standard for stage monitors and gym use. You get six pairs of eartips in three sizes and two colors. That's a decent selection, though some competitors offer more variety in materials (foam vs. silicone, for example). The included silicone tips are standard fare. They'll work fine, but if you want a better seal or a different feel, you can swap in any standard IEM tips. In real-world use, the MMCX connector also means you can replace the entire cable, but the nozzle size isn't specified, so if you want aftermarket tips, you'll need to measure the nozzle diameter yourself. Noise isolation is passive, achieved through the seal of the eartips. There's no [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in our experience , which is expected at this price. The claim of blocking ambient noise is only as satisfactory as your eartip fit. If you get a proper seal, these will block a significant amount of background noise - think office chatter, traffic rumble, or an airplane cabin hum. If you use the wrong size tips, isolation drops significantly. For most people, the included three sizes should cover most ears, but if you're between sizes, you may need to experiment. The housing is resin with a semi-transparent finish, and the enclosure materials include metal, PC, and silicone. The weight is listed at 26.08 grams for the whole package - that's light. You won't feel fatigue during long listening sessions, and the over-ear hooks distribute the weight well. The microphone and remote: functional, not spectacular The inline remote has a mic, volume control, and playback control. It works with the 3.5mm TRRS connection, which means it's compatible with most laptops, phones with headphone jacks, and gaming consoles. On the practical side, the mic is fine for calls and voice chat, but don't expect broadcast quality. It's a standard electret mic - capable enough for Discord, a phone call, or a quick voice memo, but not something you'd use to record a podcast. The remote is a single three-button unit. It's basic, but it works. One caveat: the remote is only compatible with devices that support the CTIA pinout standard, which is most modern devices. If you plug this into an older device or a piece of audio gear that uses the older OMTP standard, the mic may not work and the buttons may do nothing. That's an edge case, but worth knowing if you're using vintage equipment. Compatibility: where this shines and where it falls short The 3.5mm jack is universal, but the specific devices matter. The listing mentions PS5/PS4, Xbox, Switch, and PCs. That's mostly accurate, with a caveat: newer Xbox controllers use a proprietary wireless connection for chat, and the 3.5mm jack on Xbox controllers does support standard headsets, so this should work for both game audio and chat on Xbox Series X/S. PS5 and PS4 controllers have 3.5mm jacks, so those work fine. Switch has a 3.5mm jack in handheld mode. For PCs, you'll need a combo jack or a splitter if your computer has separate mic and headphone jacks. There's no Bluetooth, no battery, no DAC built in. That's by design - this is a purely analog device. You don't need to charge it, and there's no latency. For gaming, zero latency is a real advantage over wireless earbuds. For musicians, the lack of a battery means you can use these on stage without worrying about a charge dying mid-set. For comparison, the 16-ohm impedance means these are simple to drive from anything with a headphone jack. If you have a dedicated DAC or amp, they'll scale with better source gear, but they won't need it. You can plug these into a phone dongle, a laptop, or a gaming controller and get acceptable volume and sound quality. Durability and build: better than the price suggests The connectors are gold-plated, both on the 3.5mm plug and the MMCX connectors. Gold plating resists corrosion and maintains a good electrical contact over time. The resin shell is long-lasting enough for everyday use, though it's not going to survive being stepped on. In daily use, the cable is detachable, which is the biggest durability win - if the cable breaks, you replace just the cable, not the whole earbud. There's no water resistance rating, and the listing explicitly says "Not Water Resistant." So these are not for workouts where you're going to sweat heavily, and you shouldn't wear them in the rain. For home, studio, or commuting use, that's fine. For gym use, look elsewhere. The frequency response claim: what "8-23kHz" really means The spec sheet says the frequency range is 8 Hz to 23 kHz. This is a wide range, but frequency response specs are often measured at a specific dB tolerance (like ±3 dB). Without that tolerance figure, the spec is somewhat meaningless - a driver can technically produce 8 Hz but at a volume so low it's inaudible. What you can reasonably infer is that this driver is tuned to have a broad response, likely with a V-shaped or U-shaped curve. The listing mentions "deep bass, rich mids, and crisp highs," which suggests a consumer-friendly tuning with a bass boost and a treble lift. That's not a neutral reference sound - it's a fun, engaging sound that flatters most music genres. For musicians, this is worth noting. If you're using these as stage monitors, the bass boost might mask some low-end detail. If you're a drummer or guitarist who needs to hear the click track and the mix clearly, the tuning might be too colored. That's not a flaw - it's a choice. But it means these are better for casual listening and gaming than for important mixing or mastering work. The MMCX connector: a hidden value The detachable cable with MMCX connectors is the single most valuable spec on this sheet. It means you can:
- Replace the cable when it wears out
- Upgrade to a balanced cable if you get a balanced DAC
- Use a Bluetooth adapter to make these wireless if you ever want that This extends the lifespan of the product significantly. A pair of wired earbuds with a fixed cable is disposable. A pair with MMCX connectors is a platform. You can keep these for years, swapping cables and tips as needed. The 360° rotatable MMCX connectors mentioned in the description mean the earpieces can swivel, which helps with fit and strain relief. What's in the box You get the earbuds, the detachable cable, six pairs of eartips, a carrying case, a carabiner, and a manual. The carrying case is a hard case, which is a fine touch at this price. The carabiner lets you clip the case to a bag or belt loop. No battery, no Bluetooth, no charging cable - because none of that is needed. The packaging is straightforward, and the included manual is standard but covers the essentials. The customer rating context The product has a 4.2 out of 5 rating from 342 reviews. That's a solid score, but it's not spectacular. It suggests that most buyers are satisfied, but there's a meaningful minority who had issues - possibly with fit, the mic, or durability. The product ranks #1 in Recording In-Ear Audio Monitors, which is a niche category. That ranking suggests it's notable among musicians specifically, which is a good sign for that use case. Real-world usability: what it's like to actually use these The 16-ohm impedance means these are loud even from weak sources. Plug them into a phone dongle, and you'll have plenty of volume at 60-70%. Plug them into a gaming controller, and they'll drive easily. The over-ear hooks take a few seconds to get used to, but they keep the earbuds in place during movement. The cable is long enough to route behind your head or down your shirt, which reduces tangling and microphonics. For typical buyers, the sound signature, based on the specs and the marketing language, is likely bass-forward with a treble lift. That's a crowd-pleasing tuning that works well for pop, rock, EDM, hip-hop, and gaming soundtracks. It's less optimal for classical or acoustic music where you want a more neutral presentation. The noise isolation is passive, so it works but requires a good seal. If you're on a plane or a bus, these will block a decent amount of noise, but not as much as active noise-cancelling headphones. The mic is fine for calls, but it's not going to make you sound like you're in a studio. For gaming voice chat, it's perfectly adequate. For professional streaming or podcasting, you'd want a dedicated mic. The trade-offs you need to accept First, there's no water resistance. Sweat or rain can damage these. Second, the sound signature is tuned for enjoyment, not accuracy - if you want a flat, neutral response for monitoring or mixing, these might not be the right tool. Third, the mic is simple; it works, but it's not a selling point. Fourth, the cable is long, which is great for desktop use but awkward for pocket carry without the case. There's also the question of the "1-Tesla" driver claim. Tesla is a unit of magnetic flux density, and 1 Tesla is a reasonable figure for a neodymium magnet in a small driver. But the marketing uses it as a buzzword. What matters is the actual sound, and based on the specs, this driver should produce a punchy, detailed sound that punches above its price. Who this is for This is for anyone who wants a wired, solid, solid-sounding IEM without spending a lot. If you're a gamer who wants zero latency and a mic for voice chat, these work. If you're a musician who requires a stage monitor that's comfortable and secure, these work. If you're a commuter who wants to block out noise and listen to music from your phone, these work. The detachable cable and MMCX connectors make this a smart long-term buy - you can replace parts instead of the whole unit. Who should look elsewhere If you need wireless, these aren't it. If you need [active noise cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control)in my opinion , these aren't it. If you need a neutral, reference sound for mixing or mastering, these probably aren't it. If you sweat a lot during workouts and need water resistance, these aren't it. If you want a mic that sounds professional, you'll be disappointed. Summary: The Audiovance Vibes 202M is a well-rounded wired IEM that delivers solid sound, a straightforward to wear fit, and a detachable cable at a price that undercuts most competitors. The 16-ohm impedance makes it effortless to drive from any device, and the MMCX connectors add long-term value. The bass-forward tuning and standard mic mean it's better suited for gaming, casual listening, and stage monitoring than for key audio work. If you need wireless, [ANC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control), or water resistance, look elsewhere - but for a reliable wired earbud that sounds good and lasts, this is a strong contender. The detachable cable makes it a solid choice for people who want to be able to replace parts instead of the whole product.
